# Vision

## Present purpose

This repository supports the Austrian effort to assess and, where justified, challenge DAC8 and its national implementation. Its immediate value is practical: reduce duplicated source collection, expose adverse authority early, distinguish viable procedural routes from expensive dead ends, and show which facts still require evidence.

The primary current users are the Austrian legal and research team. Public communications and website work may draw from the same evidence base, but must not turn legal hypotheses into established facts.

## Open-source direction

The repository will be public. Over time it should help researchers, civil-society groups, affected persons, service providers, and counsel evaluate comparable proceedings in other European countries.

European reuse means a repeatable method, not copied conclusions. A future country dossier should separately establish:

- the EU baseline and the national implementation;
- national additions or divergences;
- the claimant, standing, and available remedies;
- controlling national and EU precedent;
- the evidence needed for the concrete proceeding.

## Austria first

The present repository should remain optimized for the live Austrian work. Do not build speculative country scaffolding or describe an Austrian conclusion as generally applicable across the EU. Add another jurisdiction when actual sources and a concrete use case justify it.

## Editorial priorities

The useful output is substantive legal research: exact provisions, holdings, procedural posture, counterarguments, evidence gaps, and current verification dates.

Prefer accuracy over advocacy, primary sources over repetition, and explicit uncertainty over persuasive filler. Target audience and repository purpose belong here rather than as recurring narrative in reports.

Confidential mandate material remains outside the public repository unless its owner expressly authorizes a reviewed, redacted inclusion.
