# DAC8 Individualantrag, MiCA, Fundamental Rights, and Wealth-Register Report

## Executive verdict

The most defensible Austrian strategy is a coordinated two-track case, not a single blended citizen application:

1. an Austria-home CASP uses Article 140 B-VG against a precise Austrian excess, supported by current and quantified implementation burdens;
2. a concrete user builds an actual processing record through GDPR requests, the DSB, and the BVwG and asks the deciding court for an Article 267 reference and, where necessary, Austrian norm review.

For a citizen, a direct Individualantrag is now a fallback. VfGH G 178/2024 treats DSB → BVwG → Article 144 review as a reasonable route for mandatory data processing. The data-retention case remains important, but its exceptional unknown-provider, continuous-generation, and short-deletion facts cannot simply be assumed for DAC8.

The strongest national merits issue remains section 20(1) Krypto-MPfG: Austria requires the section 18 tax-report information to be determined, collected, stored, and processed for all customers, all transactions, and all crypto-assets irrespective of reportable-user status. The claim becomes materially stronger with a reliably non-reportable user and a working reportable-user-first alternative.

## What successful Individualanträge teach

The procedure-checked corpus does not support a slogan that expensive or universal compliance is automatically unconstitutional. It supports a narrower method:

- G 37/02 ua is the closest provider analogue: direct technical duties and significant costs without requiring a deliberate violation; the underlying surveillance-facilities duty nevertheless survived.
- G 118/2015 ua and G 100/05 ua show that concrete preparations and mandatory infrastructure expenditure can create current effect before sanctions.
- G 20/2015 ua favours the actually regulated businesses over an association without its own injury.
- G 52/2024 shows that a concrete person can be directly affected although a prohibition formally addresses professionals.
- G 59/12 and G 62/70/71/12 are the correct direct-applicant dockets in the data-retention litigation; G 47/12 itself was Kärnten's abstract application.
- G 606/2015 ua is the essential adverse control: standing based on tax-compliance preparation does not establish disproportionality.

The initiating brief must carry the complete admissibility and merits case. It should identify each claimant and right, quote the exact challenged words and version, show current legal effect without an intervening decision, audit every ordinary route, use a remedy-complete main request and unconditional eventual requests, allocate each objection to each provision, and attach claimant-specific evidence. Essential grounds cannot be outsourced to annexes or repaired later.

For the provider, the evidence centre is the incremental Austrian build: field provenance, tax aggregation for confirmed non-reportable users, contracts, invoices, engineering time, FTE, storage, access control, deletion, DPIA, and the difference from MiCA, TFR, AML, accounting, and the DAC8 minimum. For the user, it is the contract, tax-residence and reportability evidence, actual 2026 processing, Article 15 response, purposes, recipients, retention, access logs, and the provider's response to deletion or restriction.

The official guidance currently states a EUR 340 filing fee from 1 July 2025 and says roughly 15 pages will usually suffice, subject to case complexity. Counsel must recheck both at filing and use the required electronic channel.

## MiCA and TFR do not erase the tax-purpose problem

MiCA, TFR, and Krypto-MPfG overlap operationally but have different legal purposes:

| Regime | Main purpose | Core record | Ordinary retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| MiCA | supervision, market integrity, client protection | services, activities, orders, and transactions under Article 68(9) | five years, up to seven on authority request |
| TFR | AML/CFT travel rule | granular originator, beneficiary, account/DLT-address, and transfer identifiers | five years; possible national AML/CFT extension after a necessity assessment |
| DAC8/Krypto-MPfG | tax transparency and automatic exchange | identity, residence, and annual per-asset/per-category aggregates | DAC8 and national tax-data rules |

Existing MiCA or TFR data can reduce the CASP's marginal cost. They do not automatically authorise a new universal tax-profile purpose. TFR Article 25 is particularly useful because it restricts personal-data processing to AML/CFT and bars incompatible reuse.

MiCA Articles 60 and 65 also require home-state precision. Article 60 is the notification route for specified regulated entities in their home state; Article 65 governs passported cross-border services. An Austria-home provider is the cleanest section 15 claimant. A foreign-home provider needs an actual Austrian duplicate demand after sections 16 and 17 are considered.

Section 20(4) must not be misstated. Its ten-year deletion rule is worded for information transmitted under the Act. Section 39 concerns compliance documentation. The period for section 20(1) tax profiles that are never transmitted remains an interpretation, precision, and storage-limitation issue to prove from the actual provider system; it is not a confirmed universal ten-year raw-data rule.

## Fundamental-rights case

The strongest justiciable rights are:

- section 1 DSG, Article 8 ECHR, and Charter Articles 7, 8, and 52(1): legality, purpose, necessity, minimisation, retention, security, safeguards, and effective review;
- Article 6 StGG and Charter Article 16 for an Austria-home CASP's proven incremental burden;
- equality only with a precise comparator and actual different treatment;
- legal certainty where storage, deletion, access, or responsibility is genuinely ambiguous;
- Charter Article 47 and data-subject remedies where access, correction, downstream propagation, or independent review is ineffective.

Speech, association, professional secrecy, non-discrimination, physical integrity, and chilling are claimant-specific. They belong in a pleading only when the fields reveal the protected activity and a concrete effect is proved. They remain legitimate communication and research strands if labelled as supported risk or advocacy hypothesis.

The public argument must retain exact schema limits. DAC8 does not itself report wallet addresses, transaction IDs, dates, or counterparties. It reports identity-linked annual values, units, and counts per crypto-asset and category. A richer behavioural graph arises only if those data can legally and technically be linked to provider, TFR, public-ledger, beneficial-ownership, or other records.

## The wealth-register argument

DAC8 is not a formal comprehensive wealth register. It does not list every holding, liability, self-custodied asset, or current net worth. Calling it one as a legal fact would weaken the case.

The stronger argument distinguishes three architectures:

1. a formal central register containing assets and values;
2. a federated asset-discovery layer connecting separate registers;
3. a functional wealth profile produced by joining identity, accounts, transaction aggregates, beneficial ownership, provider records, and public-ledger data.

DAC8 can contribute to the third. Enacted AMLD6 infrastructure also matters: by 10 July 2029 national mechanisms and BARIS are to identify bank, payment, securities, crypto-asset accounts, and safe-deposit relationships across borders. The minimum BARIS fields identify relationships and opening/closing dates; they are not balances or a central transaction history. Directive 2024/1654 supplies case-specific serious-crime access and formats for transaction records requested under national law, not routine central ingestion of every transaction.

The Commission's 2024 feasibility study examined national interconnection and a central EU asset database. It warned that central cross-asset aggregation could reveal habits, daily life, and social relationships, risk a manifestly disproportionate interference, and create severe cyberattack consequences. This is unusually strong institutional evidence against a future comprehensive design. The Commission subsequently described the project as a study and confirmed that the AML package did not establish a comprehensive EU asset register.

The constitutional objections to a comprehensive or functionally equivalent system are design-specific:

- aggregation can change the nature and severity of the interference;
- universal discoverability reverses a targeted investigative model;
- transaction aggregates can produce incomplete or misleading wealth inferences;
- purpose stacking can expand recipient groups and uses without a fresh necessity assessment;
- a concentrated high-value dataset magnifies insider, breach, extortion, kidnapping, and home-invasion risk;
- durable visibility can chill lawful saving, donations, association, journalism, or privacy-enhancing conduct;
- crypto-only visibility can distort equality and enforcement where comparable asset classes remain opaque;
- federated controllers can make access, correction, provenance, deletion, and judicial remedy illusory.

The State has serious answers: tax equality, AML, confiscation, cross-border concealment, better audit logs, and the possibility that one controlled query is less intrusive than indiscriminate inquiries to many providers. CJEU *Luxembourg Business Registers* and ECtHR *L.B. v Hungary* are strongest against public or broadly searchable access; they do not invalidate a narrowly designed non-public system. ECtHR *G.S.B. v Switzerland* confirms that controlled tax exchange with effective safeguards can be lawful.

The campaign position should therefore be:

> DAC8 is not yet a complete wealth register. It adds an identity-linked crypto-activity layer that can become part of a de facto asset map when combined with account registers, provider transaction records, beneficial-ownership data, and public-ledger analysis. No comprehensive or federated wealth profile should be created without a distinct democratic mandate, proof of strict necessity, minimal fields, precise purposes, restricted and logged access, short retention, strong security, correction, and effective independent review.

## Communications and other countries

Public materials should distinguish legal fact, supported risk, and advocacy hypothesis. The positive frame is targeted tax enforcement with constitutional safeguards, not opposition to paying tax.

Each country file should separately map:

1. the mandatory EU baseline;
2. national transposition, discretion, additions, authority practice, and unknowns;
3. claimant, standing, ordinary route, Article 267 court, and available remedy;
4. field-level evidence, retention, access, costs, security, correction, and the precise requested relief.

The Austrian finding that section 20(1) is an addition must not be copied into another country without the enacted national text. Nor can Austrian Individualantrag rules be exported. The reusable asset is the comparison and evidence method.

## Immediate case-building priorities

1. secure an Austria-home CASP with a clear MiCA Article 60/63 and Krypto-MPfG section 15 position;
2. produce a field-by-field, system-by-system, costed EU-baseline versus Austrian-addition map;
3. obtain an actual non-reportable-user access, purpose, retention, and deletion record;
4. obtain a written Austrian authority position on disputed home/host and duplicate-reporting issues;
5. commission a reproducible correlation and threat-model report rather than assume ledger linkage;
6. draft the precise main/eventual perimeter only after the actual system and ordinary routes are known;
7. maintain a public claims register with source, evidence level, caveat, and correction history;
8. treat any future wealth-register or AMLD6 implementation challenge as a separate case against its enacted fields, access, retention, and remedies.

## Known constraints

- The user cannot compel an Article 267 reference. A court can reject an invalidity argument but cannot itself invalidate an EU act under *Foto-Frost*.
- The DAC2 Austrian trilogy and CJEU C-623/22 are serious adverse authority.
- The reported Bull Bitcoin French action still lacks an official docket, authenticated pleading, order, or reference in the located record.
- This research is a litigation and communication work product, not a ready-to-file pleading or a substitute for Austrian and EU-law counsel.
- Confidential prompt-only material was excluded from the wiki, raw corpus, outputs, legal-document corpus, and repository artifacts.
