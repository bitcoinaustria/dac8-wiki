# AML, Crypto, DAC8, and Fundamental-Rights Research Report

## Executive verdict

AML strengthens the State's public-interest case but does not answer the Krypto-MPfG proportionality question. The current Austrian challenge should use AML, TFR, and MiCA as the counterfactual baseline: subtract what a CASP already collects and retains, then prove the additional tax-purpose fields, aggregation, access, storage, deletion, and costs imposed by section 20(1) Krypto-MPfG for a reliably non-reportable user.

The research does not support attacking “the AML package” in the current Individualantrag. Most AMLR and recast AMLD6 rules are future-dated; Austria's located WiEReG 2027 text is a ministerial draft, not enacted law; and the strongest AML judgments reject unconditional public access while preserving controlled authority, FIU, obliged-entity, and internal register use. Any future AML challenge should target an enacted Austrian provision and its exact fields, recipients, thresholds, retention, profiling, and remedies.

The communications case can be broader. The risk is a layered financial-intelligence architecture in which tax identity and annual crypto aggregates can be joined with provider transaction records, TFR data, beneficial ownership, institutional account discovery, and public-ledger analysis. That is not proof of a current complete wealth register. It is a reason to demand purpose boundaries, differentiated access, data minimisation, reliable inference, bounded retention, correction, independent review, and strong security before deeper interoperability becomes operational.

## Current law versus future law

| Date/status | Instrument | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| current until 10 July 2027 | AMLD4/5 through national law | preventive AML baseline, CDD, FIUs, reporting, registers, and supervision |
| applies since 30 December 2024 | TFR, Regulation 2023/1113 | CASP travel rule; person-to-person transfers without a CASP remain outside; self-hosted addresses are not prohibited |
| generally applies since 1 July 2025 | AMLA Regulation 2024/1620 | EU authority, convergence, FIU support, selected-entity supervision architecture, and supervisory database |
| current in Austria | FM-GwG, MiCA-VVG, WiEReG, KontRegG | CASP AML duties, FMA supervision, beneficial ownership, and controlled account-relationship discovery |
| in force, generally applies 10 July 2027 | AMLR, Regulation 2024/1624 | directly applicable CDD, monitoring, self-hosted risk, sensitive-data, and retention rulebook |
| general national deadline 10 July 2027 | Directive 2024/1640, recast AMLD6 | FIUs, supervisors, beneficial ownership, national account mechanisms, access, and sanctions |
| transposition due 23 November 2026 | Asset Recovery Directive 2024/1260 | criminal tracing, freezing, and confiscation, including crypto-assets |
| due 10 July 2029 | BARIS interconnection | cross-border discovery of covered institution-held account relationships, not a register of every wallet address |

The phrase “AMLD6” is ambiguous. Directive 2018/1673 concerns criminal money-laundering offences. Directive 2024/1640 is the institutional and national-mechanisms companion to AMLR. They should always be cited by number.

## Current Austrian crypto AML baseline

FM-GwG section 6 requires risk-based customer and beneficial-owner due diligence. Section 11a began applying on 30 December 2024; its current amended wording applies from 1 January 2026 and requires risk assessment and suitable controls for CASP transfers to or from self-hosted addresses. Section 16 governs suspicious-transaction reports to the FIU, section 20 tipping off, section 21 ten-year retention of necessary CDD and transaction evidence followed by deletion subject to other federal retention authority and specified criminal proceedings, and sections 25, 31, and 34 FMA supervision and enforcement.

TFR requires defined originator and beneficiary information whenever at least one CASP is involved, including for self-hosted transfers regardless of value. EUR 1,000 is only the additional ownership-or-control assessment trigger. It is not a travel-rule threshold, automatic suspicious report, one universal proof method, or prohibition of self-custody.

MiCA and MiCA-VVG govern authorisation and supervision. Austria's legacy VASP transition has ended; a provider claimant needs its actual current MiCA home-state and Article 60/63 position. WiEReG records entity beneficial ownership and control. KontRegG records defined account relationships, not balances, public-chain addresses, self-hosted wallets, or all CASP transfers. Under KontRegG, underlying tax account inspection is a separate, BFG-authorised step.

## Consequences for the present DAC8/Krypto-MPfG case

### Merits

The useful question remains whether Austria may require section 18 tax-report information to be determined, collected, stored, and processed for every customer, transaction, and crypto-asset irrespective of reportable-user status. AML does not eliminate that question. It changes the proof burden.

The claimant should show:

1. which identity and transfer fields already exist solely because of FM-GwG, TFR, MiCA, accounting, fraud prevention, or contract;
2. which fields or derived aggregates are newly processed for the Krypto-MPfG tax purpose;
3. why the user is reliably non-reportable after the required self-certification and validation;
4. how a reportable-user-first architecture would still detect residence or status changes;
5. the exact production, archive, backup, access, correction, and deletion lifecycle;
6. for a CASP, only incremental engineering, personnel, vendor, legal, DPIA, storage, and security cost.

Current FM-GwG section 21's ten years and future AMLR Article 77's five years plus a case-specific extension are a useful proportionality comparison. Current AMLD4 and TFR allow a justified national extension up to ten years, so Austria's present period cannot be described as facially contrary to the current EU corridor. AMLR also preserves other compliant retention bases and pending-proceeding rules. None of this proves that every Krypto-MPfG profile is held for ten years. Section 20(4) Krypto-MPfG is worded for transmitted information; section 39 concerns compliance documentation. Never-transmitted all-customer profiles require an exact statutory and operational retention analysis.

### Procedure

For an individual citizen, AML research does not cure the subsidiarity problem. A data-subject route through GDPR requests, the DSB, BVwG, and Article 144 review may remain a reasonable path, with an Article 267 request addressed to the deciding court. An Austria-home CASP with direct current duties and quantified implementation work remains the cleaner potential Article 140 claimant. The final route, perimeter, deadline, and evidence package require Austrian constitutional and EU-law counsel.

Future AMLR, BARIS, or WiEReG 2027 provisions should not be added to the current annulment request merely because they may later connect adjacent data. They become separate review targets when an enacted rule directly affects the chosen claimant.

## Fundamental-rights and precedent assessment

The strongest theory is lifecycle-specific:

- **collection and minimisation:** justify each field and affected population;
- **risk classification:** use evidence, current information, accuracy controls, human review, and non-discriminatory criteria;
- **access:** separate public, legitimate-interest, obliged-entity, FIU, supervisory, tax, and criminal-investigation tiers;
- **linkage and reuse:** provide a legal basis, purpose, recipients, and safeguards for each new data flow;
- **retention:** justify each period and deletion exception;
- **consequences and remedies:** provide notice where possible, access, correction propagation, review, and an independent remedy.

CJEU C-37/20 and C-601/20 and VfGH G 265/2023 are strong against unconditional public beneficial-ownership access. VfGH G 62/2025 simultaneously protects useful access for journalists and other legitimate-interest users. VfGH E 2189/2025 supports internal identity resolution and controlled beneficial-owner data. Together they require differentiated access; they do not establish a right to total financial anonymity.

CJEU C-562/20 and C-235/14 are central adverse authorities because evidence-based enhanced AML controls and stricter national rules can be lawful. The Austrian DAC2 chain—especially VfGH E 2097/2021 and VwGH Ro 2023/04/0021—and CJEU C-623/22 are stronger adverse tax-reporting authorities. CJEU C-175/20 supplies a narrower claimant-side analogy requiring clear law, specific purpose, objective criteria, strict necessity, and bounded time for a tax-data request. CJEU C-694/20 protects lawyer-client communications from unnecessary indirect disclosure, but it cannot be generalised to ordinary CASP transaction records.

Communications-retention, PNR, and surveillance cases are useful comparators for blanket collection, access, profiling, independent authorisation, and lifecycle safeguards. They must be labelled analogies rather than direct AML invalidity holdings.

## AML and the wealth-register argument

The current legal record supports four precise statements:

1. DAC8/Krypto-MPfG creates identity-linked annual crypto activity, not a complete statement of net worth.
2. KontRegG and future BARIS discover covered institutional account relationships; they are not central balance or transaction-history databases and do not list every self-hosted wallet. BARIS will have case-specific FIU, designated serious-crime, and future insolvency access paths, not public access.
3. Provider, TFR, AML, beneficial-ownership, tax, and public-ledger data can produce a much richer functional profile when legally and technically joined.
4. The constitutional risk depends on the enacted linkage, fields, recipient, search mode, retention, security, accuracy, and remedy—not the label “register” alone.

The Commission-commissioned asset-register feasibility study remains the best institutional source for a future comprehensive-register argument. It identifies serious proportionality, profiling, and cyber-risk problems in central cross-asset aggregation. It was a feasibility study, not a decision to build such a register, and the 2024 AML package did not itself create one.

Recast AMLD6 Article 18 adds a separate 2029 national real-estate access point for competent authorities, including ownership, acquisition price, encumbrances, and ownership/price history. This materially expands asset discovery but still does not establish one unified EU net-worth register.

## Strongest State case

Any credible pleading must answer the following:

- AML/CFT, equal taxation, sanctions enforcement, and asset recovery are weighty public interests.
- Crypto is cross-border, portable, pseudonymous, and can move outside classic accounts.
- Universal status work can detect false or changing residence and prevent reporting gaps.
- CASPs already retain extensive identity and transaction material, reducing marginal burden.
- Confidential annual tax aggregates are materially less intrusive than public access or complete real-time transaction histories.
- Controlled account mechanisms can reduce indiscriminate requests to hundreds of providers while improving query logs and speed.
- CJEU and VfGH authority upholds evidence-based CDD, controlled register processing, and broad DAC architecture.
- AMLD is minimum harmonisation in material respects, and CJEU C-562/20 confirms that Member States may adopt stricter, properly published AML rules subject to legality, proportionality, and non-discrimination.
- A court may prefer a narrow or constitution-conforming interpretation over annulment.

The response is a field-level narrower design with equivalent enforcement effectiveness, a concrete claimant, actual data flows and costs, and precise safeguards or gaps. General warnings about surveillance or a future wealth register cannot carry the current case alone.

### Publishable German lines

> Selbstverwahrung ist nicht verboten. Sobald ein regulierter Kryptowerte-Dienstleister beteiligt ist, greifen jedoch gesetzliche Identitäts-, Transfer- und risikobasierte Prüfpflichten.

> Die AML-Verordnung gilt grundsätzlich ab 10. Juli 2027; die Transfer-of-Funds-Verordnung gilt bereits seit 30. Dezember 2024.

> BARIS ist kein Register aller Wallet-Adressen. Geplant ist eine grenzüberschreitende Auffindbarkeit bestimmter, bei Institutionen geführter Kontenbeziehungen.

> DAC8 ist noch kein vollständiges Vermögensregister. Die Gefahr liegt in der Verknüpfung von Steueridentität, Krypto-Aggregaten, Kontenbeziehungen, Transaktionsdaten und öffentlichen Blockchain-Daten – und damit in Zweckausweitung, Zugriff, Speicherdauer, Fehlprofilen und Sicherheitsrisiken.

> Wir wenden uns nicht gegen Steuer- oder Geldwäschebekämpfung. Wir verlangen, dass Daten nachweislich erforderlich, zweckgebunden, minimal, sicher, korrigierbar, zeitlich begrenzt und unabhängig überprüfbar sind.

### Do not publish as fact

- the EU has banned self-hosted wallets;
- every P2P transfer carries identifying travel-rule data;
- AMLR already applies in full;
- BARIS contains every wallet, balance, and transaction;
- AMLA directly supervises every CASP;
- DAC8, TFR, MiCA, AML, FIU, tax, and criminal records are freely interchangeable;
- CJEU or VfGH register decisions invalidate all confidential financial reporting;
- Austria has already enacted the full 2024 AML package;
- the WiEReG 2027 ministerial draft is current law.
