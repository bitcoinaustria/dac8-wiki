# Solove: Nothing to Hide and Other Misunderstandings of Privacy

Daniel J. Solove, San Diego Law Review 44:745–772 (2007); the PDF's later production timestamp is not the publication year. Pages 756–759, 760–767 and 771–772 distinguish surveillance from bureaucratic information processing, explain social privacy interests and analyse aggregation, exclusion and secondary use. The argument supports scrutiny of concrete marginal intrusions and safeguards, not blanket rejection of legitimate security goals. This is original conceptual scholarship, not a judgment or measured causal effect. Historical examples cited inside it were not separately verified or counted as incidents here.
