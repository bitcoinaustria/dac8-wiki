# FMA: Identifizierung im Ferngeschäft (December 2024)

FMA series "Reden wir über Aufsicht", issue no. 03, dated 2024/12, topic Geldwäsche, addressed to FM-GwG obliged entities, 4 pages. The disclaimer says it creates no new law and states the FMA's expectations and legal view.

Content:
- **Four routes (p. 1–2):** the note restates that § 6 Abs. 4 FM-GwG allows four alternative safeguards: online identification under the Online-IDV; electronic ID (e.g. "ID-Austria in Vollfunktion", with audit-proof storage); QES or registered mail; and the reference-account method. It stresses that collecting ID copies alone is never enough. A frequent practical problem is mixing elements of different safeguards "entgegen der gesetzlichen Vorgabe" (contrary to the legal requirements).
- **Forged documents are common (p. 2):** "Leider zeigt sich immer wieder, dass der Einsatz gefälschter Identifizierungsdokumente trotz aller technischer Vorkehrungen im digitalisierten Alltag keine Seltenheit ist." (Unfortunately, the use of forged identification documents is repeatedly shown to be no rarity despite all technical precautions.)
- **A-FIU purple notice (margin, p. 2):** on 22 March 2024 the A-FIU issued a goAML "purple notice" stating that the number of fully forged identity documents "speziell bei Online-Identifikationsverfahren stark zugenommen hat" (had risen sharply, specifically in online-identification procedures).

Expected controls over external providers (p. 3):
- documented allocation of responsibilities;
- sufficient inspection rights in the outsourcing agreement, and a feedback loop with the provider;
- checking that audio is recorded clearly and screenshots are taken;
- checking that only the person being identified is visible;
- checking that age and identity match the face;
- additional document-forensics tools;
- device fingerprinting to compare the stated residence with login data.

Responsibility always stays with each obliged entity, and internal "second-level" quality controls are needed against fully forged documents (p. 4). Outlook (p. 4): online identification will be tied to eIDAS requirements under the new EU framework, probably detailed in AMLA regulatory technical standards (RTS).

Evidence class: official supervisory statement combining a qualitative FIU warning with FMA expectations. It confirms from the supervisor's side the A-FIU 2024 finding that remote identification is defeated by forged documents. It also shows the supervisor responding by specifying controls. This is adverse to a claim of supervisory inaction, but it contains no counts, rates or case outcomes. The purple notice itself is not public (goAML is restricted to obliged entities); only this summary was read. For DAC8, it supports the point that provider-held identity data can be wrong. It does not quantify how often.
