# Tax-data breach and consumer credit

Vyacheslav Mikhed and Michael Vogan, *How data breaches affect consumer credit*, Journal of Banking & Finance 88 (2018), pp. 192–207, [DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2017.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2017.12.002).

## Findings and limits

- **Design, pp. 12–19:** natural experiment concerning South Carolina's 2012 Department of Revenue breach, using North Carolina and Georgia comparisons. Credit data cover 2010Q1–2016Q1; protection data end in 2014Q3.
- **Denominators, Table 2, p. 43:** variable-specific panel observations, including 16,433,756 for initial fraud alerts and 20,353,470 for risk scores. These are not counts of unique breach victims.
- **Results, pp. 20–22 and 27–28:** protective-service uptake increased; central credit outcomes did not show corresponding deterioration, and extended fraud-alert filings did not increase.

This is adverse evidence against assuming universal observable financial harm. Credit proxies do not measure every misuse, nonfinancial burden or physical assault. Neither Austrian institutions, cryptocurrency holders nor DAC8 reporting were studied; no representative crypto-attack incidence follows.
