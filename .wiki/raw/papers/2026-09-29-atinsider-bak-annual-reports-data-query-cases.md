# BAK Jahresberichte 2023 and 2024: "unbefugte Datenabfrage und Datenweitergabe" as a corruption-case category

The Bundesamt zur Korruptionsprävention und Korruptionsbekämpfung (BAK, part of the BMI) publishes annual statistics on new cases within its original jurisdiction (§ 4(1) Z 1–13 BAK-G). The [2024 report](https://www.bak.gv.at/Downloads/files/Jahresberichte/BAK_Jahresbericht_2024_bf_20250422.pdf) (printed pp. 38–41) records 852 such cases in 2024, up from 754, with 1,750 registered offences. By leading offence, 769 were § 302 StGB Missbrauch der Amtsgewalt (+81, +11.77%) and 37 were § 310 StGB Verletzung des Amtsgeheimnisses (up from 22). Of the 852 cases, "116 (13,62 Prozent) Fälle fielen in die Kategorie der 'unbefugten Datenabfrage und Datenweitergabe' (darunter fallen beispielsweise auch Informationsweitergaben)" (116 cases fell into unauthorised data query and data disclosure, including onward disclosure of information). The BAK handled 39 of these itself and other units 77.

The [2023 report](https://www.bak.gv.at/Downloads/files/Jahresberichte/Jahresbericht_2023.pdf) (printed pp. 34–35) records 98 of 754 cases (13%) in the same category, with 688 cases led by § 302 StGB. The 2024 report states that its statistics count only newly registered cases in the year. A case is "geklärt" (cleared) when at least one suspect is identified by name. Not every case is investigated by the BAK itself.

Evidence class: official institutional statistics (primary). The category is broad: it combines queries and disclosures and covers the whole public sector within BAK jurisdiction, not only BMI or BMF. The pages read give no breakdown by employer, database or outcome. A registration is a suspicion under investigation, not a finding of guilt; convictions, diversions and discontinuances are not reported here. The figures are lower bounds on actual misuse because they count only cases reported or detected. They are also not a rate, because no denominator of authorised users or queries is given.

For DAC8, the data support the claim that unauthorised data queries and disclosures by Austrian officials are a recurring, officially categorised phenomenon, roughly 100 new criminal cases a year. They do not show any case involving tax-administration crypto data, and they do not measure BMF control effectiveness.
