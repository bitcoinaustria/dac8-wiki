# Commission impact assessment SWD(2021) 190

Commission Staff Working Document, Impact Assessment accompanying the July 2021 AML package (AMLR, AMLD6, AMLA, recast TFR), dated 20 July 2021. Locators are PDF pages of the Eerste Kamer copy (printed page in brackets where different).

Problem definition (PDF p. 11): citing Europol 2017, 0.71–1.28 percent of EU GDP is "detected as being involved in suspect financial activity" (EUR 117–210 billion in 2019), with about 2 percent seized and 1 percent confiscated. FIU section (PDF p. 16 [printed p. 12]): inadequate FIU feedback means "reporting has become an automated process" producing "false positives"; "Indications confidentially provided by credit institutions to the Commission estimate that between 50% and 75% of reports submitted to FIUs" are of no significance, with industry studies pointing to about 10 percent of STRs being "of use"; "In many cases, only 1 in every 10 suspicions reported to the FIU is subject to an analysis shared with law enforcement" (PDF p. 17). Annex on evaluation (PDF p. 78): "The effectiveness of the existing AML regime in reducing the amount of ML can only be indirectly perceived via proxy indicators", which "can be misleading"; AMLD4/AMLD5 had not operated long enough for meaningful data; the 2019 post-mortem report found "defensive over-reporting". Costs (PDF p. 44, footnote 55 [printed p. 40]): compliance costs "vary greatly"; the only figure given is a LexisNexis estimate of USD 41–54 million a year for a medium-sized cross-border EU bank.

Evidence class: official legislative-policy document. The 50–75 percent false-positive figure is unaudited, confidential industry input relayed by the Commission, not a Commission measurement; the "about 10 percent of use" figure derives from Europol/PwC material already in the wiki (Europol 2017). The document is written to justify harmonisation, AMLA and the single rulebook, so its criticism targets fragmentation and feedback, not the CDD model.

Transfer limits: supports that the Commission itself had no direct measure of AML's effect on laundering, no EU-wide compliance-cost estimate, and acknowledged defensive over-reporting. It is not a measured precision rate and does not concern DAC8 or tax reporting; it does show that the EU legislature adopted AMLR and crypto Travel Rule expansions without an outcome baseline.
