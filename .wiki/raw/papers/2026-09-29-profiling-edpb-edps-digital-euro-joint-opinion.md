# EDPB-EDPS Joint Opinion 02/2023 on the Proposal for a Regulation on the establishment of the digital euro

European Data Protection Board and European Data Protection Supervisor, on COM(2023) 369. The adoption date of 17 October 2023 comes from the document listing; the text extraction did not show the cover date. The joint opinion was published 18 October 2023. The official 34-page PDF was read in full.

**Statements on transaction data.** Para. 3 recalls the EDPB's 2021 letter warning of "general tracking of transactions across the payment infrastructures" and treating cash as the privacy benchmark. Para. 5 recalls the EDPB's June 2022 consultation response. The EDPB favoured no AML/CFT checks below a threshold for low-value transactions and avoiding "centralisation of the transactions by any public or private entity". It also stated that access to transaction data by the ECB and national central banks "for the purposes of fraud mitigation or the enforcement of tax rules should be avoided". Para. 23 finds that recording all online transactions "regardless of their amount" is not in line with the Proposal's own data-protection aim, and recommends a privacy threshold for low-value online payments (developed in para. 94). Para. 48 warns that programmable money "could allow inferring the spending habits" of users. Para. 53 notes that centralised settlement data on all online users means "the consequences of any data breach could potentially harm a large number of individuals". Para. 102 treats processing of "sensitive personal data as it relates to digital euro users' finances" as a DPIA high-risk criterion. Footnote 15 cites a Bundesbank poll: 54% of respondents named possible "general surveillance of the purchasing habits" as a drawback.

**Evidence class and DAC8 transfer.** This is an advisory opinion under Article 42 EUDPR, not binding law, and it concerns a CBDC proposal (legislative status in 2026 not verified here). It is the clearest institutional statement found that the EU data-protection authorities regard systematic, centralised, state-accessible transaction records as a proportionality problem. It also names tax enforcement as a purpose for which central-bank access should be avoided. The transfer to DAC8 has limits. DAC8 does not give a central bank item-level transactions. It gives tax authorities annual aggregates under an express legal obligation, and the opinion does not address tax-information exchange. It can be cited as a statement of DPA values on centralisation, breach scale and low-value privacy thresholds. It is not authority on DAC8's validity.
