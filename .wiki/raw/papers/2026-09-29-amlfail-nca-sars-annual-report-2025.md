# UKFIU SARs Annual Report 2025

National Crime Agency, UK Financial Intelligence Unit, SARs Annual Report covering April 2024 to March 2025 (18 pages). Headline statistics (p. 4): 866,616 SARs (2023-24: 872,048); 57,666 Defence Against Money Laundering (DAML) requests (57,081); GBP 382.6 million denied to suspected criminals from DAML requests (GBP 240.1 million); 2,048 account freezing orders, forfeitures or restraints obtained through DAMLs (1,785). Sector shares of 848,036 portal SARs (p. 6): banking 85.54 percent, financial services 10.34 percent, virtual assets 1.67 percent.

DAML detail (pp. 7–9): 3,276 refused DAMLs (about 6 percent of DAMLs received) led to GBP 359.9 million denied; the report attributes GBP 103 million to a single case and notes that single years can be driven by a few large cases, so it also gives a three-year rolling average (GBP 298.4 million). DAML volume was stable partly because a 2023 amendment raised the POCA s339A threshold from GBP 250 to GBP 1,000. The UKFIU states it "is not in a position to comment" on whether sector reporting volumes are proportionate to risk (p. 6).

Evidence class: official FIU performance report; it presents asset-denial outputs of the consent regime, a UK-specific mechanism with no direct Austrian equivalent. It gives no figure for how many ordinary (non-DAML) SARs were disseminated, used in investigations or linked to convictions, so volume cannot be converted into usefulness. Asset denial includes temporary restraints and is not final confiscation.

Transfer limits: adverse to a claim that suspicious reporting yields no measurable enforcement effect, and useful for showing that virtual-asset firms file a small share of reports. It does not show crime reduction, net benefit, or anything about tax reporting.
