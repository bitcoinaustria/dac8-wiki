# FinCEN deepfake media alert 2024

FIN-2024-Alert004, dated 13 November 2024 (6 pages), SAR key term "FIN-2024-DEEPFAKEFRAUD". Basis (p. 2): FinCEN analysis of BSA data, open-source reporting and law-enforcement and interagency information. FinCEN observed that, beginning in 2023 and continuing in 2024, suspicious-activity reporting describing suspected deepfake media increased, with criminals "altering or creating fraudulent identity documents to circumvent identity verification and authentication methods" (p. 1).

Findings (pp. 2–3): BSA data indicate criminals used GenAI to create falsified documents, photographs and videos "to circumvent financial institutions' customer identification and verification and customer due diligence (CDD) controls", combining GenAI images with stolen or fake personal data to form synthetic identities. Malicious actors "have successfully opened accounts" with suspected GenAI identities and used them to receive and launder proceeds of other frauds and as funnel accounts. Detection (pp. 3–4) often occurred only on later re-review of onboarding documents or through enhanced due diligence triggered by other suspicious activity. Red flags (pp. 4–6) include third-party webcam plugins during live checks, refusal of multifactor authentication, photos matching GenAI face galleries and rapid activity toward gambling or digital-asset exchanges.

Evidence class: official regulator alert based on filed SARs; it gives no count, rate or loss figure, so it establishes the existence and documented growth of a typology, not its prevalence or the failure rate of verification systems. It treats KYC as defeatable but also as the control that eventually detected the cases.

Transfer limits: supports the narrow point that an identity-verified account may be held under a synthetic or stolen identity, so a KYC record is not proof of the beneficial actor. It is US-specific and says nothing about EU remote-identification standards, DAC8 due diligence or error rates in reported TINs.
