# Penney: Chilling Effects, Surveillance and Wikipedia Use

Jonathon W. Penney, Berkeley Technology Law Journal 31:117–182 (2016), DOI 10.15779/Z38SS13. January 2012–August 2014: 48 English-language articles, over 81 million pageviews, but 32 monthly statistical observations—not independent people. Results pp. 147–153 and appendix: immediate declines appear across models; slope findings depend on including Hamas and other selection/autocorrelation choices. General Wikipedia traffic also fell. No user-level motives, geographic population or substitution to other reading sources was observed (pp. 171–172). This is quasi-experimental aggregate behaviour, not a randomised surveillance intervention or DAC8 effect. Mechanical credibility 1: known academic +1, landmark age exemption; student-edited law-journal publication is not assumed to be external peer review.
