# Project Hertha

BIS/Bank of England, 5 June 2025; printed pp. 7–10, 12–18, 24 and 27, especially Table 1.

The experiment uses 1.8 million synthetic accounts, 308 million transactions and 2,000 simulated laundering schemes. At a common target flagging 1% of test accounts, illicit-account recall is 44% for banks, 39% for the minimal payment-network model and 49% with active collaboration. New-typology recall rises from 33% to 41%.

This is useful evidence of network information gain, but also a counterexample to assuming equal utility of the minimised standalone model. It is not real criminal detection, crypto analysis or a tax-retention test. Metric terminology must distinguish false discovery share among flags from the classical false-positive rate among negatives. No archive-effectiveness comparison exists.

Scoring: recent +1, recognised institutions +1; institutional pilot, not independent operational validation.
