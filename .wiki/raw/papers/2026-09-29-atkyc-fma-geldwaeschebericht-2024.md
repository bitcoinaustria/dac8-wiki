# FMA-Geldwäschebericht 2024

FMA, "Prävention von Geldwäscherei und Terrorismusfinanzierung – Themenschwerpunkte und Trends 2024", 40 pages, announced 22 May 2025. Page numbers are the printed "n / 40" numbers. No 2025 edition had been published on fma.gv.at by 29 September 2026; the FMA annual report 2025 carries the 2025 figures (separate raw file).

**Supervisory activity (pp. 4, 9–10):**
- 20 on-site inspections, 20 on-site reviews ("Vor-Ort-Einsichtnahmen") and 11 management meetings;
- 78 investigation and 12 remedial proceedings;
- 4 VASP registration proceedings and 11 fit-and-proper tests.

Of the 20 inspections, 13 were at credit institutions, four at VASPs, two at insurers and one at an investment firm. The number of investigation proceedings has fallen since 2022. The FMA attributes this partly to greater awareness among obliged entities of the "Finanzagent:innen" (money-mule / undisclosed-trust) phenomenon, better-calibrated monitoring, and higher-quality STRs (p. 9). In 2024 the A-FIU passed 18 analysis reports to the FMA on suspected non-disclosure of trust relationships or money laundering (p. 10).

**Remote identification (p. 11):** the FMA says online identification is one of the permitted § 6 Abs. 4 safeguards. It then states: "Praktische Probleme iZm Online-Identifikationsverfahren, etwa die Verwendung von gefälschten Ausweisen, verdeutlichen das Risikopotenzial der technologischen Entwicklung." (Practical problems with online-identification procedures, such as the use of forged IDs, illustrate the risk potential of technological development.) Obliged entities must carefully select a trustworthy provider. They must collect all required information, on a risk basis often beyond the minimum ID data (e.g. registered address), and run internal quality controls.

**VASPs (pp. 4–5, 12, 21–22):**
- The share of VASPs in the FMA's high-risk class rose to 33.3 percent (+12 points) because the sector shrank while high-risk firms stayed the same; another third were medium risk.
- Weaknesses in this "very heterogeneous" group were risk understanding, the risk-based approach, and technical and staff resources.
- The FMA targeted dormant "Vorratsregistrierungen". Registered VASPs fell from 25 (December 2022) to 13 on 30 December 2024, when MiCAR became applicable.
- Transition: registered VASPs may continue until 31 December 2025 under § 23 MiCA-VVG. By April 2025 only one CASP licence had been granted in Austria.
- Most VASPs surveyed planned to implement the TFR with a bought-in external product.

**Sanctions (pp. 14–15):**
- 44 first-instance penalty proceedings and 13 penalty decisions (nine against credit institutions or their branches, three against VASPs), with fines of about EUR 4,331,080.
- The focus was on missing or deficient strategies, procedures and controls (§ 23 FM-GwG).
- Largest cases: EUR 2,070,000 and EUR 791,000 (correspondent banking); EUR 476,000 (beneficial-owner structure of a high-risk client); EUR 588,000 (risk classification). These decisions were not final when published.
- The Federal Administrative Court (BVwG) confirmed an FMA finding on inadequate verification of high-risk clients' beneficial owners and reduced the fine from EUR 2,748,000 to EUR 1,978,560.
- Measures against European American Investment Bank AG contributed to the ECB withdrawing its licence (not final).

Evidence class: official supervisory statistics and qualitative findings, self-reported by the supervisor. They are adverse to any claim that Austrian AML supervision is inactive: VASPs are inspected and fined. None of the named 2024 fines is described as a remote-identification case, and the report gives no count of forged-ID onboardings. For DAC8, it supports the point that the supervisor recognises forged-ID onboarding as a live risk in the channel CASPs use. It is not an error rate for CASP identity records.
