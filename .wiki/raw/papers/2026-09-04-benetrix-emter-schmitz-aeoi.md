# Bénétrix, Emter and Schmitz: Automatic for the tax people

The 2024 *Economic Policy* article uses restricted BIS Locational Banking Statistics for 2014 to 2019 and IMF portfolio and direct-investment data. It estimates that bilateral household deposits from non-haven countries in haven banks decreased by 28.5 percent after the AEOI legal event, while the broader non-bank effect was 12.5 percent. The authors estimate USD 119 billion of household deposits withdrawn from havens.

The study also reports displacement to non-participating havens, no comparable reduction for non-bank financial-institution deposits, and increased deposits between haven-based non-bank financial institutions. The authors interpret these patterns as evidence that AEOI is effective for less sophisticated evaders but may encourage or leave room for more complex shell-company and trust structures.

Deposit movements are not observed tax returns, and the study does not evaluate DAC8, a crypto-asset reporting field or rights costs. It supports both the State's general deterrence case and the need to test displacement and coverage claims against the actual reporting design.
