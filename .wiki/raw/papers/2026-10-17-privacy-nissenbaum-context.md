# Nissenbaum: Privacy as Contextual Integrity

Helen Nissenbaum, Washington Law Review 79:119–158 (2004); original author-hosted article. PDF pp. 21–30, Part III.A–C, and pp. 37–40/Conclusion explain appropriateness, distribution and evaluation of changing norms. A fact's being previously shared does not settle whether another recipient or purpose is appropriate. Existing norms can be unjust and beneficial information aggregation is possible; contextual integrity is not an absolute no-sharing or no-change rule. This is a normative analytical framework, not a statute, universal personal veto or empirical harm estimate.
