# Guidelines on Automated individual decision-making and Profiling for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679

Article 29 Data Protection Working Party, WP251rev.01, adopted 3 October 2017, last revised and adopted 6 February 2018; endorsed by the EDPB on 25 May 2018. The official 37-page PDF from the Commission's Article 29 newsroom was read in full.

**Definitions (Chapter II.A, pp. 6–7).** Profiling under Article 4(4) GDPR has three elements: automated processing, personal data, and the objective of evaluating personal aspects. It "is often used to make predictions about people, using data from various sources to infer something about an individual, based on the qualities of others who appear statistically similar". Classification for aggregate statistics without conclusions about individuals is not necessarily profiling. Chapter III.A.1 (p. 9) says profiling creates "derived or inferred data about individuals – 'new' personal data that has not been provided directly by the data subjects themselves".

**Special categories (Chapter III.C, p. 15).** Controllers need an Article 9(2) condition for "special category data derived or inferred from profiling activity". Profiling "can create special category data by inference from data which is not special category data in its own right but becomes so when combined with other data". The example given is inferring health from food shopping records combined with nutritional data. The guidelines cite a Facebook Likes study predicting male sexual orientation (88%), ethnic origin (95%) and Christian/Muslim religion (82%). Chapter IV.B (p. 22) gives a hypothetical: a card issuer lowers a limit based on the behaviour of other customers who live in the same area and shop at the same stores.

**Status and DAC8 transfer.** This is authoritative but non-binding guidance, which the EDPB's PSD2 Guidelines 06/2020 rely on for the "behavioural patterns" statement. It supports the view that inferences drawn from DAC8 or provider data are themselves personal data subject to Article 9 where sensitive. It does not say that tax reporting is profiling. Aggregate reporting to a tax authority falls under Article 4(4) only if the data are used to evaluate personal aspects of the reported person, for example in risk scoring. The examples depend on item-level data.
