# Bundeskriminalamt Cybercrime Report 2025

Source digest. The report was published by BMI II/BK in August 2026; the PDF metadata title is "Cybercrimereport 2025". It was announced in a [BK news item dated 27 August 2026](https://www.bundeskriminalamt.at/newsb0e5.html?id=423074733843546346386f3d). The PDF was fetched and converted to text on 29 September 2026. Locators below are printed page numbers; the PDF page is in brackets.

**Figures and crypto passages.**

- **Totals (p. 7 [9], table):** 63,459 reported cybercrime offences in 2025, of which 19,570 were cleared (30.8%).
- **Internet fraud (p. 9 [11]):** "Die Schadenssumme im Jahr 2025 belief sich hierbei ähnlich wie im Vorjahr auf zirka 120 Millionen Euro" (the loss in cyber-trading fraud was about €120 million, similar to the prior year). The BK set up a dedicated investigation group in response.
- **Phishing (same page):** besides bank and card customers, there were increasingly phishing attacks on "Inhaberinnen und Inhaber von Krypto-Börsen-Konten" (holders of crypto-exchange accounts). The BK says it is in close exchange with affected financial institutions so that potential victims can be warned.
- **Crypto fraud trends (section 2.4, p. 11 [13]):** "einen signifikanten Anstieg von Betrugsdelikten im Kryptowährungssektor" (a significant rise in fraud offences in the crypto sector), with fake trading platforms promising 200–300% returns, address poisoning, rug pulls and liquidity-mining fraud. The report says MiCA and stricter KYC "sollen mehr Transparenz und Sicherheit schaffen" (are meant to create more transparency and security).
- **Pig butchering (section 2.5, pp. 12–13):** contact goes to "zufällig ausgewählte Personen" (randomly selected persons) via messenger or LinkedIn. When victims stop paying, fraudsters threaten them "mit rechtlichen Konsequenzen, Gewalt oder der Veröffentlichung ihrer intimen Kommunikation" (with legal consequences, violence or publication of intimate material).
- **Carinthia case (section 4.2, pp. 34–35 [36–37]):** a crypto investment fraud network involving the WKStA and LKA Kärnten was "gerichtlich abgeschlossen" (concluded in court). About 40,000 people were affected at home and abroad, with proven losses of about €20 million.
- **Tracing (section 6.5, pp. 53–54 [56]):** crypto is used in laundering, and anyone "kann selbst anonym eigene Wallets erstellen" (can create wallets anonymously). The 3rd Transfer of Funds Regulation (from 30 December 2024) eases traceability. Tracing and seizure remain "ressourcenintensiv" (resource-intensive).
- **Glossary (p. 68 [70]):** the C4 cybercrime centre has been able to seize crypto since 2018 and holds about 1,000 "Behördenwallets" (authority wallets).

**What the report does not say.** A text search of the whole report found no mention of robbery, home invasion, kidnapping or extortion by physical coercion against crypto holders. It also has no statement that leaked customer data were used to select phishing or physical targets, and nothing on tax data or DAC8. Pig-butchering targets are described as randomly chosen. The police statistics count offences by legal category; the report gives no crypto-motivated count of violent crime.

**Evidence class and relevance.** This is an official police situation report, with statistics taken from reported offences. It establishes that fraud and phishing are the dominant documented crypto-crime harms in Austria, and that exchange-account holders are a recognised phishing target group. It does not establish Austrian incidence of physical attacks. The seizure and tracing passages are adverse to absolute "irreversibility" claims but confirm that recovery is costly and not assured.
