# FMA circular on due diligence (01/2022): remote business chapter

FMA-Rundschreiben "Sorgfaltspflichten zur Prävention von Geldwäscherei und Terrorismusfinanzierung", document no. 01/2022, published 23 February 2022, status February 2022, 103 pages. The disclaimer says it is not a regulation. It states the FMA's legal view, and no rights or duties beyond the law follow from it. Locators are marginal numbers (Rz) and printed pages. No later consolidated version was found on fma.gv.at on 29 September 2026. FMA's December 2024 supervisory note (separate raw file) still cites the circular as the reference.

Key interpretive positions (chapter 5, pp. 72–78):
- **Absent safeguards (Rz 246):** remote identification without safeguards is a factor of potentially higher risk under Annex III.
- **Closed list and EDD (Rz 247):** the § 6 Abs. 4 safeguards are "taxativ" (a closed list). Once one is used, the mere absence of the customer creates *no* obligation to apply enhanced due diligence.
- **Five routes (Rz 248):** online identification, electronic ID, QES, registered mail, and first payment via a reference account.
- **Trust relationships (Rz 250):** remote routes are excluded where the customer acts as trustee.
- **Online identification (Rz 251–273):**
  - it may be used for third-country residents (Rz 253);
  - head movement and reading out the serial number are meant to defeat a photo held to the camera (Rz 264);
  - under the biometric route, the ID must be checked by reading the NFC chip from 1 January 2023 (Rz 268);
  - mandatory abort covers poor light, image quality or transmission (Rz 269–270);
  - where a suspicion arises, identification is completed, the relationship is refused, and an STR is considered (Rz 271);
  - service providers may be used, but responsibility stays with the obliged entity and FMA inspection must not be impaired (Rz 272–273).
- **QES (Rz 275–276):** the customer signs the legal declaration with a QES. The further conditions apply only to legal persons (seat declaration) and third-country residents (bank confirmation). The circular points to the EU Trust Services Dashboard for qualified providers.
- **Registered mail (Rz 277–278):** requires personal-delivery registered mail. The Austrian "Ident.Brief" procedure qualifies. An ID copy is required.
- **Reference account (Rz 279–283, also Rz 107):**
  - the first payment must come from an account in the customer's own name at a credit institution that is a qualified third party under § 13;
  - either copies of documents or the bank's written confirmation of prior identification are needed;
  - an account in a representative's name does not qualify.

Evidence class: supervisory interpretive guidance, non-binding but authoritative for FMA practice. It supports the point that the FMA treats the five remote routes as equivalent to in-person identification for risk purposes (Rz 247). This is relevant because the A-FIU and NRA 2025 report forged-ID fraud through exactly the QES and reference-payment routes. The circular pre-dates those findings. It does not measure fraud and does not show whether the FMA has since changed its position. For DAC8, it shows how Austrian CASP and bank identity records may rest on chained or delegated identification. That is a structural point, not a measured error rate.
