# DEDC Report 3: Emergency Economic Measures Order

Report of the Special Joint Committee on the Declaration of Emergency, "Third Report", December 2024, 44th Parliament, 1st Session; 132-page PDF retrieved 20 October 2026. Locators are printed page numbers.

## Scope of the measures (pp. 62–63)

"Once the Order was made, it culminated in the freezing of approximately 280 financial products, including bank accounts, credit cards and lines of credit, representing a total of $8 million, of which $3.8 million was from one payment processor." The RCMP reported 257 financial products frozen and 57 entities disclosed to financial institutions; the RCMP Commissioner said 170 bitcoin wallet addresses were shared with virtual-asset service providers. The Canadian Bankers Association put bank freezes at about 180 accounts worth CAD 8.3 million, "for the most part … larger accounts"; credit unions froze fewer than 10. Accounts were unfrozen as of 21 February 2022 and all were available "no later than 24 February 2022". The Order had no retroactive effect.

## Role of financial institutions and donors (pp. 63–64)

The RCMP sent lists of names to institutions, which decided whether to cease dealings; they faced no penalty for not following a list and had immunity under section 7 of the Order. Identifying information, "sometimes only a licence plate number", was shared. "[D]onors' names were never provided to financial institutions. Small donors or buyers who supported the protesters did not have their accounts frozen, unless they were within the prohibited area." Four or five accounts not on RCMP lists were frozen by banks. Testimony conflicted on how much discretion institutions had.

## Further context (p. 63)

No written economic impact assessment preceded the measures. FINTRAC witnesses reported no money laundering or terrorist financing that justified the Order. No charges were laid for contravening the Regulations or Order.

## Evidence class and limits

An official parliamentary committee report summarising testimony; figures differ by source and date. It does not decide legality (see [2024 FC 42](../articles/2026-10-20-canada-2024-fc-42-emergencies-act.md) and [2026 FCA 6](../articles/2026-10-20-canada-2026-fca-6-emergencies-act-appeal.md)). It contradicts claims that small donors had accounts frozen. Canadian emergency measures are not tax reporting and say nothing about DAC8.
