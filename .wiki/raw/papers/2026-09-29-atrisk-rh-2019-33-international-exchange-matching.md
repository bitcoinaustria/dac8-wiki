# Rechnungshof Bund 2019/33: International information exchange in tax matters

**What it is.** Austrian Court of Audit report, Reihe Bund 2019/33 (III-324 d.B. XXVI. GP). The audit ran from August 2017 to June 2018 at the request of a parliamentary minority (2087/A). It covered 2009–2017 at the BMF, the tax-investigation Central Liaison Office (CLO), large-business audit, PACC and twelve tax offices (pp. 17, 25). Footnote 2, p. 25: PACC was set up with effect from 1 June 2016 to steer deployment on a risk basis. Its stated means were a risk assessment of tax processes "nach neuesten wissenschaftlichen Methoden" (using the latest scientific methods) and the predictions it yields of the control and audit measures needed.

**Matching (TZ 13, pp. 51–54).** Of 255,590 DAC1 records for reporting periods 2014–2016 (as at 13 February 2018), about 86% were matched to taxpayers automatically. 35,178 (14%) needed manual matching by tax offices, and 1,367 (0.5%) could not be matched at all. Rates by sending state ranged from 94% (Germany) to below 10% (Denmark, Romania, Lithuania, Portugal). The BMF said matching DAC2 data was "deutlich schwieriger" (considerably harder) because the data come from many financial institutions. The IT procedures for DAC2 matching were still in a test phase. The Court criticised the fact that Austria had no stable TIN, because tax numbers changed when the competent office changed. It recommended fixed tax numbers.

**Volume (TZ 26, p. 84).** Austria first received "rd. 357.000 DAC2–Meldungen" (about 357,000 DAC2 records) in September 2017, for tax periods from 1 January 2016.

**Use (TZ 25, pp. 80–82).** Tax offices called automatic exchange valuable, but said incoming reports were often wrong or ambiguous. They said the actual scope and tax relevance could be clarified only in an investigation procedure. A BMF decree of October 2016 announced two lists. One was a worklist of matched persons with no existing tax link. The other covered cases selected by "der automatisierten Risikobewertung" (the automated risk assessment), mainly employee assessments. Neither list existed at the time of the audit. The Court concluded it was not ensured that the legally required assessments were made in all cases. It recommended delivering the worklists promptly (TZ 25.2), and the BMF answered in general terms (TZ 25.3–25.4). TZ 37 (pp. 115–116) records that PACC was responsible for quality assurance of the data to be evaluated.

**Evidence class and transfer.** This is an official audit with findings and ministry responses kept separate. It is strong evidence that exchanged financial-account data enter an identity-matching and worklist pipeline linked to automated risk assessment. It also shows that, at least in 2017–2018, the data were under-used and error-prone and needed human investigation before any tax consequence. It does not show current CRS use rates, CRS-driven additional revenue, or whether CRS data are PACC model features. The findings are eight or more years old. Low matching quality and non-use support a necessity argument against collecting more (DAC8). They also cut toward the State's reply that humans review exchanged data before acting.
