# US Treasury 2026 NMLRA: digital assets

The Department of the Treasury published the *2026 National Money Laundering Risk Assessment* in March 2026, together with the terrorist-financing and proliferation-financing assessments. It is the fifth money-laundering iteration since 2015 and succeeds the 2024 NMLRA currently cited in the wiki.

Section X ("Digital Assets", printed pp. 48–49; PDF p. 53) states: "In general, the volume of money laundering via digital assets remains well below that conducted through fiat currency and other methods that do not involve digital assets. However, certain illicit actors, particularly those engaged in digital asset-native crimes, may primarily use digital assets in the laundering process." It adds that "many illicit actors using digital assets prefer stablecoins" because of their relative stability and liquidity, and that they often convert stablecoins into fiat through diffuse channels, including OTC brokers. The section notes that monthly public-blockchain transactions reached 3.8 billion in early 2025 (a16z index proxy covering selected chains).

Limits: this is a US qualitative risk assessment. It gives no numerical share of laundering by channel and no global ratio. The updated wiki claim is `wiki/references/crypto-crime-measurement-and-aml-evidence.md` ("US Treasury's 2024 US assessment places virtual-asset laundering far below fiat and conventional methods"; evidence-matrix row "US Treasury NMLRA 2024"). The substance is unchanged, but the current edition is 2026 and it adds the stablecoin-preference finding.
