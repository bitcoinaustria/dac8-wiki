# A-FIU Lagebericht Geldwäsche 2024

Bundesministerium für Inneres / Bundeskriminalamt, Geldwäschemeldestelle (A-FIU), "Lagebericht Geldwäsche 2024" (Vienna, 2025; file version 20 October 2025). Page numbers are printed page numbers.

Volume (pp. 25–26): 12,924 file intakes in 2024; 11,086 STRs from obliged entities plus 25 from authorities. A new goAML-based counting method means earlier annual reports are not directly comparable; on the recalculated basis STRs rose about 15 percent. By sector: banks 9,704, VASPs 1,228 (about 20 percent more than 2023, attributed to crypto price gains; a further strong rise is expected under MiCAR), then single or double digits for notaries, insurers, lawyers, gambling and tax advisers. Quality (p. 27): 9 percent of first-time STRs were returned for improvement. Predicate categories (p. 28): fraud 38 percent, tax offences 11 percent, terrorist financing 7 percent, "money laundering without identified predicate" 10 percent.

Use and outcomes (pp. 30–34): of 11,111 STRs, 2,973 (27 percent) were sent as analysis reports to law enforcement or foreign FIUs; 8,138 were retained for passive future analysis "mangels Anfangsverdachts oder weiterer Analyseansätze". Foreign FIUs were the largest recipient group (15 percent of reports), about 2 percent went to tax/customs authorities. Asset-securing measures initiated by the A-FIU totalled about EUR 20 million in 2024. Final money-laundering convictions were 152 in 2024 (89, 85, 81, 120 in 2020–2023); the report states that analysis reports are often decisive for convictions for other offences, which it does not count.

KYC circumvention (pp. 35–36): the A-FIU reports bank accounts opened with forged Austrian passports bearing real, uninvolved persons' data, verified through qualified electronic signatures or one-cent reference-payment procedures; one group was linked to ten false identities and 38 accounts at three banks (21 at one bank), used to open further accounts and move fraud proceeds, sometimes where the real person already banked there. The A-FIU says outsourced remote identification leaves wide interpretive room and that quality differences between providers are "enorm". Crypto section (pp. 38–40): despite MiCAR/TFR, on-chain and off-chain analysis remains a significant challenge; multi-bridge transfers were increasingly used in fraud cases; it cites Chainalysis and Europol IOCTA.

Evidence class: official FIU statistics and typologies; self-reported, no cohort tracking from STR to outcome, no cost data. The EUR 20 million and 152 convictions are adverse to any claim that the Austrian system produces nothing, but they are not linked to specific STRs or to VASP reports. The identity-fraud case supports that a KYC-verified account can belong to a stolen identity; it is a case typology with an acknowledged unknown dark figure, not a prevalence estimate.
