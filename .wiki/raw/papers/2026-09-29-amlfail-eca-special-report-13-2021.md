# ECA Special Report 13/2021

Special Report 13/2021, "EU efforts to fight money laundering in the banking sector are fragmented and implementation is insufficient", adopted by Chamber IV on 20 May 2021 (OJ 2021/C 254/05). It audited the Commission (DG FISMA), the EBA and the ECB, focusing on the banking sector (paras 17–18). Paragraph I cites Europol's estimate of suspicious transactions at 1.3 percent of EU GDP and global estimates close to 3 percent of world GDP; these are borrowed figures, not audit findings.

Findings: "institutional fragmentation and poor co-ordination at EU level" and limited EU tools to ensure national application (paras V, 112); no autonomous EU high-risk third-country list beyond the FATF list after Council rejection in 2019 (paras 34–36); a supranational risk assessment that does not prioritise risk or give geographical focus, with residual risks rated "constantly significant" (paras 44–45, 49); Commission transposition checks taking 20–40 months against a six-month target, with some Member States overstating transposition (paras 54, 58); ML/TF statistics collected but "not reported on ... to date" (para 48); the EBA never opened an own-initiative ML/TF breach investigation (para 70), delayed Commission requests (para 74), faced documented lobbying of panel members (para 78) and made one positive ML/TF breach-of-Union-law finding since 2010 (para 119). The ECB made good progress on information-sharing (para VII).

Evidence class: official EU performance audit of governance and implementation. It does not measure whether AML reduces laundering or crime, and it does not audit national FIUs, obliged-entity CDD or compliance costs. Its critique supported the move to directly applicable regulation and AMLA (recommendation 2, para 117), so it is not evidence that the AML model itself should be abandoned.

Transfer limits: supports the claim that EU-level AML implementation and monitoring were fragmented and that the EU lacked published statistics to measure performance as of 2021. It predates AMLR/AMLA and MiCA/TFR crypto rules and does not concern DAC8 or tax-information exchange (for DAC, see ECA Special Report 03/2021).
