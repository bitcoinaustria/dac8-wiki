# Cancer Loyalty Card Study (CLOCS)

Hannah R. Brewer, Yasemin Hirst, Marc Chadeau-Hyam, Eric Johnson, Sudha Sundar, James M. Flanagan, *JMIR Public Health and Surveillance* 9: e41762 (2023), PMC9912145. The abstract (Europe PMC) and the PMC full text were read through a text-extraction tool.

**Design and findings.** This was an observational case-control study of loyalty-card purchase histories from two UK high-street retailers. It compared women with (n = 153) and without (n = 120) ovarian cancer, all with explicit consent. Purchases of pain and indigestion medication were higher among cases, with maximum discrimination 8 months before diagnosis (Fisher exact OR 2.9, 95% CI 2.1–4.1). Increased indigestion-medication purchases were detectable up to 9 months before diagnosis (adjusted OR 1.38, 95% CI 1.04–1.83). The maximum AUC was 0.65 at 13 months (0.68 for late-stage disease). The authors restrict data access "due to … the sensitive nature of the data". Their stated limitations are that purchase is not consumption, only two retailers were covered, and the sample was reduced by COVID-19.

**Evidence class and DAC8 transfer.** A peer-reviewed but small study; confidence is medium on sample size and modest discrimination. It gives empirical support to the regulators' examples (WP29 WP251rev.01, EDPB 06/2020) that ordinary purchase records can carry health signals. The data are product-level retail baskets, far more granular than payment records, which usually show merchant and amount but not items. The finding does not transfer to crypto payment records or DAC8 aggregates. Its use is illustrative: it shows why Article 9 GDPR analysis can reach non-medical commercial records.
