# Known Physical Bitcoin Attacks (Lopp list)

Source digest. Repository `jlopp/physical-bitcoin-attacks`, README table and CONTRIBUTING.md, fetched 29 September 2026. The latest commit was on 25 September 2026. The repository also has `attacks.json`, a map/dashboard, and a copy of an academic paper on wrench attacks (`Wrench_Attacks_Research.pdf`).

The README describes the list as "not comprehensive; many attacks are not publicly reported." CONTRIBUTING.md admits only attacks with a physical-world component and a crypto-related motive or target, and requires "at least one credible published source (news article, police report, court document)". Purely digital incidents such as SIM swaps, phishing and exchange hacks are excluded, as are verbal or online threats without physical action. Most entries link to a media article plus an archive copy.

Counts from the README table, parsed locally by year (one row per incident; rows with a partial date are included): 2014: 1; 2015: 5; 2016: 4; 2017: 12; 2018: 26; 2019: 10; 2020: 15; 2021: 36; 2022: 36; 2023: 26; 2024: 42; 2025: 87; 2026 (to 19 September): 63; total 363. France accounts for 23 of the 2025 rows and 42 of the 2026 rows. Austrian rows: Wels 2018 (robbers posing as postmen) and Wels 2019, plus a Vienna November 2025 homicide. In the Vienna case, per media, the victim was betrayed by a fellow student and killed after revealing wallets. Only a few rows mention a data source. Examples: a November 2025 Savoie entry linking to reporting on the Waltio leak, and a 2024 doxxing entry. No row attributes an attack to tax-authority data.

Limits: the list is self-curated and depends on the media. Reporting intensity, language coverage and the maintainer's attention bias the counts, and there is no denominator. Rows are not independently verified. They combine robberies during in-person trades, kidnappings and attacks on businesses. The list undercounts compared with official French figures: a minister reported 45 French cases for 2025 and 77 for the first half of 2026 (see `2026-09-29-bearer-france-interior-crypto-guidance.md`). It can show that the threat class exists and a trend in reported cases. It cannot give the attack probability for a reported person or show what DAC8 adds to that probability.
