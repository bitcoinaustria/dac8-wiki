# OGH 5 Ob 54/24p (3 June 2025)

Oberster Gerichtshof, decision of 3 June 2025, 5 Ob 54/24p, ECLI:AT:OGH0002:2025:0050OB00054.24P.0603.000 (ÖBA 2025, 833). The case was an injunction action under § 28a KSchG by a qualified consumer association against a bank. It came up from HG Wien 11 Cg 23/23d and OLG Wien 3 R 153/23a.

**Practice challenged (para 3).** When a customer says a payment was unauthorised, the bank does not re-credit the account if it believes it has damages claims for careless conduct or other defences.

**Lower courts (paras 6–8).** Both lower courts found the practice unlawful in core respects. HG Wien held that the refund may be withheld only on an obvious fraud suspicion reported to the FMA, and that the bank must otherwise pursue its damages claim actively. OLG Wien confirmed, by partial judgment, the prohibition on refusing rectification on the ground of a damages claim.

**OGH (paras 12–16).** Proceedings are stayed until the CJEU rules in C-70/25. The OGH records the Polish question: whether Article 73(1) with Article 74(1) PSD2 lets a provider refuse the immediate refund where the payer was grossly negligent. It then notes three points:

- § 67 ZaDiG 2018 implements Article 73 PSD2, and the practice rests on claims under § 68(3) (Article 74(1)).
- In 8 Ob 106/20a the 8th Senate departed from 10 Ob 102/15w, which had allowed set-off under the former ZaDiG.
- Because of contrary German commentary, there is no acte clair.

Also on record: RIS Rechtssatz RS0133685 (8 Ob 106/20a, 25 March 2021, T4) holds that clauses denying a refund of an unauthorised transaction on intentional or grossly negligent breach of care duties contradict § 67 ZaDiG.

**Use.** This is official Austrian case status. As of the OGH's stay, whether Austrian banks may withhold the immediate refund for alleged gross negligence was unresolved pending C-70/25; no later OGH decision was checked. It concerns unauthorised payments only, not payments the customer released under manipulation (see 7 Ob 95/24g). For the bearer-asset comparison: the credential-theft refund backstop is strong in principle but contested in its timing.
