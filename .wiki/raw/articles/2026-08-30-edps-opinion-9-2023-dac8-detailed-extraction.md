# EDPS Opinion 9/2023 on DAC8: detailed recommendations

This record supplements the earlier source stub and preserves the recommendations that matter to a lifecycle analysis. It describes an institutional opinion on the Commission proposal, not binding law and not the final enacted text.

## Extracted recommendations

- Paragraphs 5–6 recall data protection by design and default, data minimisation and accuracy and recommend that the proposal ensure compliance with Charter Article 8, the GDPR and Regulation (EU) 2018/1725.
- Paragraphs 7–16 address the proposed expansion of Article 16 DAC beyond the taxes covered by the Directive to VAT, other indirect taxes, customs and AML/CFT. The EDPS recommended that further processing for other purposes be possible only under a necessary and proportionate Union or Member-State rule satisfying GDPR Article 23(1), and that the proposal contain an exhaustive list of permitted further-processing purposes.
- Paragraphs 21–24 address the central directory and recommend clarifying the purpose of Commission access and limiting Commission statistical access to anonymous or aggregated data.
- Paragraphs 25–26 ask the legislator to allocate controller, joint-controller and processor responsibilities clearly.
- Paragraphs 27–29 accept that a minimum period may serve audits but recommend a harmonised maximum retention period and deletion at that maximum or earlier when continued storage is no longer necessary.

## Final-text comparison boundary

The final Directive narrows the Commission's ordinary access to the relevant directory data to statistical purposes, with separate technical access confined to maintenance, and identifies Member States and reporting entities as controllers and the Commission as processor. It does not enact a single closed tax-only purpose: Article 16 permits several expressly listed public purposes and conditional further national uses. Article 22(3) requires received automatic-exchange records to be kept at least five years but no longer than necessary and states no Union-wide maximum for those recipient copies.

The opinion therefore supports scrutiny of purpose breadth, role clarity and retention safeguards. It does not establish that the enacted Directive is invalid or that every use listed in Article 16 is disproportionate.
