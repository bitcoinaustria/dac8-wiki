# CJEU Ligue des droits humains on systematic profiling, retention and review

In C-817/19, judgment of 21 June 2022, the Grand Chamber described the systematic processing of all passengers as a serious interference and continuous, untargeted and systematic surveillance. It nevertheless did not invalidate the PNR Directive, instead construing it narrowly and requiring objective criteria, documentation, human review of automated matches and effective independent control (paragraphs 92 to 117, 148 to 180 and 202 to 210).

A general initial six-month retention period could remain, while continued general five-year retention without an objective risk connection could not (paragraphs 248 to 262). The case therefore shows that universal intake is not automatically invalid and that later retention and use require separate justification.

The PNR regime serves terrorism and serious-crime prevention and uses travel data for immediate risk assessment. DAC8 serves tax administration through annual reporting. The transferable method is lifecycle-specific review; the surveillance label and PNR conclusions are not direct rules for tax reporting.
