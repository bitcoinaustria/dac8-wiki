# Eric Hughes, "A Cypherpunk's Manifesto"

Text at activism.net, signed "Eric Hughes … 9 March 1993"; retrieved and re-checked 20 October 2026.

## Exact quotations (opening paragraphs)

"Privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age. Privacy is not secrecy. A private matter is something one doesn't want the whole world to know, but a secret matter is something one doesn't want anybody to know. Privacy is the power to selectively reveal oneself to the world."

"When I purchase a magazine at a store and hand cash to the clerk, there is no need to know who I am."

The surrounding text says each party to a transaction should know only what is directly necessary for it, and calls cash "the primary such system" of anonymous transactions.

## Evidence class and limits

A short advocacy essay; not peer reviewed and not legal authority. It supports a definition and a framing, not any factual claim about current payment systems, surveillance effects or bitcoin. The manifesto does not mention bitcoin, which came 15 years later. A statement that bitcoin "stands in the cypherpunk tradition" needs a separate historical source; the whitepaper's references cite Wei Dai's b-money (1998) and Adam Back's Hashcash (2002), but this record does not establish their movement affiliation.
