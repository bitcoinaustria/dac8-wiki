# Council Directive 2011/16/EU (DAC1) as originally adopted

## Source identity

- Council Directive 2011/16/EU of 15 February 2011 on administrative cooperation in the field of taxation and repealing Directive 77/799/EEC; CELEX `32011L0016`; OJ L 64, 11.3.2011, p. 1; legal basis Articles 113 and 115 TFEU
- Entry into force on publication, 11 March 2011 (Article 30)
- Transposition: general provisions with effect from 1 January 2013; Article 8 (automatic exchange) with effect from 1 January 2015 (Article 29(1))
- Repeal of Directive 77/799/EEC with effect from 1 January 2013 (Article 28)
- In force, amended by DAC2 to DAC9 (M1–M9 in the 2026 consolidation; see [2026-08-28-eu-directive-2011-16-consolidated-2026.md](2026-08-28-eu-directive-2011-16-consolidated-2026.md))

## Content

The recitals describe growing taxpayer mobility and cross-border transactions and conclude that Directive 77/799/EEC, even as amended, "no longer" met the requirements of administrative cooperation, so that a new instrument was needed. Recital 9 introduces the standard of "foreseeable relevance" and excludes "fishing expeditions". Recital 10 calls mandatory automatic exchange without preconditions the most effective means and adopts a step-by-step approach starting with five categories.

Article 8(1) as adopted required automatic exchange of information "regarding taxable periods as from 1 January 2014" that is "available" concerning residents of another Member State on: income from employment; director's fees; life insurance products not covered by other Union instruments; pensions; ownership of and income from immovable property. Exchange takes place at least once a year, within six months after the end of the tax year in which the information became available (Article 8(6)). For calendar-year Member States this puts the first exchange in 2015; the exact first-exchange date per Member State is not verified here. Article 8(5) envisaged a Commission report by 1 July 2017 and possible extension to dividends, capital gains and royalties.

The directive also restates exchange on request and spontaneous exchange, presence of foreign officials, simultaneous controls, administrative notification and feedback. The information exchanged comes from tax authorities' own files; the original text imposes no new reporting duty on private parties.

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

DAC1 is the base act that every later DAC amends; DAC8 is an amendment to this directive, not a free-standing law. The "available information" limitation and the foreseeable-relevance standard for requests are the two baselines against which later mandatory third-party reporting (DAC2, DAC6, DAC7, DAC8) can be contrasted. The CJEU interpreted foreseeable relevance and judicial review in Berlioz (see [2026-09-30-eu-dac1-cjeu-c682-15-berlioz.md](2026-09-30-eu-dac1-cjeu-c682-15-berlioz.md)).
