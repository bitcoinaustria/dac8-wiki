# DAC8 and CARF routing, purpose and onward-transfer chain

Under Article 8ad(2) of the consolidated Directive 2011/16/EU, the Member State receiving a report from a reporting crypto-asset service provider communicates the information to the competent authorities of the affected Member States. Article 21(5) provides residence-gated Member-State access to DAC8 records in the secure central directory. This is inconsistent with describing the system as automatic distribution of every full report to every participating State.

Article 16(1) permits use of exchanged DAC information not only for taxes within the Directive but also for VAT, other indirect taxes, customs, AML/CFT, recovery claims, compulsory social-security contributions and tax-law penalty proceedings. Article 16(2) permits other uses subject to a lawful recipient-state basis and origin-state approval or a communicated authorised-purpose list. Article 16(3) permits onward communication to another Member State where the information may be useful for an authorised purpose, subject to notice to the originating authority and its opportunity to object.

Article 22(3) requires automatically exchanged information received from another Member State to be kept for at least five years but no longer than necessary. Article 25 assigns controller and processor roles, imposes data-subject information and security duties and limits access within the directory. These rules are safeguards adverse to a claim of unlimited use. They do not by themselves identify every Austrian originating copy, derived file, backup, access role or actual purpose.

Austria's [Krypto-MPfG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/BgblAuth/BGBLA_2025_I_96/BGBLA_2025_I_96.html), especially sections 14 and 21, also provides for exchange with participating third countries. An active CARF exchange relationship requires the applicable bilateral activation and notifications under the [CARF Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issues/tax-transparency-and-international-co-operation/text-carf-mcaa.pdf), not merely signature of the agreement.

For information governed by the [Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/317/317.en.pdf), Article 22 limits disclosure and use principally to authorities, courts and supervisory bodies concerned with covered tax functions. Other-purpose use requires that it be permissible under the law of the supplying State and authorised by that State. The broader intra-EU Article 16 DAC purpose list must therefore not be assumed to govern every CARF third-country dataset.

The precise litigation facts are the routing rule, actual recipient, source regime, active exchange relationship, purpose code, controller, access log, onward-transfer record and retention rule for each copy.
