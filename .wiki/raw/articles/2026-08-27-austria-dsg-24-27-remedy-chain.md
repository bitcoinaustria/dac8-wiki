# Austria DSG sections 24 and 27: DSB-to-BVwG remedy chain

Section 24(1) DSG gives every data subject a right to complain to the Austrian Data Protection Authority when processing of their personal data is alleged to violate the GDPR, section 1 DSG, or the first chapter of Article 2 DSG. The complaint must identify the right, respondent where reasonably possible, facts, legal grounds, requested finding, and timeliness. An underlying data-subject request and the controller's response must be attached where relevant.

The treatment claim expires one year after knowledge of the complained-of event and, at the latest, three years after the alleged event. A successful complaint concerning a private-sector controller can lead to an order to comply with access, rectification, erasure, restriction, or portability requests to the extent required to cure the violation. A respondent may cure a violation while the DSB case is pending, which can make the complaint moot unless the complainant timely explains the remaining violation.

The DSB must inform the complainant within three months about the status and outcome of the investigation. Section 24(8) permits recourse to the BVwG if the DSB does not deal with the complaint or provide that information. Under section 27, the BVwG decides DSB appeals and delay complaints through a senate consisting of a chair and two expert lay judges.

For a DAC8 case, these provisions make an identified CASP's concrete processing under section 20(1) Krypto-MPfG reviewable without first breaching any legal duty. They do not guarantee success on the merits and do not authorize the DSB to make an Article 267 reference.
