# BMF Disziplinarkommission, 30 September 2016 (01069/7-DK/14 and 01072/7-DK/15): thousands of private AIS queries

Senate I of the Disziplinarkommission beim Bundesministerium für Finanzen decided on 30 September 2016 against an Amtsdirektor of a Finanzamt, with names and places anonymised in RIS. The decision reproduces a final (rechtskräftig) criminal judgment of a Landesgericht, date anonymised, for Missbrauch der Amtsgewalt under § 302 StGB. From 2008 to 2014 he had accessed AIS records without official reason or the data subjects' knowledge, damaging their right to data protection. The decision describes this as "mehrere tausend Angriffe auf Datensätze" (several thousand accesses to records), covering 16 colleagues, over 700 accesses to persons with one surname, 16 celebrities and athletes, and over 1,000 persons from his hometown. The criminal sentence was six months' suspended imprisonment plus a EUR 4,800 fine.

The database queries were established by the BIA (Büro für Interne Angelegenheiten, the BMF internal-affairs office) and the BAK acting for the public prosecutor, after the Senate chair reported suspected § 302 conduct. The decision quotes the BMF Erlass of 30 October 2000 (GZ 66 1009/30-VI/6/00): AIS entry or query "ist nur dann zulässig, wenn eine dienstliche Veranlassung vorliegt" (is permitted only where there is an official reason). The disciplinary penalty was a EUR 5,000 fine. The Senate reasoned that specific prevention did not predominate because he had admitted fault with a remorseful, credible confession; general prevention required a clear signal that directives must be followed.

Evidence class: official disciplinary decision reciting a final criminal conviction. This is the strongest Austrian tax-administration insider case found. It concerns the production tax database, a long undetected period (about six years), and very large-scale curiosity browsing, including of the general public, not only colleagues. The document does not say what first triggered the investigation or whether routine log review played a role. It shows no disclosure to third parties or resulting harm, and it is not evidence about DAC8 crypto-asset data or current BMF controls. The retained employment and fine-level sanction are relevant to deterrence arguments; they are not proof of systemic leniency.
