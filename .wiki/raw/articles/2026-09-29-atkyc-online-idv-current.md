# FMA Online-Identifikationsverordnung (Online-IDV), current version

RIS consolidated text, version of 29 September 2026. The FMA issued the regulation with the Finance Minister's consent under § 6 Abs. 4 FM-GwG. Original text: BGBl. II Nr. 5/2017. Amendments: BGBl. II Nr. 199/2018, 169/2020, 414/2020, 265/2021, 455/2021, 572/2021 and 470/2022. No amendment after 2022 appears in the consolidated text. § 3 Abs. 4, inserted by BGBl. II 169/2020, lapsed on 31 December 2023 (§ 9 Abs. 1). § 7 still refers to the FM-GwG as amended by BGBl. I 37/2018, a static cross-reference.

Scope (§ 1): the regulation sets the safeguards needed to offset the higher risk of identifying a person who is not physically present when a video-based electronic procedure ("Online-Identifikation") is used. The safeguards apply without prejudice to other FM-GwG duties and to data-protection law. Obliged entities may add further measures (§ 1 Abs. 2–4). It does **not** regulate the other remote routes in § 6 Abs. 4 FM-GwG: electronic ID, qualified electronic signature (QES), registered mail, or first payment from a reference account.

Required safeguards:
- **Documents (§ 2 Z 2):** the official photo ID must have optical security features at least equivalent to holograms.
- **Staff and premises (§ 3):** staff must be trained and reliable, and must work in a separate access-controlled room.
- **Recording and liveness (§ 4 Abs. 2–3):** the whole identification conversation must be audio-recorded, with screenshots of the face and both sides or data pages of the ID. The customer must move their head and read out the ID serial number or a random string of at least four characters.
- **Document checks (§ 4 Abs. 4):** staff must check tilt-visible holograms, the serial-number format, laminate integrity, that the photo has not been attached afterwards, and consistency between the person, photo, dates and existing customer data.
- **One-time code (§ 4 Abs. 5):** the customer must enter a one-time code sent by e-mail or SMS during the live video.
- **Automated biometric identification (§ 4 Abs. 6):** it is allowed only with GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) consent. It must be state of the art, at least equivalent to staff-conducted identification, and actively monitored. Its checks must be documented. It needs a video-based presence (liveness) check, which may be passive. Only ID documents whose content is electronically signed by the issuing authority (read from the chip) may be used, with signature, integrity and key-compromise checks. Until 31 December 2022, § 9 Abs. 2 allowed biometric procedures that did not use the chip, provided they met § 4 Abs. 4 Z 1–5.
- **Mandatory abort (§ 5 Abs. 1):** the procedure must stop if proper verification is impossible or if there are other inconsistencies or uncertainties. **Exception (§ 5 Abs. 2):** where a § 5 Z 4 or 5 FM-GwG suspicion case applies, identification is completed and a suspicious-transaction report (STR) under § 16 FM-GwG must be considered.
- **Processors (§ 6):** an obliged entity using a processor must ensure the processor's safeguards match the regulation in scope and quality. Final responsibility stays with the obliged entity, and outsourcing under § 15 FM-GwG must not materially impair internal control or FMA inspection.

Evidence class: enacted secondary legislation (primary legal source). It supports the point that Austrian law imposes detailed, prescriptive safeguards on video and biometric remote identification, and that responsibility cannot be outsourced. This is adverse to any claim that the remote-identification route is unregulated. It does not show how well the rules are followed or how often they are defeated. For DAC8, it describes one legal route by which the identity data later reused for reporting is collected. It is not evidence about DAC8 data quality.
