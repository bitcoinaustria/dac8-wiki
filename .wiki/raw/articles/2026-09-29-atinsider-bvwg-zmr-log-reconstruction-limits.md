# BVwG W214 2016489-1 (25 November 2016): ZMR access logs could not reconstruct a historical-address query

On 14 February 2014 a private detective at a municipal registration office obtained the complainant's residence history since 1988. An official made three ZMR queries, and the history appeared in a detective report marked "(amtliche Quelle)" (official source). The Datenschutzbehörde rejected the complaint on 27 October 2014. The Bundesverwaltungsgericht dismissed the appeal (Spruch A, § 28(1)–(2) VwGVG) and admitted an ordinary Revision under Art 133(4) B-VG. The result of any Revision was not checked.

On the merits, the court held that MeldeG (then § 16(1), now § 18(1)) permits information on former residences where there is a "berechtigtes Interesse" (legitimate interest). Such an interest is to be assumed for licensed private detectives under §§ 129–130 GewO gathering evidence for civil proceedings. The disclosure was therefore lawful; this is not an insider-misuse finding.

The record matters for detection. BMI witnesses stated that "anhand der derzeitigen datenschutzrechtlichen Protokollierung der Anwendung ZMR nicht nachvollziehbar sei, ob im Zuge der Anfrage eine historische Anfrage stattgefunden habe oder nicht" (under the ZMR data-protection logging then in use, it could not be traced whether a historical query was made). Technical logs, "die nach 60 Tagen gelöscht werden" (deleted after 60 days), would have shown the exact query. According to the BMI witness's e-mail of 4 November 2016 (procedural history, para 20), the BMI took the case as occasion to redesign ZMR data-protection logging so that future logs record whether a "historisch" or "formalisiert" query was made, traceable for three years. The court made its factual finding from other evidence, including the detective's assignment and report.

Evidence class: administrative-court decision, with witness statements from BMI staff recorded as findings. It is Austrian evidence that retained access logs may lack the fields needed to prove what was retrieved, and that more detailed technical logs had short retention. It is also adverse authority: legal access by a private intermediary was upheld despite the complainant's objection. It concerns the population register (ZMR), not tax data, and 2014 logging. It does not establish current ZMR or BMF log design.
