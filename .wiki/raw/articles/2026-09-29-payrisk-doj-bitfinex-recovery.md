# DOJ: Bitfinex hack recovery

Source: US Department of Justice, Office of Public Affairs, "Bitfinex Hacker and Wife Plead Guilty to Money Laundering Conspiracy Involving Billions in Cryptocurrency", 3 August 2023. It was read through an Internet Archive capture because justice.gov returned no text to automated requests. Later restitution filings were seen only in secondary reporting and not verified.

**Facts stated by DOJ.**

- Lichtenstein "fraudulently authorized more than 2,000 transactions in which 119,754 bitcoin was transferred from Bitfinex" to his wallet. This was a 2016 exchange hack, not a customer-authorised payment.
- He and Morgan were arrested in February 2022 "after the government seized approximately 95,000 of those stolen bitcoin from cryptocurrency wallets in the defendants' control", valued at about USD 3.6bn at the time. "Since their arrests, the government has seized another approximately $475 million tied to the hack."
- Laundering methods included fictitious identities, automated transactions, passing funds through darknet-market and exchange accounts, chain hopping into anonymity-enhanced coins, the mixers Bitcoin Fog, Helix and ChipMixer, US business accounts, and gold coins that were buried.
- Third-party claims to forfeited property were to follow the Rule 32.2 procedure.

**Evidence class.** This is an official prosecutorial statement. The facts are admitted in a guilty plea but remain the government's account.

**Use.** The case is strong evidence that stolen crypto can be traced and recovered: about 79% of the bitcoin was seized, largely because the thief kept it in wallets he controlled. It is adverse to the claim that stolen crypto "can never be recovered". It does not support a general rate. It is one outlier case of a custodial exchange hack, recovered about six years later through criminal forfeiture rather than a victim's claim, and the victim was the exchange. It says nothing about wrench attacks on individual self-custody holders.
