# OECD Global Forum statement on the Bulgarian NRA breach

Statement dated 30 August 2019; archived copy of 23 January 2024 retrieved 20 October 2026 (the original oecd.org URL has since moved).

## Exact statements

"The information technology system of the National Revenue Agency (NRA) of Bulgaria was hacked and a significant amount of its data was provided to the media on 15 July 2019. Bulgaria subsequently confirmed that this data was from NRA systems and that it included data automatically exchanged with international treaty partners under the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement and the EU Directive on Administrative Cooperation."

"All jurisdictions' obligations to automatically send data to Bulgaria will remain suspended until a satisfactory review has been concluded and the deficiencies identified have been addressed."

"It is important to note that the breach was not linked to the OECD Common Transmission System". The statement adds that breaches "can never be entirely eliminated" and that the Global Forum operates pre- and post-exchange security assessments and a breach-response mechanism.

## Relevance and limits

The leak occurred in a receiving tax administration's own systems, not in the transmission channel. It concerned CRS/DAC2 financial-account data, not DAC8 crypto data. The statement does not quantify affected persons; the CJEU later recorded more than six million (see [C-340/21 paragraph 12](2026-10-20-cjeu-c340-21-para-12-affected-persons.md)). Exchanges with Bulgaria later resumed (see the [Swiss status record](2026-09-19-sif-exchange-status-and-conditions.md)), so the suspension is evidence of both failure and a working safeguard.
