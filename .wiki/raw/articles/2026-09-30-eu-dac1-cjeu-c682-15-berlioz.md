# CJEU C-682/15 Berlioz Investment Fund

- Judgment of the Court (Grand Chamber) of 16 May 2017, Berlioz Investment Fund SA v Directeur de l'administration des contributions directes, C-682/15, CELEX `62015CJ0682`
- Reference from Luxembourg concerning a French request under Directive 2011/16/EU and a penalty for refusing to answer an information order.

Holdings, in summary of the operative part:

1. A Member State implements EU law under Article 51(1) of the Charter when it penalises a person who refuses to supply information for an exchange under Directive 2011/16/EU, so the Charter applies.
2. Under Article 47 of the Charter, a person penalised for not complying with such an information order may challenge the order's legality.
3. Foreseeable relevance (Articles 1(1) and 5) is a condition the request must satisfy and therefore a condition of the legality of the information order and the penalty.
4. The requested authority must check more than procedural regularity: it must be satisfied the information is not devoid of foreseeable relevance. The national court may review the order's legality, but only to verify that the information does not manifestly lack such relevance.
5. The court of the requested State must have access to the request. The person concerned has no right of access to the whole request, which stays secret under Article 16 of the directive; in principle it suffices that the person has the information listed in Article 20(2) (identity of the person under examination and the tax purpose).

Relevance and limits for DAC8: Berlioz concerns exchange on request, not automatic exchange, and the reviewed standard is deliberately light. It establishes that DAC enforcement against information holders is within the Charter's scope.
