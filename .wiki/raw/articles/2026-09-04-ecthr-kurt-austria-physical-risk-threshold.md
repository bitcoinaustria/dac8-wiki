# ECtHR Kurt v Austria on the real and immediate physical-risk threshold

In *Kurt v Austria*, application 62903/15, Grand Chamber judgment of 15 June 2021, the ECtHR restated that an operational Article 2 protection duty arises only where authorities knew or ought to have known of a real and immediate risk to the life of an identified individual from third-party criminal acts and failed to take reasonably available protective measures. Not every claimed risk to life can trigger the duty, which must not impose an impossible or disproportionate burden (paragraphs 157 to 159).

The case concerned domestic violence and a concrete operational protection duty, not the validity of general data legislation. It is adverse to presenting the existence of crypto-related physical attacks as a free-standing Article 2 invalidity claim. A specific documented threat to an identified person may change the analysis; generalized risk belongs primarily in the severity, security and proportionality assessment under privacy and data-protection rights.
