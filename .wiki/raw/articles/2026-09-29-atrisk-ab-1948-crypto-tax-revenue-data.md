# Parliamentary answer 1948/AB (XXVIII. GP)

**What it is.** Written answer by Finance Minister Markus Marterbauer dated 14 July 2025 (GZ 2025-0.380.510). It answers question 2411/J of 14 May 2025 (Christoph Pramhofer et al.) on crypto tax revenue in connection with the Bitcoin popular initiative. The data are KESt returns plus income- and corporate-tax declaration data, as at 3 June 2025 (p. 1).

**Figures (pp. 2–3).** Declared income or surpluses from crypto-currencies were EUR 35,596,967.93 in 2022 and EUR 14,626,898.74 in 2023. Declared crypto losses were EUR −23,428,529.94 in 2022 and EUR −5,541,959.83 in 2023. Declaration data for 2023 were not yet complete. The answer stresses that these amounts are part of the tax base, not tax revenue. Mandatory KESt withholding on crypto started only for income received from 1 January 2024. Crypto KESt withheld in 2024 was EUR 33,839,499.66, which is 0.57% of total KESt of EUR 5.90bn. Bitcoin is not recorded separately because no key figure exists for it, and introducing one would be disproportionate (questions 4–5).

**Evidence class and transfer.** This is official ministerial data. It gives a baseline for the proportionality balance: the known crypto tax base and withheld tax are small compared with the scope of DAC8/Krypto-MPfG collection. Limits:

- The figures cover only declared and withheld amounts. The State will argue that undeclared foreign-platform gains are exactly the gap DAC8 is meant to close.
- The figures say nothing about risk scoring.
- The BMF's reasoning on questions 4–5 can be cited by analogy: it declines to add an asset-specific statistical key figure where tax consequences do not differ, calling it disproportionate administrative effort. That reasoning concerns tax statistics, not DAC8 data collection.
