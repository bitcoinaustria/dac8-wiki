# ECtHR Gaughran on technological change and undifferentiated retention

In *Gaughran v United Kingdom*, application 45245/15, judgment of 13 February 2020, the Court found the indefinite, undifferentiated retention of biometric data disproportionate because it did not vary by offence, continuing need or genuine review (paragraphs 77 to 97).

The Court took account of later technical developments that made stored photographs automatically searchable. It also rejected a generic claim that retaining more data must improve crime control, while cautioning that the existence of a different or more lenient model does not alone prove disproportionality (paragraphs 86, 89 and 95).

Biometric police data and indefinite retention are far removed from annual tax aggregates and fixed statutory periods. The narrow DAC8 relevance is that actual present linkage technology and a genuine review of continued need can matter; neither abstract future capability nor another State's shorter period is enough by itself.
