# VwGH 2010/07/0171: declaratory order justified by future sanction risk

In its judgment of 24 October 2013, the VwGH stated that a declaratory order not expressly provided by statute may be admissible where it serves the public interest or is a necessary means of effective legal protection for the party. It must be suitable to clarify a right or legal relationship for the future and remove a present or future threat to a subjective right.

The court allowed the application in the case before it. The legal situation had materially changed after a VfGH annulment, the applicant sought clarification for future conduct, and it faced a concrete risk of administrative punishment. The earlier proceeding therefore did not eliminate the separate current legal interest.

This is counterauthority to any categorical claim that an unprovided declaratory order is always displaced by another proceeding. Applied to Krypto-MPfG only by analogy, it may strengthen a reporting CASP that can identify the competent authority, a genuinely unresolved duty, and a current sanction risk. It does not itself establish such a procedure or competent authority for an ordinary crypto user.
