# CJEU C-414/24: parallel GDPR supervisory and judicial remedies

The Court of Justice decided Case C-414/24, *Datenschutzbehörde and Dr G S*, on 18 June 2026 on a reference from the Austrian Verwaltungsgerichtshof.

## Holding

GDPR Articles 77 and 79 provide concurrent and independent remedies. A supervisory authority may not reject an Article 77 complaint solely because the data subject previously brought an Article 79 judicial action concerning the same subject matter and that judicial decision is not yet final. National law may coordinate the proceedings, including through a stay, but may not deprive the data subject of effective protection.

The Court also restated that a supervisory authority must investigate an Article 77 complaint with due diligence. It has discretion to select suitable and necessary Article 58(2) measures, but must act where one or more corrective measures are appropriate, necessary, and proportionate to remedy an established deficiency and ensure full GDPR compliance. Article 58(2) includes temporary or definitive processing restrictions and suspension of transfers to a third-country or international-organisation recipient.

## Relevance and limit

The judgment confirms that an Austrian data subject may have both the DSB route and an independent judicial GDPR route against a controller or processor. It therefore contradicts any general claim that a direct Article 140 B-VG Individualantrag is the only available private-person vehicle for challenging DAC8-related processing.

The Court did not decide whether Krypto-MPfG processing is lawful, whether a specific preventive order must issue, or how an Austrian court should resolve a conflict between the GDPR and a statutory reporting duty. Those questions remain claimant-, remedy-, and merits-specific.
