# Kroll claimant-data breach (August 2023)

Source digest. Two notices from Kroll Restructuring Administration LLC, claims and noticing agent in *In re FTX Trading Ltd.*, Case No. 22-11068 (Bankr. D. Del.):

- [Initial claimant notification](https://ftxquestions.kroll.com/docs/Kroll_Claimant_Notification_4894-1885-7851_v1_2.pdf).
- [Supplementary notice to FTX claimants](https://ftxquestions.kroll.com/docs/Kroll_Notice_to_FTX_Claimants_4879-3276-7625-2.pdf).

Locators: "What happened?", "What information was involved?", "Important Information for FTX Claimants".

On or about 19 August 2023, an unauthorised party took control of a Kroll employee's mobile number and accessed Kroll's cloud-based files on FTX claimants. Kroll announced the incident on 25 August 2023 and says it affected claimants of three crypto companies. The initial notice lists name, address, email address and FTX account balance. The supplementary notice lists email, name, phone number, address, claim number, claim amount, FTX account ID and/or "coin holdings and balances", plus date of birth for a limited number. Kroll says FTX systems and assets were unaffected and that it held no FTX passwords. It warns that "the attacker might use this information in a further scam" to gain access to "cryptocurrency accounts, wallets or other digital assets, wherever they may be held". It lists actions that neither the court nor the agent will ever request, such as linking a wallet or giving a seed phrase.

Evidence class: first-party breach notices issued in a court-supervised bankruptcy. They are not a judicial finding on harm. Security journalism (e.g. KrebsOnSecurity, August 2023) reports phishing tailored to victims' balances; that element is secondary and not verified here. On 10 April 2025 the same court [warned of phishing](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/news/notice-phishing-attempt) impersonating the plan administrator in the separate Terraform Labs case. That warning shows that impersonation of claims processes recurs, not that this breach caused it.

DAC8 relevance: this is the clearest adjacent case of a leak of identity and address combined with crypto holdings or balances, rather than customer status alone. It came from an outsourced professional intermediary with access to regulated data, through a single weak authentication factor. It supports the target-selection and fraud stages. No physical attack is attributed to it in the sources.
