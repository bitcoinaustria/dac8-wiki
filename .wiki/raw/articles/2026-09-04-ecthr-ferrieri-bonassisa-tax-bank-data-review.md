# ECtHR Ferrieri and Bonassisa v Italy on tax access to banking data

In *Ferrieri and Bonassisa v Italy*, applications 40607/19 and 34583/20, judgment of 8 January 2026, final on 11 May 2026, the Chamber found an Article 8 violation in targeted tax-authority access to bank accounts, transaction histories and related financial operations.

The Court accepted that tax authorities may legitimately access and disclose bank details in formal procedures. Article 8 does not provide a general right to prior notice, and tax efficiency or investigative effectiveness can justify the absence of ex-ante judicial review. Sufficient safeguards against arbitrariness and effective review remain required (paragraphs 72 to 87).

The Italian routes failed because the authorisation was not independently challengeable, a later tax case was uncertain and potentially years away, the Government did not establish an effective civil remedy in practice, and the taxpayer guarantor lacked binding powers. The law gave unfettered discretion over the conditions and scope of access and lacked effective ex-post independent or judicial review (paragraphs 88 to 106).

Under Article 46, the Court required rules defining the circumstances and conditions for access, reasons tied to those criteria, and effective independent or judicial review whose availability does not depend on a later tax assessment or completion of tax proceedings. It expressly directed attention to international tax cooperation (paragraphs 113 to 117).

The transfer to DAC8 is limited. Ferrieri concerned discretionary, targeted access under insufficiently bounded Italian provisions, not annual automatic reporting with express fields, purposes and a GDPR supervisory route. Austria can rely on the DSB and BVwG as independent and binding review. The judgment nevertheless weakens reliance on a merely possible later tax assessment and makes the practical timing, jurisdiction and corrective powers of the identified review route central. The decision was five votes to two; the dissent would have required stronger exhaustion of a possible civil route.
