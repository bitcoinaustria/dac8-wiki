# Conseil d'État, order n° 519158 of 14 September 2026

## Identity

- Court: Conseil d'État, juge des référés (signed Christine Maugüé). ECLI:FR:CEORD:2026:519158.20260914. Not published in the Recueil Lebon.
- Applicants: société Paymium (single representative), société Leonod and société Satoshi Portal Inc. The order does not name Bull Bitcoin. Bull Bitcoin's campaign site treats this as its own application ([dac8.com response page](https://dac8.com/en/resources/dac8-suspension-refused/), updated 16 September 2026). The link between Satoshi Portal Inc. and Bull Bitcoin rests on that party source and was not checked in a corporate register.
- Request registered 26 August 2026. It asked for suspension of the execution of Decree No. 2025-1276 of 19 December 2025 (reporting and due-diligence obligations of crypto-asset service providers under Article 1649 AC sexies CGI) under Article L. 521-1 CJA, plus EUR 5,000 costs.

## Grounds pleaded

The order records these grounds for serious doubt:

- incompetence, because Article 1 of the decree allegedly extends the Article 1649 AC bis I CGI reporting duty to staking and lending service providers without statutory basis, which is a claimed national addition;
- procedural defect for lack of prior CNIL consultation under Article 36(4) GDPR;
- lack of legal basis, because the transposing statute (Article 54 of Loi n° 2025-127 of 14 February 2025) implements precise and unconditional DAC8 provisions said to breach Charter Articles 7, 8 and 52(1) and Article 8 ECHR. The applicants stated that a preliminary reference was necessary.

For urgency they relied on:

- the alleged breach of EU law;
- privacy interference from collection, verification, retention and disclosure;
- increased breach risk from storage "au sein d'un registre centralisant l'ensemble des données de transactions de tous les résidents d'Etats membres de l'Union".

## Holding (paras 4–5 and dispositif)

- Weighed against "l'intérêt public qui s'attache à la prévention du risque de fraude et d'évasion fiscales", the privacy interference does not create urgency.
- The applicants did not establish how centralised storage would increase the risk of compromise. The judge added that "la simple éventualité d'un risque, dont la probabilité de réalisation est très faible, ne saurait être constitutive d'une situation d'urgence".
- An alleged breach of EU law does not, on its own, create urgency.
- Because urgency was not met, the request was rejected "sans qu'il soit besoin de se prononcer sur l'existence d'un moyen propre à créer un doute sérieux", using the Article L. 522-3 procedure (no instruction, no hearing). Article 1: request rejected.

## Limits and research use

- The order is a jurisdictional fact: official, dated and numbered. It confirms that a merits annulment request is pending ("sans attendre le jugement de la requête au fond"), although it does not give that request's number.
- It decides nothing on the legality of the decree or the validity of DAC8. It does not refer a question to the CJEU and does not indicate that a reference will be made.
- It is adverse authority for any interim-relief strategy based on generic breach risk or on alleged EU-law invalidity alone. The French urgency test is not the VfGH Individualantrag standard. The transferable lesson concerns evidence: probability-grounded, claimant-specific harm is needed.
- It updates the French lawsuit status line in `wiki/references/dac8-com-argument-audit.md`. That line said no official docket, order, suspension or referral had been located. It is also relevant as the first court decision in the Member-State challenge playbook.
