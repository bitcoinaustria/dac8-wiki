# BMI parliamentary answers 1523/AB XXI. GP (2001) and 2056/AB-BR/2004 on EKIS logging, protocol review and random checks

[1523/AB XXI. GP](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXI/AB/1523/fnameorig_597005.html), received 19 January 2001, is the Interior Minister's answer to 1507/J of 21 November 2000 on data misuse in the interior ministry; it cross-refers to other 2000 inquiries on EKIS (for example 1349/J). It states that on 17 December 2000, 29,537 staff of the interior ministry, police and gendarmerie had EKIS access (31,216 including other authorities), and that 51.1 million EKIS accesses were registered in 1999. "Die Protokollierung von (EKIS-)Abfragen erfolgt automationsunterstützt" (EKIS queries are logged automatically); logs are kept for three years under § 14(5) DSG 2000. Each query requires an entry in the BEZUG field giving the legal basis or requester, so log review can test whether a query related to a specific case.

Review occurs "entweder anlassfallbezogen oder im Rahmen einer Routineüberprüfung" (on specific occasion or as routine review), by user, office, queried person or licence plate. The minister gave no figures: "Da keine Statistiken über die durchgeführten Protokollauswertungen geführt werden ..." (since no statistics on log evaluations are kept), and he declined detail on pending criminal cases. A new Erlass required purpose documentation, strict compliance, supervision, more routine checks ("Die Anzahl der routinemäßigen Überprüfungen der EKIS-Anfragen wurde erhöht") and a shorter time-out.

[2056/AB-BR/2004](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/BR/AB-BR/2056/fnameorig_027702.html), 30 September 2004, answered 2238/J-BR/2004. It states that online access to central BMI records uses individually assigned password-protected user IDs. Following Datenschutzkommission recommendations of 23 March 2001 (GZ K210.372/002-DSK/01), "wurde der Einsatz eines Zufallsgenerators im Rahmen der Überprüfung der Zulässigkeit von Anfragen ... angeordnet" (a random generator was ordered for checking query legality), together with an online protocol for users and heads of office.

Evidence class: official ministerial statements to parliament. These are the institution's own account and are two decades old; they do not describe current (2026) BMI practice. They establish that Austrian police-database controls since the early 2000s rely on attributable logins, purpose fields, retained logs and after-the-fact random or occasion-based review, not prior approval of each query. They also show that the ministry did not track review statistics. They concern BMI police systems, not BMF tax systems or DAC8 data.
