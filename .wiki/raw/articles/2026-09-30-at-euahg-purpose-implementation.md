# EU-AHG §15: EU sanctions permission and Austrian implementation boundary

## Controlling EU provision

[Directive (EU) 2023/2226](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2226/oj/eng), Article 1(7)(a), replaces the first subparagraph of Article 16(1) of Directive 2011/16/EU. All information exchanged under DAC may be used for the specified tax purposes, VAT, other indirect taxes, customs duties and AML/CFT. Official secrecy and the protection provided for comparable information under recipient national law remain express requirements.

Article 1(7)(b) adds a subparagraph to Article 16(2): received information/documents may be used without the ordinary sender permission for purposes covered by an act based on Article 215 TFEU and shared for that purpose with the competent national restrictive-measures authority. This is not unrestricted use for every sanctions regime or by every authority. General application is from 1 January 2026.

The adopted text was checked in its official indexed text and the repository's full legal document, `legal-docs/core/eu-dac8-directive-2023-2226.pdf`, printed page 14/38. The European rule applies to all DAC categories, not only crypto-asset information.

## Austrian statutory text

[EU-AHG §15, NOR40273392](https://ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Bundesnormen/NOR40273392/NOR40273392.html), in force from 1 January 2026, contains:

- Paragraph 1: official secrecy and tax/VAT/indirect-tax/customs/AML/CFT uses.
- Paragraph 2: specified recovery, compulsory social-security and tax-penalty uses.
- Paragraph 3: sender consent and domestic-law permission for purposes outside paragraphs 1–2.
- Paragraph 3a: an alternative mechanism for purposes listed by the sending State.

It does not itself expressly reproduce the Article215 sanctions exception.

[BGBl. I 96/2025, authenticated PDF](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/BgblAuth/BGBLA_2025_I_96/BGBLA_2025_I_96.pdf), printed page 26, Article 3 number 5, moves directly from amendment a) to c). The same sequence appears in the Gazette HTML. This documentary observation is not merely an HTML extraction omission; it does not establish why the sequence occurs.

[RV 306 explanatory notes](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXVIII/I/306/fnameorig_1723216.html), discussion of §15 EU-AHG, explains AML/CFT, VAT terminology and the cross-reference adjustment but supplies no explanation of an explicit sanctions exception.

## Other national provisions and unresolved conclusion

[Sanktionengesetz 2024](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/GeltendeFassung.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=20012831), §§12(6) and 14, contains assistance, information-exchange and processing provisions. Whether those or other rules independently authorize the particular use of DAC-origin information, and how they interact with its purpose restrictions, was not conclusively established.

Supported: the European directive expressly waives sender permission for the specified Article215 purposes; §15 EU-AHG does not itself expressly mirror that clause.

Unresolved: the complete Austrian legal authorization and implementation for this specific use. Do not infer unlawful transposition, a blanket prohibition, unrestricted national access or actual sanctions queries from the textual comparison alone. A correctly framed EU-level history statement is not contradicted by this unresolved national-law question.
