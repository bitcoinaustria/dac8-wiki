# Eurojust: AudiA6 takedown

Eurojust press release dated 11 June 2026 on actions of 10 June 2026 in Georgia, in a parallel investigation involving US (EDPA, USSS, IRS-CI), French (Paris cybercrime prosecutors), Polish (Łódź), Georgian and Icelandic authorities, supported by Eurojust and Europol. The service "AudiA6" was suspected of laundering more than EUR 336 million in criminal cryptocurrency between 2022 and 2025, chiefly for ransomware actors, returning "cleaned" funds within about an hour for 3–10 percent commission.

The release states the operation relied on "thousands of fake accounts opened using stolen or purchased identities" and that over 6,000 Know Your Customer records "linked to money mule accounts" were identified; intermediaries were recruited to move proceeds through cryptocurrency exchanges. Results: two alleged administrators arrested in Georgia (a co-perpetrator was arrested in Poland in September 2025), 25 domains and more than 30 servers taken down or seized, over 80 vehicles and multiple properties seized, EUR 692,000 in crypto frozen and over EUR 86,000 seized.

Evidence class: official judicial-cooperation press release describing allegations at the investigative stage, not a judgment. Figures are suspected amounts. The release does not name the exchanges, their jurisdictions or whether they were EU-regulated.

Transfer limits: supports that KYC-verified exchange accounts can be opened at scale in stolen or purchased identities, so the identity on file need not be the person controlling the funds. It is equally evidence that exchange KYC records became investigative material in a successful cross-border action. It does not measure how common such accounts are among regulated EU CASPs.
