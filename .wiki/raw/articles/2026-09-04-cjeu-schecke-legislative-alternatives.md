# CJEU Schecke and Eifert on legislative alternatives and differentiation

In Joined Cases C-92/09 and C-93/09, judgment of 9 November 2010, the Grand Chamber invalidated provisions requiring internet publication of subsidy recipients' names and amounts. The legislature had not adequately considered publication forms compatible with the objective while causing less interference, and the measure did not differentiate by relevant criteria (paragraphs 74 to 89).

The public, searchable and worldwide disclosure of named exact payments is materially more intrusive than DAC8's structured authority exchange. The judgment is useful for testing whether the legislature genuinely examined narrower designs and relevant differentiation; it does not require public field-by-field statistics or make every unexamined alternative decisive.
