# DSB competence and complainants resident abroad

## Doctrine captured

Three decisions form a consistent line.

**DSB 18.03.2019, DSB-D123.955/0002-DSB/2019** (leading case): Romanian complainant resident in Romania, controllers in Moldova. Rejected. "[E]s kann nicht Sinn und Zweck des Art. 77 DSGVO sein, Aufsichtsbehörden als zur Einbringung einer Beschwerde als zuständig zu erklären, zu denen keinerlei objektiver Konnex besteht … Dennoch kann Art. 77 DSGVO nicht so verstanden werden, als ermögliche er eine völlig freie Behördenwahl ('forum shopping')."

**DSB 08.10.2025, 2025-0.794.213**: Germany-resident complainant, Netherlands controller. Rejected. A ".at" domain does not create the Konnex, and neither does filing in German.

**DSB 17.11.2025, 2025-0.917.683**: complainant and respondent both habitually resident in South Africa; the alleged misuse of an Austrian Grundbuch document occurred in South African proceedings. Rejected, because a decision "würde die Kompetenz der Datenschutzbehörde voraussetzen, Verarbeitungsvorgänge in der Republik Südafrika … zu sanktionieren. Eine solche Zuständigkeit besteht gem. Art 55 DSGVO jedoch nicht."

The connecting factor is the processing, not the complainant. Where the controller is established in Austria and the processing occurs there, the "Ort des mutmaßlichen Verstoßes" limb of Art. 77 is satisfied on its face. DSB 21.09.2018, DSB-D130.092/0002-DSB/2018 says so expressly: "Da die Beschwerdegegnerin in Wien niedergelassen zu sein scheint, würde letzteres eine Zuständigkeit der österreichischen Aufsichtsbehörde begründen."

## Positive precedent

**DSB 07.01.2021, 2020-0.816.655**: complainant resident in Poland, Austrian controller. Merits reached, complaint partly upheld, four-week remedial order under Art. 14 Abs. 1 lit. c GDPR. "[D]ass sich die Zuständigkeit aufgrund der Hauptniederlassung der Beschwerdegegnerin … in Österreich ergibt, wobei die gegenständliche Beschwerde unmittelbar bei der Österreichischen Datenschutzbehörde eingebracht wurde und folglich von dieser (ohne Anwendung des One-Stop-Shop-Verfahrens…) zu behandeln war." The Datenschutzbericht 2021 records that the DSB offered the Polish one-stop-shop route and proceeded when the complainant declined.

That is the posture a third-country complainant would face, minus the one-stop-shop alternative, which does not exist for them at all.

## Practical mechanics

No Austrian representation and no lawyer is required. § 10 AVG allows any adult natural person or legal entity as representative. Contrast § 17 Abs. 2 VfGG and § 24 Abs. 2 VwGG, which impose Anwaltspflicht.

§ 10 ZustG is discretionary and non-preclusive: the authority "kann" order a party without a domestic Abgabestelle to name a Zustellungsbevollmächtigter within at least two weeks; failure shifts service to dispatch with deemed delivery two weeks later. It does not bar the complaint. No published DSB decision applying § 10 ZustG was located.

German is required under Art. 8 B-VG and § 13 Abs. 3 AVG, and a foreign-language filing is curable by Mangelbehebungsauftrag. VwGH Ra 2016/01/0288 RS 3 holds that a foreign-language supplement does not justify rejection where a German core exists. DSB-D130.092 failed only because the complainant expressly refused to comply.

## Statistics and their limits

The Tätigkeitsbericht 2025 records 326 incoming cross-border third-country items and 105 Bescheide for 2025, against 89 and 9 for 2024. The row classifies by the controller sitting outside the EEA, not by complainant residence. No report in the series breaks any statistic down by complainant residence or nationality.

The corollary matters: the DSB issued 105 substantive decisions in third-country cross-border matters in 2025, essentially none of which reach RIS. RIS carries only selected DSB decisions, about 360 since 25 May 2018. Absence of published authority is not absence of practice.

## Research use

This closes the open question from the first gap round in a way that cuts against the thesis and for the route. The DSB path is open to a complainant resident outside the Union where the Austrian provider is the controller, which keeps the G 178/2024 subsidiarity objection alive against a direct Article 140 application, and simultaneously confirms that the Track B route is available to the non-reportable third-country claimant.

The German-language requirement appeared in every Tätigkeitsbericht from 2019 to 2023 and is absent from the 2024 and 2025 reports, while remaining on the DSB website. The DSB's English-language site sets out the § 24 DSG requirements and deadlines but does not mention that German is mandatory.
