# CJEU Schrems I on practical protection and independent review

In C-362/14, judgment of 6 October 2015, the Grand Chamber held that protection for transferred personal data must be effective in practice and that supervisory authorities must be able to examine a person's complaint independently, while only the Court of Justice can declare the EU act invalid (paragraphs 74 to 78 and 81 to 95).

The case concerned third-country transfer and intelligence access, not intra-EU tax cooperation. Its narrow relevance to DAC8 is methodological: legal safeguards cannot be evaluated only on paper, current practice and later evidence matter, supervisory review must remain real, and the national authority cannot itself invalidate DAC8.
