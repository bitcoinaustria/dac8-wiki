# Austrian DSG and GDPR corrective-order asymmetry

Section 24(5) DSG expressly directs the DSB, where a complaint against a private-sector controller is upheld, to order restoration of the lawful state where that cannot be achieved by a declaratory finding alone. That sentence does not itself contain an equivalent catalogue for public authorities.

The absence of the private-sector sentence does not mean the DSB lacks all corrective powers against a public controller. GDPR Article 58(2) applies to supervisory authorities and includes warnings, reprimands, compliance orders, correction, erasure, restriction, recipient notification under Article 19, temporary or definitive processing limits and bans. The exact Austrian basis and permissible order must be matched to the identified controller, infringement and statutory duty.

Section 30(5) DSG excludes administrative fines against public authorities and public bodies. That narrows sanctions; it does not displace declaratory or corrective powers. A request for a fine is also different from a request for operative protection.

If the controller satisfies the complainant's individual claim during the procedure, section 24(6) can lead to informal termination after hearing the complainant. The file should therefore preserve any structural issue and the precise remaining relief rather than equate an access response with resolution of every challenged processing step.
