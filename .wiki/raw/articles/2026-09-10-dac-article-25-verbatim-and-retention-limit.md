# Article 25 Directive 2011/16/EU, consolidated to 1 January 2026

## Text captured

**Paragraph 1.** All exchange is subject to the GDPR; Member States restrict Articles 13, 14(1) and 15 GDPR to the extent required to safeguard the interests in Article 23(1)(e) GDPR.

**Paragraph 2.** Regulation 2018/1725 applies to Union institutions, with parallel restrictions.

**Paragraph 3** (as amended by DAC8). Reporting Financial Institutions, intermediaries, Reporting Platform Operators, Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Providers and the competent authorities "shall be considered to be controllers, acting alone or jointly"; the Commission is a processor.

**Paragraph 4.** Each Member State shall ensure that each reporting entity under its jurisdiction "(a) informs each individual concerned that information relating to that individual will be collected and transferred in accordance with this Directive; and (b) provides to each individual concerned all information that the individual is entitled to from the data controller in sufficient time for that individual to exercise his or her data protection rights and, in any case, before the information is reported."

**Paragraph 5.** "Information processed in accordance with this Directive shall be retained for no longer than is necessary to achieve the purposes of this Directive, and in any case in accordance with each data controller's domestic rules on statute of limitations."

**Paragraph 6.** Data-breach reporting, suspension of exchange and suspension of CCN access.

## Research use

Two independent findings.

First, the mis-citation is confirmed at first hand. Article 25 restricts data-subject rights, allocates controllership, imposes transparency duties, caps retention and handles breaches. Nothing in it obliges or permits the collection or processing of data concerning persons the directive does not reach. The Austrian materials' assignment of § 20 Krypto-MPfG to "der Umsetzung von Art. 25 der Richtlinie" therefore cannot support Abs. 1. It fits Abs. 2 and Abs. 3, which are the transparency duties of Article 25(4)(a) and (b).

Second, and newly, Article 25(5) is an express Union storage-limitation rule covering all information processed under the directive, not only reported information. Two Austrian features sit awkwardly with it. § 20 Abs. 4 Krypto-MPfG fixes ten years for transmitted information and gives no limit at all for the dataset generated under Abs. 1 for a user who is never reported, so that data is retained indefinitely rather than "no longer than is necessary". And the ten-year period itself was chosen, per the Erläuterungen, "aus Vereinfachungsgründen" by analogy to § 209 Abs. 3 BAO, which is a convenience rationale rather than a necessity assessment. Annex VI Section V(B) sets the floor at five years.

The retention argument therefore rests on directly applicable Union law read with Article 5(1)(e) GDPR, which suits a complaint to the Datenschutzbehörde against a private provider better than an argument resting on the directive alone.
