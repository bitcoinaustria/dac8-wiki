# CARF MCAA Section 7 notifications

## Rule captured

Section 7(1) CARF MCAA requires each competent authority to lodge notifications on signature covering, among other items, (a) the domestic laws in place and their effective dates, (b) non-reciprocal status, (e) "specifying safeguards, if any, for the protection of personal data", (f) confirmation of adequate confidentiality and data-safeguard measures, and (g) the list of intended partner jurisdictions.

Section 7(4) provides that the Co-ordinating Body Secretariat "will publish on the OECD website the information provided by Competent Authorities pursuant to subparagraphs 1a), b) and e)". The remaining items are made available to other signatories on written request.

Section 7(2) makes the agreement take effect between two competent authorities on the date the second has notified under paragraph 1 including listing the other under 1(g).

## Fact captured

No such publication exists. A systematic link harvest of the OECD Tax Transparency Resource Centre, the international standards page and the Global Forum publications page returned signatory lists only. Direct probing of the plausible file paths on the oecd.org origin returned HTTP 404 while known-good controls returned 200. The CARF FAQs of December 2025 contain no occurrence of "notification", "Section 7", "activation" or "safeguard".

The absence is uniform across the CRS, CARF, DPI, MDR, GIR and CbC agreements, which indicates a standing practice rather than a CARF-specific omission.

Results of the confidentiality and data-safeguards assessments are likewise unpublished, per jurisdiction and in aggregate. The 2025 Peer Review discloses one instance only, in a footnote recording that Curacao had temporarily suspended exchanges pending review of its arrangements.

## Research use

§ 14 Abs. 1 Z 2 lit. c Krypto-MPfG makes a third country a participating state only where it satisfies § 7 OECD-MCAA, and the Erläuterungen read that as requiring the notifications to be lodged and satisfied, "insbesondere datenschutzrechtliche Aspekte bzw. Vertraulichkeit und Datensicherheit". Neither a taxpayer nor a reporting provider can verify that condition for any given state.

This supports two distinct arguments: a legal-certainty objection to a statutory element whose factual basis is undisclosed, and a Chapter V argument that the safeguards relied on for a third-country transfer cannot be assessed because they are not public.
