# Austrian Krypto-MPfG legislative-materials audit

The explanatory memorandum to Government Bill 306 XXVIII GP describes section 18 as implementing Annex VI II.B and gives specific reasons for some identity, provider-source, asset-name and valuation elements. For section 18(1)(3)(c) to (i), it largely labels the remaining data as further reportable information and does not separately analyse the necessity or alternatives for each transaction group. Recitals 10 to 15 of DAC8 provide the broader EU rationale, including tax-gap closure, provider proximity, matching, analysis and capital-gains calculation.

Section 20(1)'s all-customer and all-transaction processing is repeated in the Austrian materials without a separate privacy or alternatives analysis. Universal status screening is plausibly required by DAC8 due diligence, but Annex VI distinguishes that step from the report for reportable users. Article 25 DAC8 addresses data-protection allocation; it does not itself state the full material scope of section 20(1).

For section 20(4) and section 39, the materials select ten years for simplification and by analogy to the absolute limitation period in section 209(3) BAO. Annex VI V.B directly supplies a five-to-ten-year corridor for the records used to comply with due-diligence and reporting duties, fitting section 39. The materials do not separately explain why that provision also justifies section 20(4)'s provider copy of transmitted information.

The comparison with GMSG is specific. Current GMSG section 5 similarly requires processing for all customers in order to determine reportability, but section 5(3) provides five years for transmitted information and section 7a currently provides seven years for compliance records. The 2018 GMSG materials called the five-year period appropriate for data-protection reasons. Krypto-MPfG chooses ten years for both categories without discussing that difference.

The WFA quantifies provider implementation and recurring costs and identifies expected administration benefits, but it is not a field-by-field privacy, security or less-intrusive-alternatives assessment. The committee report adds no separate granular analysis. DSB and Datenschutzrat were invited; no submission from either was identified in the official responses reviewed. Invitation and absence of an identified response do not establish approval, non-consultation or the absence of non-public assessment.

The material silence is an evidentiary finding, not a holding of invalidity. CJEU and ECtHR case law does not require a published statistical study for every field in every legislative measure. The litigation use is to identify a concrete gap between a field or duration, the tax function, the additional interference and an equally effective narrower alternative.
