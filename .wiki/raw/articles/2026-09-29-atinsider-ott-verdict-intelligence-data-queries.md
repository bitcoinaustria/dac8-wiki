# Landesgericht für Strafsachen Wien, press release on the verdict against Egisto Ott (2026): database queries passed to a foreign intelligence service

The Vienna Regional Criminal Court's press office reports on a jury trial against Egisto Ott, a former officer of the BVT (then the Austrian domestic intelligence agency), and a co-defendant police officer. The trial had 13 hearing days from 22 January 2026 and more than 40 witnesses. The release is undated on the page read; secondary reports place the verdict on 20 May 2026. Ott was convicted of Missbrauch der Amtsgewalt, geheimer Nachrichtendienst zum Nachteil der Republik Österreich (§ 256 StGB), bribery offences, embezzlement, breach of secrecy (§ 310(1) StGB) and a foreign-trade offence. He was sentenced to four years and one month's imprisonment and acquitted on fraud, data damage and a DSG offence. The jury found that, as a former BVT officer, he "ohne dienstliche Veranlassung, daher amtsmissbräuchlich – zahlreiche Abfragen von personenbezogenen Daten aus Datenbanken und über Amtshilfe getätigt und veranlasst" (without official reason, and thus abusively, made and arranged numerous personal-data queries from databases and through administrative-assistance requests). It also found that between 2017 and 2022 he supported a Russian Federation intelligence service and handed it "die unberechtigt erlangten Personendaten" (the unlawfully obtained personal data) and a SINA-S laptop with classified security hardware. EUR 56,000 was declared forfeited. The co-defendant police officer received 15 months, suspended for three years.

"Das Urteil ist nicht rechtskräftig" (the verdict is not final); Ott lodged a nullity complaint and appeal in court. The release notes that jury verdicts carry no detailed reasoning. The presumption of innocence continues to apply pending appeal. The release does not identify the persons queried, the specific databases, or how the queries were detected. Press accounts naming targets were not verified here and should not be attributed to this source.

Evidence class: official court communication about a first-instance, non-final jury verdict. For DAC8, it shows that the Austrian authorised-insider threat model includes paid tasking by a foreign state actor, with exfiltration of queried personal data over several years, not only private curiosity. It concerns security-service and police databases, not the tax administration.
