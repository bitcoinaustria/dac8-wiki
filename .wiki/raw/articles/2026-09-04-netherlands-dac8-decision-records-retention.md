# Netherlands DAC8 implementing decision: staged reportability and seven-year records

The decision of 20 April 2026 has retroactive effect from 1 January 2026.

Article 4 requires the provider to obtain and verify a self-certification from each crypto-asset user. Article 5 provides that the provider treats the user as reportable only after the residence test identifies the user as reportable. Article 8 allows a provider that is also a financial institution to rely on completed Common Reporting Standard due diligence and an existing tax self-certification that satisfies the statutory conditions.

Article 11 requires records of the steps and information used to meet collection, verification and reporting duties. The official explanation says those records include external wallet addresses or equivalent identifiers associated with transfers of reportable crypto-assets that must be reported. This is provider-side supporting evidence; the routine DAC8 report still contains annual aggregates and does not transmit the address itself.

Article 11(2), through Article 52(4) of the General Tax Act, sets a seven-year provider retention period after the reporting period. The official explanation says this permits later tax review while avoiding unnecessary provider retention of reportable-user data.

The Dutch rules demonstrate a shorter provider period and a staged reportability formulation. They do not prove that the Dutch provider's operational systems never process comparable data before the final status determination. The wallet-address record is itself an intrusive counterexample that must not be presented as a uniformly more privacy-protective implementation.
