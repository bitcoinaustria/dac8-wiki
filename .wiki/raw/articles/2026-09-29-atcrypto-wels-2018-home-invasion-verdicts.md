# Wels 2018 home invasion of a crypto broker: trial reports and final verdicts

Source digest built from four media reports, all read on 29 September 2026. No court press release or judgment text was found.

1. **Verdict report (canonical source):** futurezone/APA, 29 March 2019.
2. **Trial-day report:** [OÖN/APA, 28 March 2019](https://www.nachrichten.at/oberoesterreich/mit-baby-in-abstellraum-gesperrt-welser-schildert-brutalen-ueberfall;art4,3115085).
3. **Instigator's verdict:** [OÖN/APA, 17 January 2020](https://www.nachrichten.at/oberoesterreich/home-invasion-bei-welser-krypto-trader-moeglicher-drahtzieher-vor-gericht;art4,3213305).
4. **Initial crime report:** [oe24, 21 June 2018](https://www.oe24.at/oesterreich/chronik/oberoesterreich/krimi-um-ersten-bitcoin-raub/338189835).

This is the "robbers posing as postmen" row in the Lopp list.

**Facts as reported.** On 19 June 2018 the perpetrators rang at a family's flat in Wels posing as parcel deliverers ("gaben sich als Paketzusteller aus"). The victim was a self-employed crypto broker. The perpetrators:

- bound the broker and his partner and confined the couple and their nine-month-old baby;
- threatened the family, partly with a knife;
- demanded "die Zugangscodes zu diversen Online-Börsen" (the access codes to various online exchanges);
- per the indictment, held a smartphone to the broker's face for facial recognition;
- transferred crypto from his exchange accounts while still in the flat, over about 90 minutes.

They also took jewellery, gold coins, electronics and clothing worth about €7,500, and threatened the family against reporting to the police.

The crypto value differs between reports:

- about €210,000 in the indictment (OÖN, 28 March 2019);
- €132,000 at the time's rate in the verdict report ("zum damaligen Kurs – 132.000 Euro");
- €133,000 in the January 2020 report;
- "43 Bitcoins" worth about €217,000 in the first press report.

**Procedural status.** The jury court in Wels convicted five defendants on 28–29 March 2019 of robbery, serious extortion and, in three cases, extortionate kidnapping ("erpresserische Entführung"). The sentences were 13, 11, 10 and 4 years, plus 3 years of which 2 were suspended. On 16 January 2020 the Landesgericht Linz convicted the instigator "rechtskräftig" (with final effect) as instigator of extortion and robbery, but not of extortionate kidnapping. He received 3 years, 2 of them suspended, and joint liability with the others for €131,430 to the victims. The same report states the five executors "wurden bereits rechtskräftig schuldig gesprochen" (had already been convicted with final effect). Evidence class: court outcomes as reported by the APA wire. Not verified against a judgment or RIS.

**Target selection.** The OÖN report of January 2020 gives the background: "dass ein Freund des nun Angeklagten bei Krypto-Investments viel Geld – zwischen 15.000 und 30.000 Euro – verloren und das dem Trader zugeschrieben habe" (a friend of the defendant had lost €15,000–30,000 in crypto investments and blamed the trader). That friend took part in the raid. A week before the attack, the victim's brother-in-law had warned him that someone who felt cheated was looking for him (OÖN, 28 March 2019). The defence said the plan was only to "Schulden einzutreiben" (collect debts). Investigators in 2018 already suspected that the perpetrators knew or had surveilled the trader (oe24). The chain is therefore:

- **Selection:** a prior business relationship or grievance.
- **Locating:** personal knowledge of the victim; the reports do not say how his home address was obtained.
- **Coercion:** a home invasion with threats against the family.
- **Transfer:** forced disclosure of exchange credentials and biometrics, followed by transfers from the exchange accounts on the spot.

**DAC8 relevance.** This is the most fully adjudicated Austrian crypto home invasion found. It shows that coercion works against custodial exchange accounts as well as self-custody: codes and biometrics were extracted in person. It does not support a data-leak or tax-data pathway. No source links the case to any register, provider breach or tax data.
