# Vienna forged-ID account-opening network (2026)

ORF Wien, "Betrüger stahlen Hunderte Identitäten", 11 September 2026. The case facts are corroborated by Polizeiticker Österreich, "Wien – Zwölf Festnahmen nach Betrug mit falschen Identitäten", 11 September 2026 (https://polizeiticker.at/artikel/wien-zwoelf-festnahmen-nach-betrug-mit-falschen-identitaeten-288626), which reproduces the Landespolizeidirektion (LPD) Wien release and images. The original LPD Wien press release was not located on polizei.gv.at.

Reported facts (police statements as relayed by the media, not court findings unless stated):
- Banks filed a wave of reports in November 2024 about account openings with forged Austrian ID cards in card format. The same photo appeared on different identities. By summer 2025, more than 200 such account openings were known.
- ORF reports that identities of more than 200 people were misused.
- Polizeiticker states the account openings were made "persönlich in Bankfilialen" (in person, in bank branches). Investigators used the branches' video footage to find about 35 suspects, about 30 of them identified.
- The accounts were used to feign liquidity when ordering high-priced phones in the victims' names (about EUR 3,000–4,000 per order), partly with consumer credit. Up to EUR 1,000 was withdrawn and transferred abroad. Accounts were also sold on the darknet as "Bankdrops".
- The alleged leader ("INKO1") sold forged Austrian IDs on the German darknet platform Crimenetwork for EUR 600 each and recruited "runners" (often people in financial distress, some homeless or with addiction) for EUR 200–400 per job. He himself opened a crypto account using his real ID.
- Damage was about EUR 750,000 (ORF; an investigator suggested more than EUR 1 million). Polizeiticker says "mehrere hunderttausend Euro".
- 12 arrests; the main suspect was arrested 25 August 2025 and the forger in spring 2026. Polizeiticker reports 12 final convictions; ORF reports 15 convictions. Three main suspects are in pre-trial detention and have refused to make statements (ORF).

Evidence class: journalistic reports relaying police information. Medium confidence, because the primary LPD release was not read and the two outlets differ on conviction counts and damage. The case complements the A-FIU's remote-identification typology (which involved passports, QES and 1-cent verification). Here, **in-person** branch identification with a physical ID was defeated at scale. That is adverse to any argument that the problem is confined to remote onboarding. It is a single case, not a prevalence estimate, and the crypto account mentioned was opened in the suspect's real name. For DAC8, it supports the point that verified identity data held by financial institutions can belong to uninvolved real persons.
