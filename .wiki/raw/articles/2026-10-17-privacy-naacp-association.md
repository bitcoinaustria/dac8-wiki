# US Supreme Court: NAACP v. Alabama (1958)

30 June 1958, 357 US 449; original United States Reports text. Pages 460–466 link compelled ordinary-member disclosure, association and private pressure. Page 462 recites uncontroverted economic reprisals, employment losses and coercion threats. In these circumstances Alabama's demand could not sustain the contempt sanction; the underlying ouster merits were not decided. Officials' identities and substantial organisational information had already been supplied. The Court distinguished disclosure involving violent intimidation. This is historical US constitutional authority with a concrete record, not binding Austrian law or evidence about modern tax-data recipients. Mechanical credibility 1: primary court authority +1, landmark age exemption.
