# CJEU C-804/25, État belge v Autorité de protection des données

## Procedural facts captured

Belgian DPA Litigation Chamber, Beslissing ten gronde 79/2025 of **24 April 2025**, DOS-2021-00068. The Chamber found breaches of Articles 5(1)(b), 5(1)(c), 46(1) and 46(2)(a), of Articles 14(1) and (2) with 12(1), of Article 35(1), and of Articles 5(2) and 24.

It issued a reprimand and a compliance order, each to be fulfilled "binnen een termijn van één jaar vanaf de kennisgeving van deze beslissing". It expressly declined to prohibit: "Aangezien deze verwerkingen kaderen in een internationale verbintenis van de Belgische staat, spreekt de Geschillenkamer geen verbod uit." The reasoning at §334 refers instead to one year from the date of the decision, so the operative part governs.

The prohibition belongs to the earlier Decision 61/2023, suspended on 28 June 2023 and annulled by the Markets Court on 20 December 2023 (2023/AR/801).

Cour d'appel de Bruxelles / Marktenhof, 19th chamber A, judgment of 26 November 2025, 2025/AR/887, referred thirteen questions and stayed its own proceedings. It did not suspend Decision 79/2025 and did not suspend the transfers.

Lodged at the Court of Justice on 10 December 2025 as **C-804/25**. OJ notice C/2026/2199 of 27 April 2026, CELEX 62025CN0804. Language of the case: French. Other parties: JC and the Accidental Americans Association of Belgium.

## Questions of direct relevance

Q1 to Q4 concern compatibility with Directive 95/46 and whether Article 26(2) safeguards must be explicitly included in the agreement itself.

Q6: "Does the controller bear the burden of proving the conditions set out in Article 96 of the GDPR are met?"

Q7 asks whether Member States must make best efforts to amend, replace or revoke non-compliant treaties, and whether a state that has not done so may still rely on Article 96.

Q10 asks whether the EU-US Data Privacy Framework decision "constitute[s] an adequacy decision within the meaning of Article 45(3) of the GDPR **as regards transfers of the data at issue carried out for tax purposes**".

Q11 lists seven candidate elements of Article 46 "appropriate safeguards" — definitions, purpose limitation, accuracy and minimisation, retention limits, data-subject rights, limits on onward transfer, effective remedies, control mechanisms and accountability — and asks whether they must be explicitly included in the Article 46(2)(a) instrument, and whether that instrument is only the treaty or may include the national transposing instrument.

Q12 asks whether Article 49(1)(d) permits or prohibits such an agreement, and whether the level of reciprocity is relevant.

## Research use

Q10 and Q11 are the two that decide the Chapter V limb of a CARF transfer challenge. Q11 in particular puts the EDPB Guidelines 2/2020 checklist to the Court as a legal test.

The structural advantage of a CARF case over this FATCA case should be preserved in any note: the CARF MCAA dates from November 2023, so the Article 96 questions Q1 to Q7 — the hardest part of the Belgian case — do not arise. The underlying Multilateral Convention is pre-GDPR, so a state may attempt an Article 96 argument at that layer only.
