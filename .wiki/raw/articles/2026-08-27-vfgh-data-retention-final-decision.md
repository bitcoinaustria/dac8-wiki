# VfGH final decision on Austrian data-retention provisions

## Admissibility captured

The VfGH reiterated that the law must itself interfere directly and currently with the applicant's legal sphere and that no reasonable protective route may exist. It found part of the individual application admissible but rejected other applications or challenge parts where the attack perimeter or other procedural requirements failed.

## Merits captured

After the CJEU invalidated the underlying directive, the VfGH assessed the Austrian implementation under domestic data-protection and ECHR guarantees. It treated general, suspicionless retention as a serious interference and repealed interlocking provisions whose safeguards, access limits, purpose connection, or erasure rules were insufficient.

The decision also states that abstract suitability does not require a proven success percentage. A DAC8 challenge therefore needs more than an assertion of poor measured effectiveness; it must address necessity, tailoring, data scope, safeguards, and less intrusive means.

## Research use

This is the closest procedural analogue for the proposed route, but not a merits precedent that automatically governs tax reporting.
