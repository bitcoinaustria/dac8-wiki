# ENFSI Guideline for Evaluative Reporting in Forensic Science, version 3.0

The European Network of Forensic Science Institutes guideline, approved version 3.0 of 8 March 2015, supplies a non-binding methodological benchmark for evaluative expert evidence.

Sections 2.1 to 2.4 require case-specific competing propositions and evaluate the probability of findings under each proposition, ordinarily through a likelihood ratio. Sections 3.1 to 3.6 require the key issue and propositions to be set before examination, pre-assessment where appropriate, documentation of limitations, and advice if the proposed examination is unlikely to discriminate between propositions. Sections 3.8 to 3.12 require valid controlled methods, competent personnel and a case file recording materials, methods, results, conditioning information and conclusions.

Guidance Note 1 identifies balance, logic, robustness and transparency as the reporting requirements. In particular, the expert must not transpose the conditional by reporting the probability of a proposition merely from the probability of findings given that proposition.

Applied to DAC8 linkage, the guideline supports pre-registration, alternative hypotheses, staged data additions, error and sensitivity reporting, explicit abstention and a stop rule where the available annual aggregate cannot discriminate. It is not an Austrian rule of evidence and does not validate any blockchain heuristic by itself.
