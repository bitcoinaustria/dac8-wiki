# OGH 17 Os 10/15x (14 September 2015): tax official's private AIS queries on 27 colleagues

The OGH decided on the nullity appeal against a judgment of the Landesgericht für Strafsachen Graz of 21 January 2015 (GZ 8 Hv 129/14f-12). That court had convicted a Finanzamt official of § 302(1) StGB. Between 22 April 2008 and 30 August 2013, he had accessed "Grunddaten" (basic data) and "Veranlagungsdaten" (assessment data) on 27 named colleagues in the Abgabeninformationssystem der Finanzverwaltung (AIS) "ohne dienstliche Veranlassung" (without official reason). The trial court found he acted "um seine private Neugierde zu befriedigen" (to satisfy private curiosity), knowing that AIS use required an official reason.

The OGH annulled the conviction of its own motion and remitted the case. For queries without disclosure or exploitation, intent must target the data subject's § 1(1) DSG secrecy claim. No such claim exists where data are publicly available, for example through a public register, or already known to the official. Basic data such as date of birth were lawfully available through the internal personnel directory (ESS, § 9 BDG), so querying them was justified "ungeachtet missbräuchlicher Verwendung des AIS" (despite misuse of AIS). For the tax data, general terms such as "Veranlagungsdaten" or "Steuerdaten" were insufficient without concrete findings on what he intended to learn. The retrial outcome was not located.

Evidence class: supreme-court holding with first-instance findings; no final conviction is established by this document. This is Austria's most directly tax-relevant appellate decision found: a tax official used production tax-system access for private curiosity over five years. It is also adverse authority for any claim that every unauthorised tax-data query is automatically a crime. Criminal liability depends on specific findings about which secret data were targeted. It does not show that the misuse was detected by routine log review; the document does not state how it was detected.
