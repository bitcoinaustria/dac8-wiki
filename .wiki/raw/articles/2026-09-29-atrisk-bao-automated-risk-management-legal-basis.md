# BAO provisions on automated risk management and automated processing

**Source and currency.** Current consolidated Bundesabgabenordnung (BGBl. Nr. 194/1961) as read in RIS on 2026-09-29. Sources were the § 114 norm page (canonical URL above) and the full consolidated PDF "BAO, Fassung vom 29.09.2026". Section numbers are recorded as shown in RIS. The data-protection sub-section appears to have been renumbered in 2025: the RIS pages for §§ 48e–48i show "Im RIS seit 28.07.2025". Older literature may therefore cite different numbers.

**Text read:**

- **§ 114(4):** "Abgabenbehörden dürfen personenbezogene und nicht personenbezogene Daten für Zwecke des automationsunterstützten Risikomanagements und der Betrugsbekämpfung verarbeiten, soweit dies nach dem Grundsatz der Verhältnismäßigkeit zur Erfüllung ihrer Aufgaben geeignet, erforderlich und angemessen ist." In English: tax authorities may process personal and non-personal data for automated risk management and fraud-fighting, to the extent that this is suitable, necessary and appropriate for their tasks under the proportionality principle. Under § 323(52) and (53) BAO the provision came from the Deregulierungsgesetz 2017 (BGBl. I Nr. 40/2017). Its current wording is from the Materien-Datenschutz-Anpassungsgesetz 2018 (BGBl. I Nr. 32/2018), in force since 25 May 2018. § 114(1) sets the underlying duty to ensure that all taxpayers are recorded and treated equally, and that the authorities collect, update and exchange information relevant to assessment.
- **§ 48b(1):** general basis for "ganz oder teilweise automatisierte sowie die nichtautomatisierte Verarbeitung" (wholly or partly automated and non-automated processing) where necessary for tax collection or the authority's tasks. **§ 48b(2):** Art. 9 GDPR special-category data may be processed where there is a substantial public interest under Art. 9(2)(g).
- **§ 48e(1)(1)(b):** the Art. 13/14 GDPR information duty does not apply where giving the information would endanger the authority's tasks and the interest in withholding it prevails. One listed reason is that the information "Rückschlüsse auf die Ausgestaltung automationsunterstützter Risikomanagementsysteme zulassen könnte" (could allow inferences about how automated risk-management systems are designed). Under **§ 48f(1)(1)**, the Art. 15 access right is excluded to the same extent.
- **§ 48i:** log data on processing operations actually performed, "insbesondere Änderungen, Abfragen und Übermittlungen" (in particular changes, queries and transmissions), must be kept for at least three years unless the law provides otherwise.
- **§ 96(2):** documents produced by automated data processing need no signature and are deemed approved by the head of the authority. This is the basis for machine-generated tax assessments ("Bescheide").

**Negative finding.** A full-text search of the consolidated BAO PDF found no reference to "Art. 22" GDPR. It also found no provision expressly authorising solely automated decisions with suitable safeguards under Art. 22(2)(b) GDPR. The only "DSGVO" references are in §§ 48b–48h and the § 90a access-portal rule. This is a text search, not a search of all tax laws. Other statutes (e.g. EStG rules on the application-free employee assessment) were not searched.

**Transfer.** § 114(4) is the likely statutory basis for PACC scoring. It is a broad purpose clause with only a general proportionality test. It does not list data sources or model types, and it provides no transparency, error-correction or human-review safeguards specific to scoring. Its specificity is open to challenge under Art. 6(3) GDPR, Art. 52(1) CFR and § 1(2) DSG, and C-634/21 paras 65–68 on national authorising law is relevant. § 48e(1)(1)(b) allows scoring logic to be withheld from data subjects, which tends to show that affected persons cannot learn how they are scored. Adverse point: the State will say that § 114(4) contains an express necessity and proportionality test and that, because officials decide, Art. 22 is not engaged.
