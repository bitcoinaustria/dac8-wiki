# EDPB and EDPS AML/CFT safeguards

The EDPB's 2020 statement and 2021 letter accept AML/CFT as an important public interest but call for precise legal bases, purpose limitation, necessity, risk-based scope, data avoidance, separation, abstraction, access restriction, and defined retention. The 2022 letter warns that AML risk inferences can exclude persons from banking and other services and criticises vague source, watchlist, sensitive-data, and automated-processing rules.

The 2023 letter focuses on AML information-sharing partnerships and demands clear roles, purposes, recipients, data categories, safeguards, and independent supervision. EDPS Opinion 12/2021 supports harmonisation while insisting on defined controller roles, calibrated access, and protection against disproportionate profiling.
