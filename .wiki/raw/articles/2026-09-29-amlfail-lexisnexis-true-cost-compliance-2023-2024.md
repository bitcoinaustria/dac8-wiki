# LexisNexis True Cost of Financial Crime Compliance

LexisNexis Risk Solutions press releases of 26 September 2023 (global) and 6 March 2024 (EMEA), reporting a commissioned Forrester Consulting online survey of 1,181 senior financial-crime-compliance decision-makers in May–June 2023. Global estimate: total cost to financial institutions of USD 206.1 billion; EMEA costs said to exceed US/Canada by 39.8 percent. EMEA estimate: USD 85 billion, from 482 respondents in the Baltic States, France, Germany, Kenya, Poland, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, the Netherlands and the UAE. Reported shares: costs rose for 98 percent of institutions in 2023; 35 percent named escalating regulation and regulatory expectations as the main driver; 72 percent saw higher labour costs and 70 percent higher technology costs, including KYC software; 81 percent were prioritising cost cutting.

Only the press releases were read; the aggregation method (how respondent costs were scaled to national or regional totals) was not examined. "Financial crime compliance" includes sanctions and fraud as well as AML/KYC. Austria is not in the EMEA sample.

Evidence class: commercial, vendor-commissioned survey by a seller of KYC and screening products, with an interest in high cost and technology-spend narratives. The figures are self-reported estimates, not audited costs, and change with sample and method across editions. They are nonetheless the series that Pol (2020) and the Commission's 2021 impact assessment rely on, which shows the absence of an official European cost measure.

Transfer limits: may be cited only as an order-of-magnitude industry estimate with its bias stated. It does not measure Austrian costs, crypto-asset service provider costs, customer-side burdens or the cost of DAC8 reporting, and it cannot be combined with modelled crime or recovery figures into a reliable cost–benefit ratio.
