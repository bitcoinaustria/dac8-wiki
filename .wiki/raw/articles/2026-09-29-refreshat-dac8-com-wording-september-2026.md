# DAC8.com wording as at 29 September 2026

This is party and advocacy material. It records what the campaign now says, not what is legally true.

## Litigation statements (homepage, "Procedure timeline")

- The site states that the summary petition of 24 February 2026 opened the case, that "the fuller brief developing the substantive legal arguments was filed on 22 May 2026", and that an interim-relief application "filed on 26 August 2026 … was refused on 14 September 2026, without any examination of the decree's lawfulness".
- New claim: "The case on the merits is proceeding: the Conseil d'État will rule after a reference to the Court of Justice of the European Union." No official source supports a decision to refer. The 14 September 2026 order (n° 519158) records only that the applicants said a reference was necessary. Treat the statement as the campaign's expectation or request.
- Inconsistency: `/en/resources/legal-action-conseil-etat/` still shows "Updated 3 July 2026" and says the mémoire ampliatif is "still to come". The homepage says it was filed on 22 May 2026. Neither date for the fuller brief is court-verified.
- The response page `/en/resources/dac8-suspension-refused/` (updated 16 September 2026) quotes the order, links to the official text and publishes the applicants' 26 August 2026 application (25 pp.). Its timeline of French breaches (DGFiP FICOBA 18 February 2026; ANTS 15 April 2026; DGFiP intrusions disclosed 12, 13 and 18 August 2026) is party-compiled and needs independent sourcing before reuse.

## Data-content statements that remain inaccurate or need qualification

- Homepage: each CASP reports "values, units, dates, transaction counts and annual aggregates". The DAC8 Annex VI Section II report contains annual per-asset aggregates. It has no transaction dates. "Dates" is still inaccurate.
- `/en/resources/what-is-dac8/` (updated 3 July 2026): "values, dates and transfer information". Same problem.
- Suspension response page: the decree requires reporting of "the complete transaction history of every one of their users". This contradicts the aggregate reporting design, and reporting is limited to Reportable Users.
- Homepage: "every EU crypto-asset service provider (CASP)"; "identified in 27 databases"; "~54 M holders across 27 centralized databases". The "27 databases" framing is still used.
- New framing on the suspension response page: data will be "poured into a single European directory, hosted by the Commission, which will aggregate the transactions of all residents of the Union". This has partial textual support. Article 21(5) of Directive 2011/16/EU, as amended by DAC8, requires the Commission to provide a secure central directory by 31 December 2026 for information communicated under Article 8ad(2) and (3). Each competent authority may access only information on its own residents, and Commission access is limited. The directory records exchanged information on Reportable Users, not "all residents of the Union", and the data are annual aggregates, not transactions. The campaign's statement that "no end-to-end encryption is planned" was not verified.
- The homepage also now says that transfer information includes "aggregate transfers to non-VASP distributed-ledger addresses where applicable". This is closer to Annex VI than a claim that addresses are reported.
