# CJEU C-684/24 and C-685/24 (Across Fiduciaria), judgment of 21 May 2026

Judgment of the Court (First Chamber), 21 May 2026, *Across Fiduciaria SpA and Others*, joined cases C-684/24 and C-685/24, ECLI:EU:C:2026:410. The references came from the Italian Consiglio di Stato and concern Italian fiduciary mandates (*mandato fiduciario*) under Directive 2015/849 as amended by Directive 2018/843. The text was read in the French judgment text reproduced at ipcuria.eu (`https://ipcuria.eu/details?reference=C-684%2F24`), because the language of the case is Italian and curia.europa.eu returned only a script shell to automated retrieval.

Operative part: (1) and (3) examination revealed nothing affecting the validity of Article 31(1), (2) and (10), or of Article 31(4), first subparagraph, point (c) (legitimate-interest public access for trusts and similar arrangements). (2) Member States may treat Italian fiduciary mandates as "other types of legal arrangements". (4) National law may condition access on a direct, concrete and current legal interest. (5) A non-judicial administrative body may decide exemption requests under Article 31(7a), but national law must provide interim judicial protection where an exemption is refused. Reasoning: legitimate-interest access is "in principle" capable of limiting the Charter Articles 7 and 8 interference to what is strictly necessary (para. 106). Restricting access to authorities and obliged entities alone would necessarily obstruct the transparency objective (para. 107). The data can still profile identification data and economic situation (para. 109). Compared with general public access, the legitimate-interest regime is a "considerably less serious" interference (para. 111, citing *Luxembourg Business Registers*, para. 85).

Relevance: the judgment predates the 27 August 2026 verification of `wiki/topics/dac8-and-the-wealth-register-risk.md`, but that article did not record it. It confirms that article's statement that *Luxembourg Business Registers* invalidated unconditional public access, not controlled or conditioned access. It is also adverse authority. The Court treated AML transparency as capable of justifying even serious interferences and upheld conditioned public access. The C-798/24 judgment of 3 September 2026 relies on paragraph 106.
