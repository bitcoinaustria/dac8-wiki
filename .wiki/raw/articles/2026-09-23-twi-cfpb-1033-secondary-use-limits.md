# Required Rulemaking on Personal Financial Data Rights (12 CFR parts 1001 and 1033), Final Rule

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, released 22 October 2024; published in the Federal Register 18 November 2024 (FR Doc. 2024-25079). Status: final agency rule under CFPA section 1033, effective 17 January 2025 per the rule; subsequent CFPB reconsideration and litigation affecting it were not checked in this ingest. The CFPB's prepublication PDF was downloaded and its text inspected; page numbers below are the printed page numbers of that PDF.

In the section-by-section discussion of § 1001.2(b) (pp. 435–436), the CFPB adds "financial data processing" to the definition of financial products or services so that activities involving consumers' "potentially sensitive personal financial information" are subject to the CFPA. Under § 1033.421(a) (summary, pp. 7–8; discussion, pp. 354–357), authorised third parties must limit collection, use and retention of covered data to what is reasonably necessary to provide the requested product, and targeted advertising, cross-selling and data sales are deemed not reasonably necessary to any other product; the CFPB reasons that such uses serve third parties' own benefit and that consumers often do not expect or understand them.

In the section 1022(b) analysis (Part VI, printed pp. 496 and 509), the CFPB notes that account inflows and outflows ("cash-flow" data) are predictive of serious delinquency and can distinguish repayment risk among consumers with similar credit reports, and that such information may reveal a signal lenders treat as higher credit risk, leading to higher prices or denial of credit.

This establishes that a US federal regulator treats transaction-level account data as sensitive and as revealing a person's financial condition (repayment capacity, credit risk), and restricts secondary use accordingly. It does NOT establish anything about aggregated annual totals, government tax reporting, or EU law; the rule governs consumer-authorised access by private third parties and its operative status after 2025 requires separate verification.
