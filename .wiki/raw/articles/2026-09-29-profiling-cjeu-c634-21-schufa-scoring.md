# C-634/21, SCHUFA Holding (Scoring)

Judgment of the Court (First Chamber), 7 December 2023, ECLI:EU:C:2023:957. The official English text was read in full (HTML manifestation from the EU Publications Office Cellar, CELEX 62021CJ0634). This is distinct from the joined cases C-26/22 and C-64/22 (discharge from remaining debts) already in the wiki.

**Holdings.** Para. 43: Article 22(1) has three cumulative conditions: a decision, based solely on automated processing including profiling, with legal or similarly significant effects. Paras. 44–46: "decision" is broad and can cover the result of calculating creditworthiness as a probability value. Para. 47: SCHUFA's activity meets the Article 4(4) definition of profiling. Paras. 48–50: where a bank draws "strongly" on the value (an insufficient value leads "in almost all cases" to refusal), establishing the value is itself a decision. Paras. 58–59 repeat recital 71: profiling evaluates aspects such as "economic situation, health, personal preferences or interests, reliability or behaviour, location or movements", with risks of discriminatory effects. Paras. 61–63: a narrower reading would create a gap in legal protection in three-party settings. Paras. 65–68: a Member State law authorising such decisions under Article 22(2)(b) must provide suitable safeguards and cannot authorise profiling in disregard of Articles 5 and 6. Para. 73 and the operative part state the holding.

**Relevance and limits.** This is binding EU case law defining when inference-based scoring triggers Article 22. It applies to scores that feed decisions about a person. DAC8 itself mandates reporting, not scoring. The judgment becomes relevant if a tax administration or provider derives automated risk scores from DAC8 or provider records that strongly determine audits or other measures affecting the person. Whether any Austrian tax risk-analysis system uses DAC8 data this way has not been verified. Evidence would be needed before the argument is made. Article 22(2)(b) permits authorisation by Union or Member State law with safeguards, so a state defence is available.
