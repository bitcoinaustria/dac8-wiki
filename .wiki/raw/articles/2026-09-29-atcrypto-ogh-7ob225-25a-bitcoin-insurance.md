# OGH 7 Ob 225/25a: Bitcoin loss not covered as "simple theft"

Source digest. The source is the OGH's official decision summary, "„Diebstahl" von Bitcoins?", for 7 Ob 225/25a, decided 25 March 2026. The full text is in RIS as JJT_20260325_OGH0002_0070OB00225_25A0000_000 (linked from the OGH page); only the summary was read, on 29 September 2026.

**Facts.** The claimant kept his bitcoins on a physical hardware wallet. The OGH notes that the recovery phrase allows access to the whole balance even without the device. In March 2024, malware on his PC prompted him to enter the recovery phrase; he did, lost access, and the perpetrators moved the bitcoins to third-party addresses. He claimed under his household insurance, arguing burglary theft ("Einbruchdiebstahl").

**Holding.** The lower courts dismissed the claim and the OGH confirmed. Under Art 20.4.1.4 ABHP 2011, "simple theft" requires a "Sachwegnahme unter Bruch der tatsächlichen Sachherrschaft" (taking of an item in breach of actual physical control). "Dem Kläger wurde hier kein physischer Gegenstand entwendet" (no physical object was taken from the claimant). The malware was used to obtain credentials and the recovery phrase. Access to the balance "auf der Blockchain" (on the blockchain) happened in a further step by the perpetrator. "Darin liegt jedoch keine Sachwegnahme … und somit kein „einfacher Diebstahl"" (this is no taking of an item and therefore no simple theft).

**Evidence class and limits.** This is a final supreme-court holding on contract interpretation of specific standard insurance conditions (ABHP 2011). It is not a criminal-law ruling on whether crypto is a "Sache" under §§ 127 or 142 StGB, and other policy wordings or cyber covers may differ. No Austrian criminal-law holding on that question was checked on this path.

**DAC8 relevance.** It supports the bearer-asset point that, in Austria, loss of keys through social engineering typically falls on the holder, with no bank-style refund and no default household-insurance cover. It concerns malware and phishing, not physical coercion or data leaks. It says nothing about DAC8 data.
