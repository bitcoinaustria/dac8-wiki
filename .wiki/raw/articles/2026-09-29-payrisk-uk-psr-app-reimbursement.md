# UK APP scams reimbursement requirement

Sources: Payment Systems Regulator, *PS25/5 Consolidated policy statement: APP scams reimbursement requirement* (May 2025), pp. 8–9 (policy summary; paras 3.2–3.4), p. 15 (paras 3.37–3.39) and p. 23 (Q&A 6). PSR, "APP scams reimbursement dashboard for Q1 2026", updated 30 July 2026. The legal instrument is Specific Direction 20; it was not read separately.

**Rules (PS25/5).**

- Sending providers must reimburse APP scam victims within five business days, subject to "stop the clock".
- Receiving providers bear 50% of the cost.
- Exceptions: first-party fraud and gross negligence (the "consumer standard of caution"). Neither the gross-negligence exception nor the excess (maximum GBP 100) may be applied to vulnerable consumers.
- The cap is GBP 85,000 per claim for both Faster Payments and CHAPS. The PSR expected this cap to reimburse in full "99.8% of all APP scams by volume, and 90% by value" (para 3.37).
- Claims may be denied more than 13 months after the last payment. Multi-step fraud is in scope.

**Crypto exclusion (para 3.3).** The requirement does not apply to "payments which take place across other payment systems – for example, if a consumer sends funds to their account at a crypto exchange and then pays a fraudster via a cryptocurrency". Nor does it apply to international payments, payments for unlawful purposes or civil disputes. Under Q&A 6, the payment must be received "in a relevant account in the UK that is not controlled by the consumer".

**Outcomes (dashboard, 7 Oct 2024 – 31 Mar 2026).**

- 88% (GBP 316m) of money lost to in-scope APP scams was reimbursed.
- About 438,300 claims were reported, of which 301,500 were in scope.
- 82% were closed within five business days.
- 3% were rejected for insufficient customer caution.
- Q1 2026: 89% of value reimbursed, GBP 72.6m.

The data are reported by sending firms through Pay.UK and are not validated against receiving firms. The dashboard notes that "public reporting of, and research into, APP fraud is broader than faster payments – e.g. in relation to crypto scams".

**Use.** This is a non-EU comparator and official regulator data. It shows that a legislature or regulator can shift losses from authorised but deceived payments to banks: the UK does so broadly, the EU PSR only for PSP impersonation. The same regime expressly stops at the crypto on-ramp. Once funds reach a crypto account the consumer controls, the UK refund does not follow them. This supports the contrast for self-custody, and qualifies it: bank-side protection against manipulation is a policy choice that varies by jurisdiction. It is not inherent in bank payments. No Austrian or EU equivalent is in force.
