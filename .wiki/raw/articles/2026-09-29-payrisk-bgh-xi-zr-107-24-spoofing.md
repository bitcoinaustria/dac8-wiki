# BGH XI ZR 107/24 (22 July 2025)

Bundesgerichtshof, XI. Zivilsenat, judgment of 22 July 2025, XI ZR 107/24, ECLI:DE:BGH:2025:220725UXIZR107.24.0, on appeal from OLG Naumburg and LG Halle. The text was read from the copy on curia.europa.eu's national-decisions page. This is a German comparator, not Austrian authority. The BGB provisions implement PSD2 Articles 64, 69, 73 and 74.

**Facts (paras 3–8).** A pop-up asked for personal data. Shortly afterwards the customer received a call displaying the Sparkasse's number from a caller who knew her adviser's name and her last three transactions. Over two calls on consecutive days she generated TANs in manual chipTAN mode and read them out. The fraudster used them to raise the limit and send a EUR 35,555 real-time transfer. On the TAN generator she had keyed in a payee IBAN and amount.

**Holdings.**

- Para 21: no error in the finding that the transfer was **not authorised** by the customer. The fraudster initiated and confirmed it; she generated TANs believing she was installing security software.
- Paras 22–23: the bank may nonetheless refuse re-crediting under § 242 BGB to the extent it has a damages claim against the payer (following XI ZR 294/19 and XI ZR 107/22).
- Paras 25–32: gross negligence under § 675v(3) no. 2 and § 675l(1) was correctly found. TANs are personalised security features, and the key breach was passing TANs in the second, pre-arranged call, after nearly a day to reflect. This was no momentary lapse (para 30). Inexperience does not exclude gross negligence (para 31). The spoofed caller ID did not exonerate her, given bank warnings and media coverage (para 32).
- Paras 33–34: the damages claim is not excluded under § 675v(4) no. 1 because SCA was required for the transfer itself; SCA at login is irrelevant.
- Paras 36–39: a login-stage SCA lapse can in principle reduce the bank's claim through contributory fault (§ 254 BGB). Here the appeal court's refusal to reduce it was free of legal error (para 37).

**Use.** This is supreme-court authority in Germany, adverse for victims. Spoofing that induces the victim to hand over credentials can leave the payment unauthorised yet fully uncompensated where gross negligence is found. The § 242 set-off technique is what C-70/25 and the OGH stay put in question. The judgment does not address duress. For the bearer-asset comparison, even the "unauthorised" route gives no assured refund in manipulation cases.
