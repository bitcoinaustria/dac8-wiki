# French Government answers on crypto-related violence and register addresses

Source digest. Three Assemblée nationale written questions (17th legislature), fetched as official PDFs:

- **QE 12853** (J. Lechanteux, to the Interior Minister). Question JO 10 February 2026, p. 1149; answer JO 15 September 2026, p. 8475.
- **[QE 11723](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE11723)** (A. Le Meur; answered by the Economy Ministry). Question JO 16 December 2025, p. 10199; answer JO 21 April 2026, p. 3392.
- **[QE 12588](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE12588)** (T. Benoit, to the Justice Ministry). Question JO 3 February 2026, p. 770; answer JO 26 May 2026, p. 4639.

**Question text (party assertion, not government finding).** QE 12853 cites the Ledger co-founder abduction, a May 2025 attempted abduction in Paris and a Toulon case. It states that about sixty attacks were counted in 2025, against about forty the previous year. No source is given for these figures.

**Interior Ministry answer (QE 12853).** The ministry says attacks on crypto entrepreneurs and professionals, "ou de particuliers censés en détenir" (or private individuals believed to hold crypto), have risen sharply. It adds that they are not a new form of organised crime but a variant of long-used French criminal methods. Measures cited:

- the Law of 13 June 2025 on drug trafficking, the PNACO prosecutor's office and a national organised-crime staff;
- the Decree of 22 August 2025 on home-address protection in the company register (RCS);
- arrests under PNACO in 2025 and 2026;
- OCLCO fugitive tracing abroad;
- a gendarmerie cyber unit (UNC), with more than 400 trained investigators, handling "crypto-rapts" and laundering;
- COMCYBER-MI investigative tools;
- awareness campaigns;
- a ten-measure plan presented by the minister in June 2026, built on three axes: understand, inform, neutralise.

**Economy and Justice answers (QE 11723, QE 12588).** Both questions state that company registers publish home addresses and birth data, enabling identity theft, burglary and threats to personal security. The Economy Ministry answers that Decree 2025-840 gave "une réponse rapide à une situation d'urgence susceptible de mettre en danger la sécurité des personnes" (a rapid response to an emergency liable to endanger personal safety), "dans un contexte de risques accrus pour les dirigeants d'entreprises" (amid increased risks for company directors). It concedes that the protection fails where a sole trader's registered seat is the home. A further draft decree is before the Conseil d'État, and broader harmonisation work is planned for 2026. The Justice Ministry confirms that masking takes effect within five days, that listed authorities retain full access, and that sole traders will be addressed in later work.

These are official government positions published in the Journal officiel. They are not judicial findings and not statistics. They establish that the French State treats public availability of home addresses as a security risk warranting urgent regulatory response, in the context of crypto-related abductions. They do not attribute any attack to tax or DAC8 data. The Interior Ministry's description of an established criminal method applied to a new target is limiting evidence for claims that crypto creates a new type of threat. The Economy Ministry's "urgency" framing is a general safety rationale; it is not tied to crypto in the answer text.
