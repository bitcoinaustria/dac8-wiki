# Cybernews: Bitpanda denies an alleged 5.4 million-user breach

Source digest. Paulina Okunytė, "No, Bitpanda has not just suffered a breach of 5.4 million users", Cybernews, 2 July 2025, updated the same day with Bitpanda's comment. Read on 29 September 2026.

**Claim.** In a post on a cybercrime forum on 29 June 2025, a threat actor claimed to have breached Bitpanda's "Tier 1 infrastructure". The seller offered data on over 5.4 million verified users for $15,000, including full names, IP addresses, "wallet info", KYC status and IBANs. The seller called it a "fresh dump", posted a 200-record sample and named affected countries including Austria. Cybernews does not state that it validated the sample.

**Response.** After an internal investigation, Bitpanda said there was no unauthorised access to its systems. Its spokesperson said: "these claims are false … If there were ever any risk to our users' data or assets, we would communicate it immediately and openly." The Cybernews research team said it often sees "invalid, combined data" sold under big names. Such data are "often used for targeted phishing campaigns" to steal crypto and seed phrases.

**Evidence class and relevance.** The source is a trade-media report of an unverified criminal claim plus a company denial. No supervisory or data-protection-authority statement was found. The report does not establish a breach at an Austrian CASP; it is adverse to asserting one. It illustrates that fraudsters market datasets pairing crypto-platform KYC status with identity and bank fields, and that recycled or combined lists feed phishing. It does not link any such data to physical attacks or to tax data. Searches on 29 September 2026 found no confirmed breach at Bitpanda, Coinfinity or an Austrian Bitcoin-ATM operator.
