# BMF Disziplinarkommission, 6 August 2019 (RIS DKT_BMF_20191009_02_084_18_DK_17): AIS queries on family, logfile analysis and BMF query directives

Senate II of the BMF Disziplinarkommission, after an oral hearing on 6 August 2019, found a Finanzamt "Teamexperte" guilty of duty violations under §§ 44, 47 and 56 BDG 1979 and imposed a EUR 15,000 fine. The findings (Spruch points 2 and 3) cover AIS queries "ohne dienstliche Veranlassung" (without official reason) between 10 January 2011 and 5 February 2018 on his own tax number and relatives. The reasoning counts 319 such accesses up to 6 June 2017. On two days in November 2017 he made 129 and 75 queries without assignment. Point 1 concerned unauthorised tax advice to a third party. No criminal proceedings are recorded in the passages read.

Detection was event-driven. On 3 July 2017 the employing office asked the BIA for a "Logfileanalyse" (logfile analysis) concerning a particular taxpayer. The BIA list did not include that taxpayer but flagged tax numbers "ohne augenscheinlich erkennbare dienstliche Veranlassung" (without apparent official reason) and many name queries within his family. A supplementary BIA analysis dated 11 February 2019 found he had continued querying relatives after the disciplinary charge of 1 December 2017.

The decision (section V) quotes BMF directives. An Erlass of 20 June 2001 provides that officials see a warning on "die Sensibilität der Daten, auf die Folgen einer missbräuchlichen Verwendung sowie auf die Protokollierung des Zugriffes" (data sensitivity, consequences of misuse and logging of access), with a similar notice in the AIS-DB2 entry screen. An Erlass of 16 November 2004 (BMF-320700/0001-I/20/2004, "Anlassbezogene Logfile-Auswertungen") says the BIA "obliegt ... die Überwachung der Rechtmäßigkeit von Zugriffen auf das AIS für das gesamte Ressort" (is responsible for monitoring the lawfulness of AIS access across the ministry). An update was issued on 21 June 2017 (BMF-320700/0004-I/1/2017).

Evidence class: official disciplinary decision; facts found by the authority, disciplinary not criminal. It supports three propositions. BMF logs AIS queries and can reconstruct them per user and day. The BIA monitors legality, and the cited directive title describes "anlassbezogene" (occasion-driven) analysis. A logged-in official who had been warned still queried relatives for years. The directive text quoted does not establish whether proactive or random log review existed. The document does not describe DAC8 systems, current (2026) controls or any disclosure harm.
