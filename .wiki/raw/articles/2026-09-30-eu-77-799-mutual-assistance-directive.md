# Council Directive 77/799/EEC (mutual assistance, direct taxation)

## Source identity

- Council Directive 77/799/EEC of 19 December 1977 concerning mutual assistance by the competent authorities of the Member States in the field of direct taxation
- CELEX `31977L0799`; OJ L 336, 27.12.1977, p. 15–20; legal basis Article 100 EEC Treaty
- Date of document 19 December 1977; entry into force 23 December 1977 (date of notification, per EUR-Lex/Cellar metadata); transposition deadline 1 January 1979 (Article 12(1))
- End of validity 1 January 2013: repealed by Article 28 of Directive 2011/16/EU
- Verified via the Publications Office Cellar text and metadata (EUR-Lex HTML was behind a bot challenge on 2026-09-30)

## Content

The recitals cite cross-border tax evasion and avoidance, budget losses and distortions of capital movements, and the Council resolution of 10 February 1975 on combating international tax evasion and avoidance. They state that bilateral cooperation could not counter increasingly multinational evasion.

Article 1 requires the competent authorities to exchange any information that may enable a correct assessment of taxes on income and on capital. Three exchange modes are set out: on request in a particular case (Article 2, with a requirement that the requesting State first exhaust its usual sources), automatic exchange "for categories of cases" to be determined under the Article 9 consultation procedure (Article 3), and spontaneous exchange in listed situations such as suspected loss of tax or artificial transfers of profits within groups (Article 4). Article 7 imposes secrecy and limits use to taxation and related proceedings. Article 8 lets a Member State refuse where its own law or practice would bar the enquiry, where a commercial, industrial or professional secret or public policy is at stake, or where reciprocity is lacking.

Only tax authorities exchange information; the directive places no reporting duty on private parties. Automatic exchange existed only as an empty frame dependent on later agreement between authorities.

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

The directive is the starting point of the DAC lineage and shows that EU tax cooperation began as authority-to-authority assistance with reciprocity and domestic-law limits, not as mandatory third-party reporting.
