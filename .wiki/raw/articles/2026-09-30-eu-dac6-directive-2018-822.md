# Council Directive (EU) 2018/822 (DAC6, reportable cross-border arrangements)

## Source identity

- Council Directive (EU) 2018/822 of 25 May 2018 amending Directive 2011/16/EU as regards mandatory automatic exchange of information in the field of taxation in relation to reportable cross-border arrangements; CELEX `32018L0822`; OJ L 139, 5.6.2018, p. 1; corrigendum OJ L 31, 1.2.2019, p. 108
- Entry into force 25 June 2018 (20th day after publication, Article 3)
- Transposition by 31 December 2019; application from 1 July 2020 (Article 2(1))
- Commission proposal COM(2017) 335 of 21 June 2017 (see [2026-09-30-eu-dac6-proposal-com-2017-335.md](2026-09-30-eu-dac6-proposal-com-2017-335.md))
- Optional COVID-19 deferral of filing and exchange deadlines by Directive (EU) 2020/876 (see [2026-09-30-eu-dac6-covid-deferral-2020-876.md](2026-09-30-eu-dac6-covid-deferral-2020-876.md))

## Content

The recitals refer to calls for mandatory disclosure of potentially aggressive tax-planning arrangements along the lines of OECD BEPS Action 12, and place the obligation on all actors usually involved in designing, marketing, organising or managing such arrangements.

New Article 8ab requires intermediaries to file information within their knowledge, possession or control on reportable cross-border arrangements within 30 days after the arrangement is made available or ready for implementation or the first step is taken; service-provider intermediaries report within 30 days after giving aid or advice. Where there is no intermediary, or the intermediary claims a waiver for legal professional privilege (Article 8ab(5)), the relevant taxpayer reports (Article 8ab(6)–(7)). A cross-border arrangement is reportable if it contains a hallmark in Annex IV; certain hallmarks count only if the "main benefit test" is met (obtaining a tax advantage is the main benefit or one of the main benefits). Arrangements whose first step was implemented between 25 June 2018 and 30 June 2020 had to be reported by 31 August 2020 (Article 8ab(12)). Competent authorities exchange the information through the central directory quarterly, within one month after the quarter; first exchange by 31 October 2020 (Article 8ab(18)).

Reporters are private intermediaries (tax advisers, lawyers, banks and others) and, in the alternative, taxpayers.

## Case law

- C-694/20 Orde van Vlaamse Balies (8 December 2022): Article 8ab(5) invalid under Charter Article 7 insofar as it obliged a privileged lawyer-intermediary to notify other intermediaries who are not its clients. Recorded at [2026-08-27-cjeu-c694-20-lawyer-privilege.md](2026-08-27-cjeu-c694-20-lawyer-privilege.md).
- C-623/22 Belgian Association of Tax Lawyers (29 July 2024, ECLI:EU:C:2024:639): DAC6 upheld against equal treatment, legal certainty, legality in criminal matters (Charter Article 49(1)) and Article 7 challenges; the C-694/20 invalidity is confined to persons practising under the lawyer titles in Article 1(2)(a) of Directive 98/5/EC. Recorded at [2026-08-27-cjeu-c-623-22-dac6.md](2026-08-27-cjeu-c-623-22-dac6.md).

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

DAC6 is the first DAC regime that makes private advisers report about their clients' arrangements on a hallmark basis, and it produced the first CJEU validity rulings on a DAC amendment. Those rulings mostly upheld the scheme, which is adverse authority for broad Charter challenges; the severed privilege component shows that discrete disproportionate elements can fall.
