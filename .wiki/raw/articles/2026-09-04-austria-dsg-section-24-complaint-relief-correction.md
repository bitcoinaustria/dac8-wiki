# Austria DSG section 24 complaint and private-controller relief

Section 24(2)(5) DSG requires the complaint to contain a request that the alleged infringement be established.

Section 24(3) requires the underlying request and any controller response to be attached only where applicable. A prior erasure request is therefore not a universal admissibility requirement. It is necessary where the alleged infringement is the rejection or non-response to that request.

If a successful complaint is attributable to a private-sector controller, section 24(5) requires an order to comply with the complainant's request for access, rectification, erasure, restriction or portability to the extent necessary to eliminate the established infringement. The provision does not state a free-standing general claim to a complainant-selected Article 58(2) measure.

The supervisory authority retains the corrective powers conferred by Article 58(2) GDPR. CJEU C-768/21 requires an appropriate corrective response where necessary but does not create an automatic entitlement to a chosen sanction. CJEU C-46/23 confirms that a supervisory authority may order erasure of unlawfully processed data without a prior Article 17 request.
