# ESAs revised joint warning on crypto-assets

Source digest. Joint warning by EBA, EIOPA and ESMA under Article 9(3) of their founding regulations. It was published with a joint press release on 6 October 2025 (per the [EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eu-supervisory-authorities-warn-consumers-risks-and-limited-protection-certain-crypto-assets-and) and [EIOPA](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eu-supervisory-authorities-warn-consumers-risks-and-limited-protection-certain-crypto-assets-and-2025-10-06_en) listings; the press release was not fetched). PDF ISBN 978-92-9245-982-6, doi:10.2853/6687704. Locators: question 3 ("Are the devices I use ... secure?") and "What are the key risks": "Fraud, scams and hacks" and "Limited protection".

The warning states: "Losing private keys to crypto-assets results in the permanent and irreversible loss of access and ownership." It lists phishing and social engineering as fraud techniques. It adds that MiCA protections "are not as extensive as those applicable to more traditional financial products (e.g. you will not benefit from compensation schemes)". Firms outside the EU or outside MiCA may offer no protection at all.

This is an official supervisory statement, not binding law and not a holding. It confirms from an EU institutional source the bearer-type character of key control: whoever controls the key controls the asset, and loss of the key is not remedied by a scheme. It does not expressly address theft under coercion or say that on-chain transfers cannot be reversed. It also does not address custodial-provider liability; see MiCA Article 75(8) in `2026-09-29-bearer-psd2-mica-loss-allocation.md`.
