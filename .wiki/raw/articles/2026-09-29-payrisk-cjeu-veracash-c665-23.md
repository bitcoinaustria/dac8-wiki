# CJEU C-665/23 Veracash (2025)

Source: CJEU Press Release No 97/25, Luxembourg, 1 August 2025, "Judgment of the Court in Case C-665/23 | Veracash". The release is an "unofficial document for media use, not binding on the Court". The judgment text was not read, so no paragraph numbers are available. The case arose from the French Court of Cassation under Directive 2007/64 (PSD1).

**Facts.** A consumer held a gold-deposit account with Veracash SAS. He said he never received a new payment card, and daily withdrawals were made on it from March to May 2017. He reported them almost two months after the first withdrawal, which was within the 13-month outer limit. The French courts refused a refund for lack of notification "without undue delay".

**Holdings as summarised by the Court's press service.**

1. The duty to notify "without undue delay" is autonomous from the 13-month limit. In principle, a user who delays notification after becoming aware of the transaction loses the refund even within 13 months.
2. Where a payment instrument was lost, stolen or misappropriated, the payer, unless fraudulent, loses the refund "only if he or she delayed in notifying it with intent or gross negligence consisting of a serious breach of a duty of care". The burden of proof lies with the provider.
3. For a series of transactions, the refund is lost only for the transactions whose notification was delayed intentionally or through gross negligence. The payer-liability provision is a derogation to be "interpreted strictly", and a causal link is required.

**Use.** This is an official summary of a binding judgment, recorded at medium confidence because the judgment text was not read. It shows that the refund for **unauthorised** transactions is robust, since only intent or gross negligence defeats it. It says nothing about payments the payer authorised under deception or duress. Its bearer-asset relevance is indirect: the bank-side refund window depends on prompt notification, and self-custody offers no equivalent claim.
