# Germany KStTG: reportable-user records, notice and retention

The enacted KStTG separates the collection of a self-certification from the later treatment of a user as reportable. Section 4 requires a self-certification from a natural-person crypto-asset user. Section 3 provides that the provider treats the user as reportable from the date on which the user is identified as such under the statutory procedures.

Section 11(1)(2) requires the identity and annual transaction aggregates for each reportable user and reportable controlling person. Section 14(1)(2) and (3) likewise frames the relevant transaction-reporting records around each reportable user and reportable controlling person. This drafting differs from Austria's section 20(1) Krypto-MPfG, which expressly requires section 18 information to be determined, collected, stored and processed for all customers, transactions and crypto-assets regardless of whether the customer qualifies as reportable.

The German text proves a different legislative formulation; it does not prove that German providers never create comparable internal intermediate data for non-reportable users.

Section 13 requires the information notice before the first report and sufficiently early for the person to exercise data-protection rights. It does not expressly require delivery of the final concrete report dataset before filing.

Section 14(2) requires provider records to be retained for ten years after the end of the calendar year in which the record was made. Section 16(5) requires the Federal Central Tax Office to retain received information for ten years; receipt of a correction restarts that authority-side period. Germany therefore is not a shorter-retention comparator.
