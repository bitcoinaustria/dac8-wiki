# Watchlist Internet: Bitpanda phishing wave and Coinbase-leak phishing

Source digest. Two items from Watchlist Internet, the ÖIAT-run and publicly co-funded consumer-protection platform, read on 29 September 2026:

1. "Bitpanda-Falle: Warnung vor unautorisiertem Wallet-Transfer ist ein Phishing-Versuch!", 11 March 2026 (canonical URL).
2. ["Datenleck bei Coinbase: Massive Phishing-Welle rollt"](https://www.watchlist-internet.at/news/datenleck-bei-coinbase-phishing), 22 May 2025.

**Bitpanda (2026).** Watchlist writes that a Bitpanda phishing attempt "wäre aktuell die unangefochtene Nummer eins" (would currently be the undisputed number one) among reported scams, and has been reported "seit einem ungewöhnlich langen Zeitraum" (for an unusually long period). The method:

- An SMS reads "Verdacht auf unautorisierten Wallet-Transfer … Bitte kontaktieren Sie … unseren Support: +4312051523" (suspected unauthorised wallet transfer; please contact our support). A more detailed email adds a fake IP, location and "Verifizierungscode".
- The call centre then builds pressure to obtain online-banking logins and two-factor approvals, or to have transfers released.

The operators "versuchen augenscheinlich über die schiere Masse an Scam-Nachrichten zum Erfolg zu kommen" (apparently rely on the sheer mass of messages). Recipients "müssen gar nicht über ein eigenes Bitpanda-Wallet verfügen" (need not have a Bitpanda wallet at all). Watchlist does not attribute the recipient lists to any Bitpanda data leak.

**Coinbase (2025).** After hackers stole and resold Coinbase customer data, Watchlist reported "vermehrt Phishing-Versuche im Namen von Coinbase" (increasing phishing attempts in Coinbase's name) by email and phone. It lists the leaked fields as names, addresses and email addresses, not passwords, and notes calls even to people without a Coinbase account. The corporate facts are covered in `2026-09-04-coinbase-2025-data-theft-8k.md`.

**Evidence class and relevance.** These are consumer-protection warnings based on reports received; they give no incidence or loss figures. Two points are supported:

- Customers of Austria's largest domestic crypto broker are the most frequent brand-impersonation target in Austrian phishing reports.
- A foreign exchange leak produced observable phishing activity reaching Austria.

For target selection, the Bitpanda wave is mass, untargeted phishing. The Coinbase item links a leak to later contact attempts but does not show that Austrians were contacted because of leaked records, as distinct from generic spam. Neither item concerns physical coercion or tax data.
