# Tornado Cash: US sanctions, Van Loon and the Storm prosecution

US primary sources, recorded for status only. They do not bind EU or Austrian authorities.

**Designation (OFAC press release jy0916, 8 August 2022).** Treasury designated Tornado Cash under E.O. 13694, describing it as a mixer that "operates on the Ethereum blockchain" and had been "used to launder more than $7 billion worth of virtual currency since its creation in 2019". The figure is an agency allegation, not an adjudicated finding.

**Van Loon v. Department of the Treasury, No. 23-50669 (5th Cir. 26 Nov. 2024), Willett J. for Jones, Willett and Engelhardt JJ.** Official slip opinion inspected. Holding (pp. 2–3): "Tornado Cash's immutable smart contracts (the lines of privacy-enabling software code) are not the 'property' of a foreign national or entity", so they "cannot be blocked under IEEPA" and "OFAC overstepped its congressionally defined authority"; reversed and remanded for partial summary judgment under the APA. The opinion distinguishes mutable from immutable contracts (p. 5) and applies only to the immutable pool contracts. Its background section is a judicial description, not a holding. It states (p. 4) that blockchain transactions are "visible to anyone", showing sender, recipient and amount. Addresses are "in theory, pseudonymous", but once an address is matched to a person "the blockchain reveals other transactions that belong to the same person" (citing the Bitcoin whitepaper). It records plaintiffs' reasons for using the mixer, including keeping net worth and donations private and a plaintiff whose crypto activity had been linked to his physical address (p. 12).

**Delisting (Treasury press release sb0057, 21 March 2025).** Treasury "exercised our discretion to remove the economic sanctions against Tornado Cash as reflected in Treasury's Monday filing in Van Loon", while reaffirming concern about DPRK laundering.

**United States v. Storm (S.D.N.Y.).** The SDNY release of 6 August 2025, read on the IRS-CI mirror at irs.gov/node/150161 because justice.gov returned no text, states that Storm was convicted after a four-week jury trial of "one count of conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business" (maximum five years). According to secondary reporting (The Block, 26 August 2026), the jury did not reach verdicts on the money-laundering and sanctions conspiracy counts, a Rule 29 acquittal motion argued in April 2026 remains undecided, and a retrial is set for 26 April 2027. These points were **not verified against the docket**.

**Relevance.** This record shows the legal exposure of account-model privacy tools rather than their technical effect. It includes a US appellate court's description of pseudonymity and linkage. The EU counterpart is AMLR Article 79 (from 10 July 2027), already in the wiki. Direction: nuances; the tools exist and are legally contested.
