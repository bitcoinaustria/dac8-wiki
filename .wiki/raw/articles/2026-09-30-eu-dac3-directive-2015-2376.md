# Council Directive (EU) 2015/2376 (DAC3, tax rulings)

## Source identity

- Council Directive (EU) 2015/2376 of 8 December 2015 amending Directive 2011/16/EU as regards mandatory automatic exchange of information in the field of taxation; CELEX `32015L2376`; OJ L 332, 18.12.2015, p. 1; legal basis Article 115 TFEU
- Entry into force on publication, 18 December 2015 (Article 3)
- Transposition by 31 December 2016; application from 1 January 2017 (Article 2(1))
- Commission proposal COM(2015) 135 of 18 March 2015 (see [2026-09-30-eu-dac3-proposal-com-2015-135.md](2026-09-30-eu-dac3-proposal-com-2015-135.md))

## Content

Recitals 1 and 2 cite cross-border tax avoidance, aggressive tax planning and harmful tax competition, and the European Council conclusions of 18 December 2014 welcoming a proposal on automatic exchange of tax rulings. Recital 4 identifies the defect in the existing spontaneous-exchange route: the issuing State decided which other States to inform. The recitals also refer to OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices work under the BEPS Action Plan. Neither the recitals nor the explanatory memorandum name LuxLeaks.

New Article 8a requires the competent authority that issues, amends or renews an advance cross-border ruling or advance pricing arrangement after 31 December 2016 to communicate it to all other Member States and to the Commission, within three months after the end of each half calendar year. Rulings from the five years before 1 January 2017 must also be exchanged before 1 January 2018 (those from 2012–2013 only if still valid on 1 January 2014). An optional exclusion covers pre-1 April 2016 rulings for groups below EUR 40 million turnover (other than mainly financial or investment groups), and rulings concerning only natural persons are excluded. The Commission receives a limited data set and was to set up a central directory by 31 December 2017.

Reporting is authority-to-authority; no private party files under DAC3.

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

DAC3 extends the DAC to authorities' own decisions and introduces the Commission's central directory, but it does not target individuals. It is contextual history, not an analogue for DAC8's private reporting of individual transactions.
