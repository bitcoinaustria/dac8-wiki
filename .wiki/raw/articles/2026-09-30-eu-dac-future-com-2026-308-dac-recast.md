# COM(2026) 308: proposal for a recast of the DAC

## Source identity and status

- Document: Proposal for a Council Directive on administrative cooperation in the field of taxation (recast), COM(2026) 308 final, Brussels, 24 June 2026, with Annexes 1 to 9 and SEC(2026) 186, SWD(2026) 164, 165 and 166.
- Procedure: 2026/0168(CNS), special legislative procedure (consultation). Legal basis Articles 113 and 115 TFEU, so Council adoption needs unanimity; the European Parliament gives an opinion only.
- Transmitted to the Council on 24 June 2026 as ST 11060/26 (annexes ST 11060/26 ADD 1). Council and Commission texts were read in full from the Commission explanatory-memorandum PDF (taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu) and Council register copies (data.consilium.europa.eu); EUR-Lex was not reachable by script on 30 September 2026.
- Status on 30 September 2026: **proposal, not law.** For Council, European Parliament and EDPS steps see `2026-09-30-eu-dac-future-dac-recast-procedural-status.md` and `2026-09-30-eu-dac-future-edps-opinion-20-2026-dac-recast.md`.
- The Commission documents do not use the label "DAC10". Some advisers use it as shorthand for the recast.

## What the proposal does

The explanatory memorandum presents it as part of the simplification agenda: the Commission Work Programme 2026 (21 October 2025) announced an Omnibus on taxation, and the recast "complements" that omnibus (COM(2026) 560, a separate proposal amending the substantive direct-tax directives). It codifies Directive 2011/16/EU and its eight amending directives (DAC2 to DAC9) into one act, renumbers the articles, and makes these main substantive changes:

- **DAC6:** entities within the scope of the Pillar Two Directive are carved out where no benefit lowers group taxation below 15 %. Hallmark category A is deleted. The reporting period runs from the first implementation step. The intermediary deadline goes from 30 to 90 days. Legal professional privilege is aligned with C-694/20 and C-623/22.
- **DAC7:** for sales of goods, the 30-transaction activity threshold is removed and the monetary threshold rises from EUR 2,000 to EUR 3,000.
- **DAC4/DAC9:** one group-level notification for CbCR and central top-up tax information return (TTIR) filing, due on the last day of the fiscal year, on a common template.
- **TIN (new Article 36):** a Commission TIN verification tool, mandatory for tax administrations and optional for reporting entities. Where the TIN is verified, or a government verification service or EUID is used, only the name and the verified identifier need be reported.
- **DAC1:** the life-insurance category is deleted. The remaining six categories become mandatory where the information is "available" in any national government register, a broader concept than today. Beneficial ownership of immovable property is added. Tax authorities get access to AML registers, including the real-estate access point, and to national pension registers (Articles 38 and 39).
- **Data use:** exchange statistics and CbCR data go to national statistical institutes (Article 46). KPI reporting is added (Article 48), and the Commission may publish reports on use (Article 49).
- **National gold-plating (new Article 1(4)):** Member States may not introduce or maintain domestic reporting obligations that substantially duplicate information reported under Chapter II. Recital 4 is broader and warns against disproportionate additional national reporting obligations.

## Crypto-assets (current DAC8) in the recast

A word-level comparison of recast Annex VI with Annex VI of Directive (EU) 2023/2226 found **no substantive change** to the crypto-asset reporting fields or due-diligence rules. The only differences are "or other jurisdiction(s)" wording in the self-certification of residence, updated AML references to Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, and editorial changes. The transaction fields (aggregate gross amount or fair market value, units and number of transactions per crypto-asset and transaction type, including transfers to unhosted addresses under point (ix)) are carried over unchanged into recast Article 12(3). So is the Identification Service identity shortcut. Article 12(5) states that exchange takes place "without delay and not later than" nine months after the calendar year; the first exchange concerns reporting periods from 1 January 2026.

Annex VI, Section V is carried over: the 60-day/two-reminder rule that blocks reportable transactions, and RCASP record-keeping for no less than five and no more than 10 years. The retention provision for information received by Member States keeps the minimum of "not less than five years" (EDPS numbering: Article 40(6)). Penalties remain "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" (recast Article 34, extended to Articles 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15 and 16). There are no harmonised minimum amounts.

## Dates in the proposal (all bracketed, not final)

Transposition is split. Changed DAC6/DAC7 provisions: adopt by [31 December 2027], apply from [1 January 2028]. The larger set, including Article 12(1) and (5), Article 36 and Annex VI Sections III and V: adopt by [31 December 2029], apply from [1 January 2030]. The repeal of Directive 2011/16/EU as amended also takes effect on [1 January 2030]. The legislative financial statement costs implementation at EUR 14.3 million for 2028–2034.

## Relevance and limits

The recast does not remove or narrow DAC8's contested transaction, unit or count fields, the pre-report blocking rule, or the retention ranges. It is therefore not evidence of legislative relief for current DAC8 obligations. Two elements are relevant as design comparators: the verified-TIN minimum identity route and the Article 1(4) bar on duplicative national reporting. The Article 1(4) bar would be relevant to national DAC8 add-ons only if it is adopted and only to the extent they "substantially duplicate" DAC reporting. All content can still change in Council negotiations, and every date above is bracketed.
