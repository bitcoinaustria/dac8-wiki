# ECtHR Ferrieri and Bonassisa: financial-data access requires an effective review route

In *Ferrieri and Bonassisa v Italy*, applications 40607/19 and 34583/20, the ECtHR gave judgment on 8 January 2026; it became final on 11 May 2026.

## Majority holding

Bank-account and transaction information engaged Article 8, although States retain a broad margin for purely financial information. The governing law gave the tax authorities insufficiently bounded discretion. Prior notice or ex-ante judicial control is not invariably required where it could frustrate a tax investigation, but the affected person must then have timely, independent, binding and practically effective ex-post review.

A possible tax-court challenge only after a future assessment, potentially years later, was too uncertain and late. The asserted civil route was theoretical because the Government did not demonstrate consistent analogous practice, and the non-binding taxpayer guarantor was insufficient. The Court found an Article 8 violation and did not separately examine Articles 6 and 13. Under Article 46 it stated that the domestic framework must define access conditions and provide effective independent review that is not contingent on a later tax assessment, including in international-cooperation cases.

Relevant passages: paragraphs 53-58, 69-74, 78-87, 88-103, 105-109 and 113-116.

## Dissent and limit

Two judges considered the domestic-remedies objection decisive and criticized the majority for bypassing national courts and a possible Article 267 TFEU reference. The judgment neither requires a direct constitutional application nor creates an automatic suspensive remedy. For Austria it supports a challenge only if the DSB, BVwG and civil routes are shown to be too late or practically ineffective in the concrete case; it does not establish that result abstractly.
