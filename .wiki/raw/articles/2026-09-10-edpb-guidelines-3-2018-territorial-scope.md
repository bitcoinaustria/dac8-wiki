# EDPB Guidelines 3/2018 on territorial scope, version 2.1

## Rule captured

Section 1(c), page 10: "the text of Article 3(1) does not restrict the application of the GDPR to the processing of personal data of individuals who are in the Union. The EDPB therefore considers that any personal data processing in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or processor in the Union would fall under the scope of the GDPR, regardless of the location or the nationality of the data subject whose personal data are being processed." Recital 14 is cited in support.

Example 4, page 9: a French company operating a car-sharing application exclusively for customers in Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia, with all processing carried out in France. "[E]ven though processing relates to personal data of data subjects who are not in the Union, the provisions of the GDPR will apply."

Page 14 records that Article 8 of the Charter protects everyone, not only EU citizens.

## Research use

This forecloses any argument that a crypto-asset user resident and tax-resident outside the Union falls outside GDPR protection vis-à-vis an Austrian-established provider.

It cuts both ways and should be recorded as such. It secures the substantive rights of a third-country-resident complainant, and by the same token it keeps the DSB and BVwG route open to them, which is what defeats the subsidiarity argument for a direct Article 140 application.
