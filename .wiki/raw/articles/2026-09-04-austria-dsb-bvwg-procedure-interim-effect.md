# Austria: DSB and BVwG investigation, file access and interim effect

Section 22 DSG gives the DSB investigative access and, in subsection 4, power to prohibit processing by a mandate decision under section 57 AVG where there is a substantial immediate danger to confidentiality interests and danger in delay.

Under section 57(2) AVG, an objection to a mandate decision has suspensive effect only where the decision imposes a monetary obligation. An objection against an emergency processing prohibition therefore does not ordinarily suspend it. Under section 57(3), the authority must open the investigation within two weeks or the mandate decision ceases to have effect.

By contrast, section 13(1) VwGVG generally gives a timely complaint against an ordinary administrative decision suspensive effect. Section 13(2) permits the authority to exclude that effect after balancing the affected public interests and interests of other parties where immediate execution is urgently required because of danger in delay. Suspensive effect of a complaint against a refusal does not itself create a processing prohibition.

Sections 37, 39, 45 and 46 AVG provide official investigation, party hearing, free evaluation and broad admissibility of suitable evidence. Section 17 AVG gives parties file access subject to protected interests and procedural limits. Sections 17, 27 and 28 VwGVG apply procedural rules, define the complaint's review frame and generally require the administrative court to decide the merits.
