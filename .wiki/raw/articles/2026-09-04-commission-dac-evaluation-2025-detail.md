# Commission 2025 DAC evaluation: matching, benefits and methodological limits

The Commission staff working document SWD(2025) 365 evaluates the Directive on Administrative Cooperation for 2018 to 2023. It does not evaluate DAC8 outcomes, because DAC8 had not yet entered into application.

## Use and matching

The evaluation identifies usability and correct taxpayer matching as conditions for AEOI effectiveness. For 2023, 18 Member States reported DAC2 matching rates above 80 percent; more than half reported an unmatched rate of 10 percent or less. The aggregate DAC2 unmatched rate fell from 15 percent in the 2021 yearly assessment to 8 percent in 2023. The document also records wide variation and continued resource and data-quality constraints.

## Benefits and costs

Member-State benefit reporting remained incomplete. For DAC2, 18 Member States reported additional tax assessed for 2022, covering 64 percent of the sample and totalling EUR 683 million. The evaluation extrapolates central EU-wide DAC1 and DAC2 benefits of about EUR 1.2 billion annually. It expressly cautions that not all Member States can trace DAC data to outcomes, there are no common fully integrated indicators, and submitted values cannot all be independently and comparatively verified.

The cost-benefit figure distinguishes three scenarios. Annual recurring cost is estimated at about EUR 650 million. The strict data-based scenario estimates EUR 1.18 billion in benefits and a benefit-cost ratio of 1.82. The low-range and comprehensive modelled scenarios estimate ratios of 7.60 and 10.43. The narrative's claim of an eight-to-ten ratio therefore refers to extrapolated/modelled scenarios, not the strict data-based scenario.

Private DAC2 recurring cost is estimated at EUR 550 million, roughly EUR 12 per reported account in 2023. That estimate uses interviews and surveys with 18 entities across six Member States and does not include initial setup cost.

## Litigation relevance and limit

This is strong adverse evidence against a categorical assertion that automatic tax-information exchange has no demonstrated utility. It is not evidence of DAC8-specific effectiveness, the necessity of each DAC8 field, or the effect of Austria's section 20 pre-report processing and ten-year record choices. The defensible issue is marginal and design-specific: which added field, customer class, use or retention period measurably improves the tax task, and whether an equally effective narrower design exists.
