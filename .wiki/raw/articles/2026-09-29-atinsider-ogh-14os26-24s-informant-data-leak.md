# OGH 14 Os 26/24s (8 October 2024): police officer queried and passed on an informant's identity and address

The OGH decided on nullity appeals and appeals against a Landesgericht Feldkirch Schöffengericht judgment of 13 November 2023 (GZ 16 Hv 65/21x-492). The case involved a police officer ("Da*") and two outside co-defendants. Paragraph [2] sets out the first-instance findings. Point A./I./1. covers targeted queries in "der zentralen Datenanwendung des BM.I ohne dienstliche Rechtfertigung oder Notwendigkeit" (the BMI central database without official justification or need) on several persons between May and August 2018. Under A./I./3., on 27 September 2015 he retrieved a police Amtsvermerk (file note) containing an informant's name, date of birth and home address. The note also contained confidential information the informant had given about protection-money extortion and violent clashes between Kurdish and Chechen groups. Under A./II./1., he printed it and "an unbekannte Personen weitergab" (passed it to unknown persons). Further counts covered tipping off gambling-venue operators about police controls and showing them officers' ID photos.

The OGH set aside some counts and remitted one (A./I./2.). It rejected the remaining nullity appeals and re-sentenced the officer on the surviving counts: § 302(1) StGB (A./I./1. and 3.) and § 310(1) StGB (A./II./1., 2., 4.–7.). The sentence was eight months' conditional imprisonment as an additional penalty, taking account of his Landesgericht Feldkirch convictions of 24 June 2020 (22 Hv 43/20d) and 21 September 2021 (21 Hv 65/19f). He was acquitted of the bribery counts (A./III./). On the RIS text read, the surviving convictions on the informant counts are final.

Evidence class: supreme-court decision with final convictions on the counts stated. It is the clearest Austrian judicial finding located where an insider's database access exposed a person to foreseeable physical risk: the identity and address of an informant against violent groups reached unknown third parties. The decision does not find that the informant was threatened or harmed, and says nothing about how the conduct was detected. The same officer faced three separate criminal judgments for misconduct spanning 2014 to 2018. Because the conduct here predates the 2020 and 2021 judgments, this is not reoffending after conviction; it shows prolonged undetected misuse. This is police data, not tax data.
