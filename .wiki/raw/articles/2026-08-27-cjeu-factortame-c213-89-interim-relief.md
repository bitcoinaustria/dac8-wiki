# CJEU Factortame: national courts must be able to grant effective interim relief

In Case C-213/89, *Factortame*, judgment of 19 June 1990, ECLI:EU:C:1990:257, the CJEU addressed a national rule that deprived the referring court of power to grant interim protection while the compatibility of national legislation with EU law was being decided.

The Court held that the full effectiveness of EU law would be impaired if that national rule could prevent the court from granting interim relief. The national court must therefore set aside the rule that forms the sole obstacle to such relief.

Relevant passages: paragraphs 20-23 and the operative part.

The judgment establishes procedural availability, not an automatic entitlement to suspend DAC8-related duties. The claimant still needs an admissible national proceeding and must satisfy the applicable substantive conditions for interim relief.
