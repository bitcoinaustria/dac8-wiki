# Provider practice under CARF and DAC8

## Facts captured

**Kraken CARF FAQs, last updated 14 January 2026.** "Wir melden keine einzelnen Transaktionen. Stattdessen werden Transaktionen für die CARF-Meldung basierend auf den folgenden Prinzipien aggregiert: Kern-Aggregationsregeln. Alle Transaktionen werden aggregiert nach: Krypto-Asset und Transaktionsrichtung." Three groupings are set out — crypto to fiat, crypto to crypto, transfers — with transfer categories including airdrops, staking rewards and transfers to and from other exchanges. Scope language is hedged: the provider "möglicherweise" collects additional information and reports "bestimmte" transactions. Nothing is said about non-reportable users or retention.

**Bitpanda.** No CARF, DAC8 or Krypto-MPfG explainer is published. The help centre carries only per-country guides to finding a tax identification number, stating that the number is "mandatory on your Bitpanda account under the DAC8 regulation in Europe" since January 2026. Dedicated guides exist for Switzerland and the United Kingdom, so residents of non-EU states are inside the collection process. The account-deletion page cites a ten-year retention duty grounded in FM-GwG, BAO and UGB; the Krypto-MPfG is not named. Bitpanda does not onboard US persons, which makes a US-resident complainant unconstructible at that provider.

**Absence.** No privacy policy, help-centre article, data-protection impact assessment, record of processing or engineering document at Bitpanda, Kraken, Coinbase, Bitstamp, Binance, 21bitcoin, Bison, N26 or Revolut states whether a non-reportable customer's transaction data is fully computed and stored, collected raw only, or excluded from CARF processing. No published DPIA referencing CARF or DAC8 was located from any provider or authority. CARF compliance vendors describe transaction data models without stating whether ingestion is filtered by reportability.

## Research use

The aggregation description is useful affirmative evidence that the § 18 dataset is a substantive computation rather than a filing duty, which matters for the weight of the interference under § 20 Abs. 1.

The absence is the operative finding. It establishes that the factual gate cannot be closed from public sources, and that an Article 15 GDPR request by a definitively non-reportable user against an Austrian-nexus provider is the only available route to it. It also supports a distinct transparency point: § 20 Abs. 2 and Abs. 3 Krypto-MPfG require the provider to inform users before the first report and in time to exercise their rights, and the largest Austrian provider publishes nothing that would discharge that duty as regards processing of non-reportable customers.
