# Directive (EU) 2024/1640: account-register mechanisms and BARIS

Article 16 requires national central registers or electronic retrieval systems capable of identifying holders and controllers of payment, bank, securities, and crypto-asset accounts and safe-deposit boxes. The minimum crypto-account data include a unique account identifier and opening and closing dates; the cross-border BARIS dataset is designed to identify the relationship, not to publish balances or all transactions.

The systems are to be interconnected through BARIS by 10 July 2029. FIUs and AMLA receive immediate and unfiltered cross-border access for their specified tasks; supervisory access is timely. Article 16 keeps national additional information outside BARIS, requires security and confidentiality, and ordinarily retains account-holder information for five years after closure, with a specific-case extension of up to five more years where necessary and proportionate for AML/CFT.

The Directive materially strengthens the future interoperability argument, but it does not enact a central public wealth database or automatically merge DAC8 annual values into BARIS.
