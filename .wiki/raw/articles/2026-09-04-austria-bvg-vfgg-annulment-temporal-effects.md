# Austrian B-VG and VfGG annulment and temporal effects

Under B-VG Articles 139 and 140, the Constitutional Court can annul unlawful regulations and unconstitutional statutes within the properly challenged and necessary perimeter. Publication gives the ruling general force, subject to the Court's temporal order.

Article 140(5) permits the Court to set a future expiry date, generally no more than 18 months for a statute. Article 140(7) provides that the annulled law continues to apply to facts realised before expiry, except in the Anlassfall and unless the Court pronounces otherwise. Regulation review has parallel mechanisms under Article 139. A favourable merits ruling can therefore be followed by delayed or limited operational effect.

For an Article 144 complaint, section 87(2) VfGG requires administrative authorities to restore the legal state corresponding to the Court's view using the means available to them. That is a binding consequence for the case but is not itself the same as a provider-facing deletion order or a general statutory annulment.

Section 85 VfGG permits suspensive effect for an Article 144 complaint where no compelling public interest conflicts and the balance favours it. Against a judgment merely refusing requested protection, suspensive effect does not create the omitted prohibition. Interim requests must therefore identify the positive legal effect that can actually be suspended.
