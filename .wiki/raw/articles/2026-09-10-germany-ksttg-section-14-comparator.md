# German KStTG § 14 as comparator

## Rule captured

§ 14 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 to 4 KStTG each open with "für jeden zu meldenden Nutzer und jede zu meldende beherrschende Person" and cover self-certification records, plausibility-check records, change-of-circumstances records, the reported information and the enforcement trail. Only Nr. 1 is user-independent, and it requires a description of the processes applied, not customer data.

§ 14 Abs. 2 sets ten years and adds: "Die Aufzeichnungen nach Absatz 1 Nummer 2 bis 4 sind vom Anbieter nach Ablauf dieser Frist zu löschen."

There is no German provision extending processing to all customers irrespective of reportable status. The German § 20 is "Rechtsweg".

## Research use

Germany transposed the same directive with the same deadline and did not enact the Austrian duty. If Annex VI required universal transaction processing, Germany would be in breach. The comparator therefore converts an assertion that the duty is burdensome into an argument that it is not necessary.

## Scope caveat: Germany does have a CRS-side blanket clause

The comparator holds for the crypto statute only. On the financial-account side Germany inserted § 6 Abs. 1 FKAustG by Art. 37 JStG 2024, in force 6 December 2024, obliging institutions to determine the account holder's tax residence "unabhängig davon, ob es sich bei dem Kontoinhaber oder dem sonstigen Kunden um eine meldepflichtige Person … handelt".

Two differences survive, and the argument should be framed on them rather than on a claim that Germany never legislates this way. The German duty is confined to tax residence and foreign tax identification numbers, whereas Austrian § 5 Abs. 1 GMSG reaches the whole § 3 and § 12 dataset including addresses, telephone numbers, standing orders, powers of attorney and hold-mail arrangements. And the German clause dates from December 2024, against an Austrian power from 2016 and duty from 2019.

## Adverse-authority note

The Bitkom submission on the second KStTG Referentenentwurf of July 2025 polices directive overreach on individual fields, objecting for instance that "Der Geburtsort ist gemäß DAC 8 nicht verpflichtend". It contains no data-protection or minimisation argument about processing non-reportable users, because the German draft never contained such a duty. The silence corroborates the comparator rather than undercutting it, but it should not be presented as German industry endorsement of the Austrian critique.
