# TFR Article 37 review clauses on self-hosted addresses

Text read from the signed PE-CONS 53/1/22 REV 1 version of Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 in the [local corpus](../../../legal-docs/supporting/eu-tfr-pe-cons-53-2022-rev1-full.pdf), Article 37, pp. 88–90. That version still contains date placeholders; the OJ text could not be retrieved automatically.

- **Article 37(1):** within 12 months after entry into force of the AMLR (in force 9 July 2024), the Commission must review the TFR and propose alignment amendments if appropriate.
- **Article 37(2):** "By … [18 months after the date of application of this Regulation]" (application 30 December 2024, which computes to 30 June 2026), the Commission, "after consulting EBA, shall issue a report assessing the risks posed by transfers to or from self-hosted addresses or entities not established in the Union, as well as the need for specific measures to mitigate those risks, and propose, if appropriate, amendments to this Regulation."
- **Article 37(3):** "By … [4 years after the date of entry into force of this Regulation]" (entry into force 29 June 2023, which computes to mid-2027), a report on application and enforcement, if appropriate with a legislative proposal. Point (e) requires analysis of self-hosted transfer trends and "an evaluation of the need, effectiveness and enforceability of additional mitigation measures, such as specific obligations on providers of hardware and software wallets and limitation, control or prohibition of transfers involving self-hosted addresses." Point (c) covers the need to lower or remove de minimis thresholds.

**Status and limits.** On 29 September 2026, neither web search nor the Commission AML page (`finance.ec.europa.eu`, timeline not updated past 2024) found a published Article 37(2) report. Its publication status is unverified, not confirmed absent. Several secondary blogs say Article 37(2) lets the Commission impose restrictions "via delegated act". The text does not support that. Article 37 only provides for reports and, if appropriate, proposals for amendment, which need the ordinary legislative procedure.

Enacted review clauses nevertheless keep limitation, control or prohibition options formally under evaluation, which the adverse-risk analysis should preserve.
