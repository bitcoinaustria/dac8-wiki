# BMI crypto-crime feature and a Vienna 40-BTC rip-deal

Source digest. Two items read on 29 September 2026:

1. Romana Tofan, "Krypto-Kriminalität", *Öffentliche Sicherheit* (the BMI's magazine), issue 7–8/2025 (canonical URL).
2. ["Polizei sucht Rip-Deal-Betrüger"](https://wien.orf.at/stories/3314006/), ORF Wien, 17 July 2025, reporting a Vienna police appeal.

**BMI magazine.** The article covers BK and C4 investigative capability: blockchain analysis training and the ability to seize crypto into authority wallets. It describes three cases:

- **Rip-deal (2023).** The victim lost about 140 ETH (about €250,000) and reported it to a Vienna police station. The coins were traced to a provider in the Seychelles and "konnte die Kryptowährung rechtzeitig eingefroren werden" (could be frozen in time); return to the victim was still in progress.
- **Darknet murder-for-hire.** An offender paid in Bitcoin bought from exchanges, vouchers and Bitcoin ATMs. The transactions were traced and he was sentenced to 15 years for attempted murder as instigator.
- **Law change.** From 1 January 2025 the StPO treats crypto as "Vermögenswerte" (assets), easing seizure, realisation and return to victims.

The article does not mention robbery, home invasion, kidnapping or extortion against crypto holders, or data leaks.

**Rip-deal via crowdfunding (ORF Wien).** Valentin Szaga-Doktor, an investigator in the Rip-Deal-Unit Vienna, is reported as saying that the victim, an Austrian industrialist, had sought investors "über eine Crowdfunding-Plattform" (via a crowdfunding platform) in August 2022 for a real-estate expansion. A 37-year-old Serbian suspect posing as an Israeli-Luxembourg investor then contacted him; the article does not say through which channel. After meetings in Málaga and Paris, the perpetrators, "laut Polizei" (according to police), obtained the wallet access data during one of the meetings. They took 40 bitcoins placed in the wallet as proof of capital, worth about €815,000 in 2022 and around €4 million in July 2025. ORF, citing LPD Wien, describes "Rip-Deals 2.0" as schemes in which clans "die Zugänge zu Krypto-Wallets ihrer Opfer ausspähen" (spy out access to victims' crypto wallets). The police issued a public appeal with a €3,000 reward. The suspect was identified in "Operation MEDE 2" with Europol and the BKs of Austria and Germany. Status: suspect sought; no conviction.

**Evidence class and relevance.** The magazine is an official BMI publication, partly promotional in tone; the ORF piece reports a police appeal. Two points follow:

- **Adverse evidence:** Austrian police can trace, and sometimes freeze, stolen crypto when a regulated or cooperative provider is reached quickly. This qualifies absolute irreversibility claims.
- **Target selection:** public fundraising information about wealth or capital needs can seed in-person fraud that ends in wallet capture. That pathway is self-published business information, not leaked tax or provider data.

Neither source supports a physical-attack incidence claim.
