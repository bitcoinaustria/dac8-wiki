# VfGH G 606/2015 ua: direct standing and adverse tax-compliance merits

The joined judgment of 9 March 2016 concerns electronic cash-register duties.

Paragraphs 54–65 accepted direct standing: technical and administrative preparations with potentially material cost caused current and direct effect before formal applicability, and applicants were not required to breach penalised duties to obtain review.

Paragraphs 77–87 upheld the system under Article 6 StGG. Equal taxation and preventing tax shortfalls were legitimate; electronic recording was suitable; mass tax administration justified broad, technically supported duties; and proportionally higher costs for small businesses did not prove disproportionality.

For a DAC8 CASP, the case supports admissibility and the need for a costed implementation record. It is adverse on the merits unless the applicant isolates a national excess that does not serve the tax-transparency objective or can be replaced by an equally effective, less intrusive design.
