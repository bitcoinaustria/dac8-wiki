# VfGH: mandatory consultation and formal invalidity of regulations

In [VfGH V 531/2020, 17 March 2022](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Vfgh/JFT_20220317_20V00531_00/JFT_20220317_20V00531_00.pdf), the Court applied its settled case law that omission of a legally required consultation in the regulation-making process is a formal defect. The regulation was unlawful where the authority produced neither documents showing the required consultation nor other indications of it in the regulation file.

[VfGH V 119/88, VfSlg 11.920](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Vfgh/JFT_10118797_88V00119_00/JFT_10118797_88V00119_00.pdf) held that consultation of other affected persons did not replace the consultation of the body specified by law. [VfGH V 58/00, VfSlg 16.096](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Vfgh/JFT_09989773_00V00058_00/JFT_09989773_00V00058_00.html) treated the regulation file as the relevant evidence for whether the mandatory procedural step was performed.

These holdings arose under road-traffic consultation duties, not section 21 DSG. They support a transferable formal-law proposition only after three facts are established: the particular regulation triggered the consultation duty, the legally specified body was not consulted on the relevant draft in time, and the omission is shown from the complete regulation file. Absence of a public statement is not proof that consultation did not occur.

[VfGH V 14/2019](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Vfgh/JFT_20190924_19V00014_00/JFT_20190924_19V00014_00.pdf) is adverse calibration: the exact statutory conditions for the consultation duty must be established before a formal defect follows.
