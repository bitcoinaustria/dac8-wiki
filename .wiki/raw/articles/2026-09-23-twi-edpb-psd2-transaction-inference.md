# Guidelines 06/2020 on the interplay of the Second Payment Services Directive and the GDPR

European Data Protection Board, Version 2.0, adopted 15 December 2020 (after public consultation). Status: EDPB guidelines under Article 70 GDPR; interpretive guidance, not binding law. The full PDF (26 pages) was downloaded and its text inspected. Paragraph numbers are not preserved in the text extraction; the section 5.1 statement is cited as paragraph 52 by EDPS Opinion 38/2023 (footnote 41).

Section 5.1 (Special categories of personal data, pp. 18–19; para. 52) states that financial transactions can reveal sensitive information about an individual, and gives examples: donations revealing political opinions or religious beliefs, union fees revealing trade-union membership, medical bills revealing health data, and purchases revealing sex life or sexual orientation. It adds that even single transactions can contain special-category data, and, relying on WP29's profiling guidelines (WP251rev.01), that "through the sum of financial transactions, different kinds of behavioural patterns can be revealed". It concludes that the chances are considerable that a provider processing transaction information also processes special-category data, and that a DPIA will most probably be required.

Section 6.5 (Profiling, p. 25) states that processing by payment service providers may entail profiling under Article 4(4) GDPR, that a data subject's personal financial situation could be evaluated, and that account information services may involve an extensive evaluation of payment account data. Section 1.2 (p. 7) notes, with reference to recital 28 PSD2, that account information services give the user an overall view of their financial situation, and Example 1 describes categorising spending and income (salary, leisure, energy, mortgage). Sections 6.1–6.2 (p. 21) require data minimisation: access limited to what is necessary for the specific payment service requested.

This establishes that the EU's collective data protection authority regards payment-transaction data as capable of revealing a person's financial situation, behavioural patterns and special-category information, and treats evaluating that situation as profiling. It does NOT establish the same for DAC8 annual aggregates: the EDPB's examples depend on counterparty and transaction details (payee identity, purpose), which DAC8 reports (per crypto-asset and category: total EUR amount, units, transaction count) do not contain. Any application to DAC8 must argue separately how much financial-situation inference survives aggregation, and the guidance does not address tax-reporting obligations under Article 6(1)(c) GDPR.
