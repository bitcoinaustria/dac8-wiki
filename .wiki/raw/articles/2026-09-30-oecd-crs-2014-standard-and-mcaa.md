# OECD Common Reporting Standard and CRS MCAA (2014)

## Verified dates

- **12 June 2013:** Commission proposal to extend automatic exchange under the DAC. **19 June 2013:** G8 welcomes the OECD report "A Step Change in Tax Transparency". **6 September 2013:** G20 Leaders commit to automatic exchange as the new global standard. **February 2014:** G20 finance ministers endorse the CRS. **6 May 2014:** OECD Declaration on Automatic Exchange of Information in Tax Matters. Source: CRS second edition, introduction and annexes.
- **15 July 2014:** the OECD Council approves the full Standard (CRS, Model CAA, commentaries). Endorsed by G20 finance ministers in Cairns, September 2014.
- **29 October 2014:** first signing of the Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement on Automatic Exchange of Financial Account Information. The OECD signatory list (status 13 March 2025) shows 47 current entries dated 29 October 2014, among them Austria and Cyprus; the Slovenian Financial Administration and other contemporaneous accounts speak of 51 signatories. The Global Forum's 2014 annual report places its plenary in Berlin in October 2014. Separately, the CRS second edition says the CRS "draws extensively" on the FATCA intergovernmental approach.
- **9 December 2014:** EU transposes the CRS through Directive 2014/107/EU (DAC2), with first exchanges by 30 September 2017 (COM(2025) 695). Austria signed with first exchange September 2018 on the OECD list.

## Relevance and limits

The CRS is the direct template for CARF and DAC8 (see `2026-08-29-oecd-carf-2022.md`). The CRS covers non-resident account holders only, while DAC8 recital 13 extends crypto reporting to domestic users. The exact 51/47 count reflects later changes in how signatories are listed and is not material. Berlin as the MCAA signing venue rests on the Global Forum annual report and contemporaneous reporting, not on the signatory list itself.
