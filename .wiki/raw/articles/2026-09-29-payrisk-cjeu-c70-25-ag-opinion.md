# CJEU C-70/25: Advocate General Rantos Opinion

Source: CJEU Press Release No 31/26, 5 March 2026, "Advocate General's Opinion in Case C-70/25 | [Tukowiecka]" (a fictitious party name). The reference came from the Sąd Rejonowy w Koszalinie (Poland) on 3 February 2025. The full Opinion was not read, because the EUR-Lex copy did not render, so no point numbers are cited.

**Facts.** A customer selling on a platform received a fake bank link from a purported buyer and entered her login details. The fraudster then made an unauthorised payment. She reported it the next day. The bank refused to refund, alleging gross negligence in disclosing credentials.

**Proposed answer (press summary).** Article 73(1) PSD2 requires the bank "as a first step, to refund immediately" an unauthorised transaction. The only exception is good reason to suspect fraud, communicated in writing to the national authority. "No other exception ... has been provided for", and Member States have no discretion. The refund "is not final". If the bank then proves intentional or grossly negligent breach of Article 69 duties, it may require the customer to bear the loss. If the customer refuses, the bank must sue.

**Status and limits.**

- This is an AG Opinion. It is not binding, and the press release states that judgment "will be given at a later date". Searches on 29 September 2026 found no judgment. The infocuria case listing could not be read, so a judgment delivered very recently cannot be ruled out.
- The Opinion concerns **unauthorised** transactions, where credentials were phished and the fraudster initiated the payment. It does not address payments the payer initiated under deception, or physical coercion.
- It conflicts with German practice: the BGH allows the bank to withhold the refund on the basis of its own damages claim under § 242 BGB (see `2026-09-29-payrisk-bgh-xi-zr-107-24-spoofing.md`). It also conflicts with Article 56(1) of the agreed PSR text, which lets a provider defer the refund where it suspects gross negligence (see `2026-09-29-payrisk-psr-final-compromise-impersonation.md`).
- Austria: OGH 5 Ob 54/24p (3 June 2025) stayed a consumer-association injunction case pending C-70/25 (see `2026-09-29-payrisk-ogh-5ob54-24p-refund-setoff.md`).

**Use.** If the Court follows the Opinion, bank-account victims of credential theft get a fast provisional refund, and the bank must litigate any negligence claim. That widens the gap to self-custody for credential theft only.
