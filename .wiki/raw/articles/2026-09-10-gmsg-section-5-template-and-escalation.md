# GMSG § 5 Abs. 1 and the escalation to transaction level

## Rule captured

§ 5 Abs. 1 GMSG in its current form provides: "Zur Durchführung dieses Bundesgesetzes ist jedes meldende Finanzinstitut verpflichtet, die in § 3 bzw. § 12 genannten Informationen für alle Kontoinhaber und sonstigen Kunden hinsichtlich aller bestehenden Konten und aller Neukonten zu ermitteln, zu erfassen, zu speichern und zu verarbeiten, unabhängig davon, ob es sich bei dem Kontoinhaber oder dem sonstigen Kunden um eine meldepflichtige Person im Sinne dieses Gesetzes handelt."

## Correction to the ten-year premise

The clause did not read "verpflichtet" from the start. The Stammfassung, BGBl. I Nr. 116/2015, said **"berechtigt"** — a permission to collect, not a duty. The word was replaced by JStG 2018, BGBl. I Nr. 62/2018, Art. 13 Z 4 lit. a: "In Abs 1 wird das Wort „berechtigt" durch das Wort „verpflichtet" ersetzt", in force 1 January 2019 per § 117 Abs. 3 GMSG. Verified against RIS consolidated versions dated 1 August 2018 and 1 October 2018 ("berechtigt") against 1 January 2019 ("verpflichtet").

The obligation-form template is therefore about seven years and eight months old, not ten, and for its first three and a half years the universal-collection clause was a mere permission.

## The 2018 rationale

ErlRV 190 BlgNR XXVI. GP, zu Art. 13 Z 4: "Durch diese Verpflichtung zur Erfassung von Informationen wird den rezenten Entwicklungen auf Ebene der OECD und dem Globalen Forum … Rechnung getragen. Der stete Zuwachs von Staaten und Jurisdiktionen, die am automatischen Informationsaustausch teilnehmen wollen, macht es erforderlich, zeitnah mit zusätzlichen Staaten Informationen austauschen zu können, um faire und gleiche Wettbewerbsbedingungen zu ermöglichen."

The 2015 rationale had been narrowly instrumental: "Dies ist notwendig um festzustellen, ob es sich beim Kontoinhaber um eine meldepflichtige Person handelt" (ErlRV 685 BlgNR XXV. GP).

The legislature therefore converted a screening permission into a standing collection duty and justified it by anticipated future exchange partners and level-playing-field considerations. That is a stockpiling rationale rather than a necessity rationale, stated in the state's own words.

## Delta to Krypto-MPfG § 20

| | GMSG § 5 | Krypto-MPfG § 20 |
|---|---|---|
| Object of collection | the information named in § 3 and § 12 | "die meldepflichtigen Informationen … hinsichtlich aller Transaktionen und Kryptowerte" |
| Retention | five years (Abs. 3) | ten years (Abs. 4), and no trigger at all for a never-reported user |
| Stated justification | 2015 screening, 2018 stockpiling | none given |

## BMF guidance narrowing the information duty

GMSR, GZ BMF-010221/0820-VI/8/2016 in the version GZ 2020-0.236.027 of 19 April 2020, Rz 5: "Als betroffene Personen iSd § 5 Abs. 2 GMSG kommen entsprechend Art. 25 Abs. 3 der … Richtlinie 2011/16/EU nur meldepflichtige Personen iSd § 89 GMSG in Betracht."

The BMF's own guidance thus treats non-reportable persons as collected but not entitled to be informed. § 5 Abs. 2a GMSG and § 20 Abs. 3 Krypto-MPfG, both new from 1 January 2026, speak of "jeder betroffenen natürlichen Person" without that limitation, which arguably supersedes Rz 5; the BMF has not withdrawn it.

## Research use

Two opposite uses.

Supporting: the clause predates DAC8 by eight years and appears in the CRS statute, where DAC2 equally does not require it. It travels with the Austrian legislator, not with the EU instrument. That reinforces the gold-plating characterisation independently of the DAC8 materials.

Adverse: the clause has stood for ten years. Its longevity will be argued as evidence of lawfulness and against novelty.

The adverse use is weaker than it looks. The duty form dates from 2019, not 2015, so the acquiescence period is shorter, and the amendment that created it passed consultation with no data-protection scrutiny at all.

The further answer is the change in the reference object. GMSG § 5 Abs. 1 operates at account level, "hinsichtlich aller bestehenden Konten und aller Neukonten". Krypto-MPfG § 20 Abs. 1 operates at transaction level, "hinsichtlich aller Transaktionen und Kryptowerte". CRS is balance and account based; CARF is transaction based. Carrying the old template into the new framework multiplies the interference rather than repeating it.
