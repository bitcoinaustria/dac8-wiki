# DAC8 Annex VI Section V

## Rule captured

Section V "Effective Implementation" has six subsections.

**A. Enforcement of collection and verification.** Member States must require providers to enforce the Section III requirements. Where a user does not supply the information after two reminders and not before 60 days have expired, the provider "shall prevent the Crypto-Asset User from performing Reportable Transactions".

**B. Record-keeping.** Member States must require providers "to keep records of the steps undertaken and any information relied upon for the performance of the reporting requirements and due diligence procedures set out in Sections II and III, respectively. Such records shall remain available for a sufficiently long period of time and in any event for a period of not less than five years but not more than 10 years following the end of the period within which the Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider is required to report the information **if the information is reportable pursuant to Section II**." Subsection B(2) covers orders to report.

**C.** Administrative procedures to verify provider compliance.

**D.** Administrative procedures to follow up incomplete or inaccurate reports.

**E.** Communication by the MiCA authorising authority of a list of authorised providers.

**F.** Single registration of a Crypto-Asset Operator, with the information to be communicated on registration.

## Research use

This closes the mapping question at first hand rather than through the Austrian materials. Subsection A corresponds to § 38 Krypto-MPfG and subsection B to § 39 and § 20 Abs. 4. No subsection corresponds to § 20 Abs. 1.

Two features matter for the proportionality analysis. The retained object is the steps and the information relied upon, not a per-customer transaction ledger. And the retention clock is expressly conditioned on the information being reportable, which means the directive does not contemplate a retention regime for users who are never reported.
