# DAC8 impact assessment: evidence limits and reporting options

This record supplements the earlier high-level source record. The assessment is the Commission's ex ante policy analysis. Its assumptions are legislative evidence and State counterargument, not measured DAC8 outcomes.

## Quantitative premise and limits

The assessment states that the crypto tax gap is difficult to quantify precisely. It cites an estimate of EUR 3.6 billion in realised Bitcoin gains in the EU in 2020, applies an assumed uniform 25% tax rate for modelling, and records that there was no information on how much had actually been declared and taxed. Revenue estimates also rely on Chainalysis and Joint Research Centre inputs, exclude some behavioural responses, and acknowledge possible movement toward peer-to-peer activity where no reporting intermediary exists.

## Options considered

- Balance-only reporting was considered too weak for frequently traded crypto-assets and likely to generate additional information requests.
- A more aggregated transaction model was expected to provide a general picture for risk assessment and permit targeted follow-up on higher-risk users.
- Transaction-by-transaction reporting would provide the most detail but impose the highest burden.
- The preferred hybrid or middle-ground model added per-asset acquisitions, disposals and transfers, together with values, units and counts. The Commission expected better risk selection, possible-gain estimation and fewer follow-up requests than under a coarser model.

The assessment describes the preferred dataset as the minimum required on the basis of experience with earlier DAC amendments. That is a legislative conclusion; the document does not report an independent field-ablation test demonstrating the incremental value of every unit, count, transfer-type or external-address aggregate.

## Effectiveness qualification

The assessment recognises that benefits depend on Member States actually using the information and that administrative capacity differs. It proposes later monitoring through controls, additional revenue, compliance changes and adjustment counts. DAC8-specific effectiveness was therefore to be evaluated after operation, not proved by the assessment itself.

The Regulatory Scrutiny Board gave a positive opinion with reservations and sought clearer options, assumptions and success measures. The assessment nevertheless supplies an important State answer: the final model was deliberately chosen as less intrusive than transaction-by-transaction reporting and more useful than balance-only or highly aggregated reporting.
