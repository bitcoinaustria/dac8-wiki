# Austrian media reports of tax officials convicted for private tax-data queries (2015–2026) [secondary pointer]

These are press reports, not court records; use them only as pointers to verify. Each is labelled with what the article itself states.

- **Innsbruck, [tirol.ORF.at, 27 August 2026](https://tirol.orf.at/stories/3368658/).** A former Finanzamt Innsbruck employee, 57 and previously unconvicted, pleaded guilty at the Landesgericht Innsbruck to Missbrauch der Amtsgewalt. She had looked 30 times at the tax data of nine acquaintances without their consent. "Das flog durch eine anonyme Anzeige auf" (it came to light through an anonymous report). Sentence: six months conditional plus an unconditional EUR 5,400 fine. "Das Urteil ist rechtkräftig" (final); she was dismissed.
- **Eisenstadt, [BVZ, 2026](https://m.bvz.at/eisenstadt/amtsmissbrauch-finanzbeamter-fragte-unbefugt-persoenliche-daten-ab-535441021).** A 53-year-old tax official is reported to have searched the "Abgabeninformationssystem des Finanzamts" more than 100 times from 2022 to 2024 for personal data on 11 persons, admitting that most searches were unauthorised. The outcome and exact article date were not captured.
- **Klagenfurt, [kaernten.ORF.at, 19 July 2018](https://kaernten.orf.at/v2/news/stories/2925403/).** A 55-year-old Teamreferent was convicted of Amtsmissbrauch for querying, in 2010 and 2013, tax data on his minor son and ex-partner during a maintenance dispute. Sentence: eight months conditional plus EUR 3,600; not final.
- **Vienna, [vienna.at, 14 March 2016](https://www.vienna.at/illegale-datenabfragen-wiener-finanzbeamter-missbrauchte-sein-amt/4653549).** A 45-year-old tax auditor browsed AIS data on work and former school colleagues from 2009 to 2014: 36 persons queried, 14 counts convicted, one colleague 107 times. Seven months conditional; not final.
- **Feldkirch, [vol.at, 31 July 2015](https://www.vol.at/finanzbeamter-nutzte-datenregister-fuer-private-abfrage/4407822).** A 55-year-old Finanzamt employee queried an internal register to check whether a prospective private car buyer could pay. He was convicted of Amtsmissbrauch and Verletzung des Amtsgeheimnisses and fined EUR 13,200 in total; not final.

Evidence class: secondary media. Except for Innsbruck, final status and appellate outcomes are unverified. None of the articles reports disclosure to criminals or physical harm. Only the Innsbruck report states how the misuse was detected, and it was by an outside tip, which is adverse to assuming that routine log review catches such conduct. It does not prove log review is absent. The pattern supports the claim that curiosity and personal-dispute queries of production tax data by Austrian tax officials recur and are prosecuted under § 302 StGB. None of these reports involves crypto-asset or DAC8 data.
