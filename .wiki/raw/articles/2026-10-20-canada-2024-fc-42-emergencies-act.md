# 2024 FC 42 (Federal Court of Canada)

Reasons dated 23 January 2024, dockets T-306-22, T-316-22, T-347-22 and T-382-22 (Canadian Frontline Nurses, CCLA, CCF, Jost et al. v. Attorney General of Canada); Mosley J. Official Federal Court PDF (190 pages) retrieved 20 October 2026.

## Holdings relevant here

- Para. 334: the court doubted the government's position that frozen accounts were not "seized"; "Alternatively, I am satisfied that the disclosure of information about the bank and credit card accounts of the 'designated persons' by the financial institutions to the RCMP constituted a 'seizure' of that information by the government." Financial records form part of the "biographical core of personal information" (R. v. Plant; Schreiber).
- Paras. 336–337: institutions had to disclose "without delay" on a "reason to believe" without any defined standard; the RCMP applied only "bare belief".
- Para. 340: the Order enlisted financial institutions as government subordinates for this activity.
- Para. 341: "I find that the failure to require that some objective standard be satisfied before the accounts were frozen breached s. 8."
- Paras. 358–359: no standard or process to contest designation; the s. 2(b) and s. 8 infringements were not minimally impairing and "were not justified under section 1".
- Paras. 372–373: the Proclamation was unreasonable. Two applicants (Nagle/CFN and Jost/Ristau) lacked standing.

Adverse within the same judgment: no breach of the right of peaceful assembly (para. 314) or freedom of association (para. 317). Para. 374: the case concerned how the Emergencies Act was applied, not its constitutionality.

## Limits

First-instance Canadian authority under the Charter, partly modified on appeal: [2026 FCA 6](2026-10-20-canada-2026-fca-6-emergencies-act-appeal.md) held that freezing under s. 2(1) of the Order was not a search or seizure and affirmed the s. 8 breach only for the information-sharing provisions (ss. 5–6). Not EU or Austrian law; emergency powers, not tax reporting.
