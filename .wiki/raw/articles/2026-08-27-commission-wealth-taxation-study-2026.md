# European Commission study on wealth taxation, 2026

The study page reports that wealth-tax outcomes depend on design and taxpayer responses and that the assessed taxes have not generally been major revenue sources. It nevertheless highlights effective exchange concerning beneficial owners, real estate and asset registration, third-party reporting, and tax-administration digitalisation.

This is a State and policy counterargument: asset visibility can support equality of taxation and enforcement. The study is analytical material, not a proposal establishing an EU wealth tax or a personal wealth register.
