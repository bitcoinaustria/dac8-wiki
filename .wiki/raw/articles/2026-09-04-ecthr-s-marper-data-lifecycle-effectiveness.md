# ECtHR S and Marper on data lifecycle, future use and effectiveness evidence

In *S and Marper v United Kingdom*, applications 30562/04 and 30566/04, Grand Chamber judgment of 4 December 2008, the Court held that storage itself is an interference and considered the nature, context, use, consequences and future technological uses of the data (paragraphs 67 to 71).

Clear rules on duration, use, third-party access, integrity, confidentiality and destruction were relevant. Indefinite blanket retention of biometric data from persons not convicted was disproportionate. The statistics cited by the Government did not establish the marginal value of retaining the full challenged class indefinitely (paragraphs 99 and 115 to 126).

The police, biometric and indefinite-retention context limits transfer to DAC8. The useful method is to test the added value of the challenged field or duration, rather than demand proof that the reporting regime has some usefulness at all, and to examine the entire data lifecycle.
