# Material scope of adequacy decisions for tax-authority transfers

## Rules captured

**Japan, Implementing Decision (EU) 2019/419.** Article 1(1) covers transfers "to personal information handling business operators in Japan". Recital 10: the acts governing the public sector are "not the object of the adequacy finding contained in this Decision, which is limited to the protection of personal information by 'Personal Information Handling Business Operators'". EDPB Guidelines 2/2020 confirm this at footnote 2. The Japanese tax authority is outside the decision.

**Canada, Decision 2002/2/EC.** Article 1 covers "recipients subject to the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act"; recital 5 excludes organisations to which the federal Privacy Act applies. The Commission's 2024 review restates this as adequacy "for commercial operators".

**Korea, Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/254.** Article 1(1) covers "entities in the Republic of Korea subject to the Personal Information Protection Act". Recital 5 names "public institutions" and recital 19 confirms that any public institution processing personal data is a controller under PIPA. The tax authority is inside the decision. Recital 40 additionally records that public institutions may provide personal data to a foreign government to comply with a treaty obligation.

**United Kingdom, Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1772.** Article 1(1) is general. The single carve-out in Article 1(2) concerned immigration control and was **repealed** by Implementing Decision (EU) 2025/2574 of 19 December 2025, which also extends expiry to 27 December 2031. Tax was never carved out.

**Jersey (2008/393/EC), Guernsey (2003/821/EC), Isle of Man (2004/411/EC), New Zealand (2013/65/EU), Switzerland (2000/518/EC), Argentina, Uruguay, Andorra.** General country findings with no sectoral limitation.

**Israel (2011/61/EU).** Limited to automated transfers, not by sector.

**United States, Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795.** Limited to organisations on the Data Privacy Framework List. The IRS is not and cannot be listed.

## Consequence for claimant selection

Cross-referencing with Austria's eleven activated CARF partners: Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway are EEA states and not third countries for Chapter V. The United Kingdom, Korea, Jersey, Guernsey and New Zealand are covered by adequacy decisions that reach public authorities. **Japan, South Africa and Indonesia** remain as Chapter V profiles — Japan because its decision excludes the public sector, South Africa and Indonesia because no adequacy decision exists.

## Adverse and framing authority

Article 29 Working Party WP 234 of 16 December 2015, on automatic exchange of personal data for tax purposes, states that where a third country is covered by an adequacy decision "personal data may be transferred to the receiving country, provided that the safeguards cited in the Adequacy Decision are applied under the responsibility of the proceeding tax authority". The same document holds that, given the comprehensive and systematic nature of the transfer, the public-interest derogation cannot apply. A blanket claim that adequacy never covers tax transfers is therefore unsustainable; the Article 49 closure is well supported.

EDPB Guidelines 02/2024 on Article 48, footnote 23, give the correct formulation: "The assessment whether an adequacy decision is applicable should be done on a case by case basis, taking in particular into account the scope of the adequacy decision."

Commission report COM(2024) 7 of 15 January 2024 reviewing eleven pre-GDPR decisions contains no reference to tax and frames government access only as law-enforcement and national-security access to EU-origin data, never as a public authority receiving an EU transfer. The EDPB letter of 5 December 2024 records that it issued no opinion, and that government access "has not been examined by the Commission in the procedure for adopting the eleven adequacy decisions now confirmed". That is the strongest available support for a Chapter V argument that does not depend on material scope.
