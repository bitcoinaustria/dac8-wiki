# Council Directive 2003/48/EC (Savings Directive)

## Source identity

- Council Directive 2003/48/EC of 3 June 2003 on taxation of savings income in the form of interest payments
- CELEX `32003L0048`; OJ L 157, 26.6.2003, p. 38; legal basis Article 94 EC
- Entry into force 16 July 2003 (20th day after publication, Article 19); national measures to be adopted before 1 January 2004 (Article 17(1))
- Application: Article 17(2) set 1 January 2005, conditional on equivalent measures in Switzerland, Liechtenstein, San Marino, Monaco and Andorra and in the dependent or associated territories. Council Decision 2004/587/EC of 19 July 2004 replaced that date with 1 July 2005 (see [2026-09-30-eu-savings-council-decision-2004-587.md](2026-09-30-eu-savings-council-decision-2004-587.md)).
- Amended by Directive 2006/98/EC (accession) and Directive 2014/48/EU; repealed by Directive (EU) 2015/2060 with effect from 1 January 2016, and for Austria from 1 January 2017 subject to run-off obligations.

## Content

The recitals build on the Santa Maria da Feira European Council of 19–20 June 2000 and the aim of effective taxation of interest paid in one Member State to individual beneficial owners resident in another. The mechanism is the "paying agent": the economic operator that pays or secures interest for the immediate benefit of the beneficial owner (Article 4). Paying agents must establish the beneficial owner's identity and residence, using anti-money-laundering data for older relationships and passport or identity card (with a tax identification number where one exists) for relationships from 1 January 2004 (Article 3).

The paying agent reports identity and residence, its own name and address, the account number or debt-claim identifier and the interest amounts to its own competent authority (Article 8). That authority automatically communicates the information to the beneficial owner's State of residence at least once a year, within six months after the end of the tax year (Article 9).

During a transitional period Belgium, Luxembourg and Austria were not required to apply automatic exchange but still received information (Article 10). Instead they levied a withholding tax of 15 % for the first three years, 20 % for the next three and 35 % thereafter (Article 11), retained 25 % and transferred 75 % of the revenue to the residence State (Article 12). Beneficial owners could avoid the withholding by authorising reporting or presenting a residence-State certificate (Article 13). The transitional period was to end after agreements on exchange on request with Switzerland, Liechtenstein, San Marino, Monaco and Andorra and a Council finding on the United States (Article 10(2)).

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

This is the first EU instrument that makes private financial intermediaries report customer data for automatic cross-border exchange. It also shows that Austria's banking-secrecy position was accommodated by an anonymous withholding-tax alternative until the move to the OECD standard. Belgium's and Luxembourg's later switches to exchange are not verified in this record.
