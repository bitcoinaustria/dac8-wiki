## Reconsideration

After the change of leadership, the CFPB told the court on 23 May 2025 that it had "determined that the Rule is unlawful and should be set aside" (ECF 57). It then moved for summary judgment against its own rule. On 29 Jul 2025 it changed course. It asked for a stay of the case so that it could run an "accelerated rulemaking process" that would culminate in "a new final rule that substantially revises the Rule under review" (ECF 80).

The ANPR (90 FR 40986) asks for comments on four topics:

- who counts as a consumer's "representative"
- fees for data access
- data-security threats and cost-benefit
- data-privacy threats

It also asks about compliance dates. In the privacy section the ANPR asks: "Does the PFDR Rule provide adequate protection of consumer privacy?" (Q30). It cites 12 CFR 1033.401, 1033.411(b) and 1033.421 as the rule's existing privacy provisions and asks for comments on "any modifications to the PFDR Rule's provisions".

No proposed rule has been published as of 23 Sep 2026. The Unified Agenda still lists the reconsideration at the proposed-rule stage.

## Litigation, stay and injunction

- **Tolling orders (2025):** ECF 41 (25 Feb 2025) and ECF 45 (27 Mar 2025) stayed the case and tolled the compliance deadlines by a total of 90 days. The first compliance date moved from 1 Apr 2026 to 30 Jun 2026 (ECF 90 at 4; ANPR n.11).
- **Case stayed (29 Jul 2025):** the court stayed the case (ECF 83, cited in ECF 88).
- **Enforcement enjoined (29 Oct 2025):** in ECF 90 the court granted the plaintiffs' motion and ordered that "[t]he Consumer Financial Protection Bureau is ENJOINED from enforcing the Personal Financial Data Rights Rule until it has completed its reconsideration of the Rule." The court described the relief as temporarily staying the Rule, "postponing its effective date under 5 U.S.C. § 705—pending final rulemaking on its reconsideration". It found the plaintiffs likely to succeed, based in part on their data-security arguments.
- **Not vacated:** the court did not vacate the Rule or any provision of it. The injunction and § 705 stay cover the Rule as a whole; they do not single out § 1033.421.
- **Appeals:** the FTA and the CFPB both appealed (6th Cir. Nos. 25-6164 and 26-5005, consolidated 12 Feb 2026). The briefing schedule was reset on 9 Mar 2026: opening briefs due 22 Jun 2026, appellee brief due 21 Aug 2026, replies due 11 Sep 2026. On 13 Mar 2026 the banks moved to hold the appeals in abeyance, and the CFPB and FTA opposed on 23 Mar 2026.
- **Not verified:** the RECAP record available here does not show a ruling on the abeyance motion, any briefs filed after March 2026, or any Sixth Circuit decision. No primary source seen shows the injunction being lifted, modified or reversed as of 23 Sep 2026.

## Compliance dates

- **In the CFR:** the codified dates in 12 CFR 1033.121(b) still read 1 Apr 2026 through 1 Apr 2030.
- **Tolling:** the court orders shifted them by 90 days, so the first date became 30 Jun 2026.
- **Under the injunction:** since 29 Oct 2025 the CFPB may not enforce the Rule until its reconsideration is complete, so in practice no compliance date is currently being enforced.
- **Planned extension not published:** the ANPR said the Bureau "plans to issue a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking to extend the compliance dates" (90 FR 40989). No such notice appears in the Federal Register.

## Sensitivity characterisation and § 1033.421

- **Status of § 1033.421:** it remains in the eCFR unamended. It has not been vacated or formally proposed for amendment. It is within the scope of the ANPR's privacy questions, so it may be revised. Like the rest of the Rule, it is currently subject to the court-ordered stay and the injunction against enforcement.
- **Sensitivity characterisation:** the statement that account data are "potentially sensitive personal financial information" comes from the 2024 preamble (the § 1001.2(b) discussion). It is agency reasoning, not codified text. Section 1001.2(b) itself remains in force in the eCFR, and the injunction as written covers the "Personal Financial Data Rights Rule".
- **The 2025 ANPR goes further on sensitivity.** It states: "A consumer's financial transactions reveal an enormous amount of information about their habits and lifestyle". It says transaction data can reveal "(b) financial vulnerability; (c) financial abundance that could make them the target of criminal activity". It also refers to "unwitting licensing or sale of sensitive personal financial information" (90 FR 40988-89). Under the current leadership, then, the CFPB still regards transaction data as sensitive, even though the 2025 ANPR is not a rule.

## What can and cannot be cited

- **Can be cited:**
  - that the CFPB, in a final rule (89 FR 90838, 2024), characterised consumer account data as potentially sensitive and adopted secondary-use limits in § 1033.421(a)
  - that these provisions remain codified in 12 CFR part 1033 as of September 2026
  - the 2025 ANPR's own description of transaction data as revealing lifestyle, financial vulnerability and "financial abundance" (90 FR 40988), as the reconsidering agency's current view
- **Cannot be cited:**
  - § 1033.421 as an enforceable, operative US obligation. Enforcement is enjoined and the Rule is stayed under 5 U.S.C. § 705 pending reconsideration (*Forcht Bank v. CFPB*, E.D. Ky. No. 5:24-cv-00304, ECF 90, 29 Oct 2025), and the CFPB has said it intends to substantially revise the Rule.
  - the 2024 compliance dates as current.
- **Suggested wording:** "adopted in the CFPB's 2024 Section 1033 rule (12 CFR 1033.421, codified but under reconsideration and stayed/enjoined since Oct 2025)".
- **Not verified from primary sources:** Sixth Circuit developments after March 2026, and any CFPB action after the August 2026 Unified Agenda that is not published in the Federal Register.
