# Council Directive (EU) 2016/2258 (DAC5, tax access to AML information)

## Source identity

- Council Directive (EU) 2016/2258 of 6 December 2016 amending Directive 2011/16/EU as regards access to anti-money-laundering information by tax authorities; CELEX `32016L2258`; OJ L 342, 16.12.2016, p. 1; legal basis Articles 113 and 115 TFEU
- Entry into force on the date of adoption, 6 December 2016 (Article 3)
- Transposition by 31 December 2017; application from 1 January 2018 (Article 2(1))
- Commission proposal COM(2016) 452 of 5 July 2016 (see [2026-09-30-eu-dac5-proposal-com-2016-452.md](2026-09-30-eu-dac5-proposal-com-2016-452.md))

## Content

The recitals explain that CRS reporting under Directive 2014/107/EU, applicable from 1 January 2016 (Austria 1 January 2017), requires financial institutions to look through intermediary structures to beneficial owners using AML data under Directive (EU) 2015/849. Tax authorities need access to that AML information to monitor whether financial institutions apply the due diligence correctly (recitals 2–3), and access would also serve all other forms of DAC cooperation (recital 4). Recital 6 states that access "by law" need not be an act of parliament but must be clear, precise and foreseeable in line with CJEU and ECtHR case law. Recital 8 notes that the first CRS exchanges were to be completed by September 2017.

The operative change is a single new Article 22(1a): Member States shall provide by law for tax-authority access to the mechanisms, procedures, documents and information referred to in Articles 13 (customer due diligence), 30 and 31 (beneficial-ownership information and registers) and 40 (record keeping) of Directive (EU) 2015/849.

No new reporting duty for private parties; the change widens what tax authorities may access.

## Relevance and limits for DAC8

DAC5 is the formal bridge between the AML regime and tax administration and is the reason AML-collected customer data can be reused for tax purposes. Its purpose statement is monitoring of DAC due diligence plus DAC cooperation generally, which matters when assessing purpose limitation arguments. For the AML register case law see [2026-08-27-cjeu-c37-c601-20-aml-register.md](2026-08-27-cjeu-c37-c601-20-aml-register.md).
