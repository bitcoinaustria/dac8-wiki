# MiCA and DORA security baseline for authorised crypto-asset service providers

DORA Article 2(1)(f) applies to authorised crypto-asset service providers. Article 9 requires continuous monitoring, appropriate security tools and procedures, protection of data at rest, in use and in transit, and network design capable of segmentation. Article 10 requires prompt anomalous-activity detection, tested multilayer controls, alerts and resources to monitor user activity and cyberattacks.

MiCA Article 68(7) requires resilient and secure ICT systems and continuity, response and recovery planning under DORA. Article 68(8) requires DORA mechanisms, effective AML/CFT risk procedures, periodic effectiveness review and safeguards for the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data. Article 68(9) separately requires service, activity, order and transaction records for five years, extendable to seven on authority request.

This is the provider-side State counterargument: an authorised CASP does not operate without binding ICT controls. It also supplies the correct factual test. The record must show how those controls apply to the distinct DAC8 pipeline, including tax-specific copies, privileges, service providers, exports, logs, retention and deletion. Compliance with a horizontal framework cannot be assumed from authorisation alone, but neither can non-compliance.

MiCA/DORA duties do not authorise tax-purpose reuse of TFR, AML or MiCA records. Security, legal basis, purpose compatibility, minimisation and retention remain distinct questions.
