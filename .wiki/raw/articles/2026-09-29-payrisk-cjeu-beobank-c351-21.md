# CJEU C-351/21 Beobank (2023)

Judgment of the Court (Fifth Chamber), 16 March 2023, *ZG v Beobank SA*, C-351/21, reference from the justice de paix du canton de Forest (Belgium). The text was read through the EU Publications Office copy of CELEX 62021CJ0351. It interprets Directive 2007/64 (PSD1). PSD2 Articles 64, 73 and 74 carry forward the same structure, but the judgment does not rule on PSD2.

**Facts (paras 2, 20–21).** A cardholder claimed two debit-card payments of EUR 1,984 were unauthorised after his card was stolen. The bank refused a refund, "contending that the transactions were authorised or that, at the very least, ZG was grossly negligent".

**Holdings used here.**

- Para 37: the liability regime for unauthorised or incorrectly executed transactions "has been the subject of full harmonisation". A parallel liability regime for the same operative event, or a competing regime triggered by other events, is incompatible with the directive. The Court cites *CRCAM*, C-337/20, 2 September 2021, EU:C:2021:671, paras 42 and 46. *CRCAM* itself was not read; only this citation was.
- Para 38: an alternative national regime can compete only if it does not adversely affect the harmonised regime (again citing *CRCAM*, para 45).
- Para 39: a national court "cannot rule on a claim for reimbursement ... without first classifying those payments as authorised or unauthorised".
- Operative part: under Article 47(1)(a), the payer's provider must give information identifying the payee, not only what it has available.

**Evidence class and use.** This is a binding CJEU interpretation of PSD1. It supports the point that, in EU payment law, the refund depends on the authorised/unauthorised classification, and that national general liability law cannot replace that regime for the same event. It does **not** define consent where the payer was deceived or physically coerced, and it gives no ruling on push payments that the payer initiates. For the bearer-asset comparison, it confirms that the bank-side backstop depends on classification. A payment the payer authorised falls outside the refund, and a self-custody transfer has no counterpart to either rule.
