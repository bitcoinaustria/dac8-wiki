# French Interior Ministry statements on crypto-related targeting

Source digest. Three items:

1. **"Crypto-actifs : protéger les particuliers"**, dated 22 January 2026. Fetched through a text-rendering proxy because the site blocks automated clients.
2. **"Criminalité dans le domaine des cryptomonnaies : halte aux idées reçues"**, 2025, in the [Wayback capture of 13 January 2026](http://web.archive.org/web/20260113050510/https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/actualites-du-ministere/criminalite-dans-domaine-des-cryptomonnaies-halte-aux-idees-recues).
3. **The minister's 30 June 2026 announcement**, available only through media ([Capital via Yahoo, 1 July 2026](https://fr.finance.yahoo.com/actualites/cryptomonnaies-77-faits-s%C3%A9questration-enl%C3%A8vement-080353851.html); [Journal du Coin, 1 July 2026](https://journalducoin.com/actualites/crypto-rapt-france-plan-action-laurent-nunez)). The official press release was not retrieved.

**January 2026 page.** The page says professionals remain exposed, but "l'actualité montre que les particuliers sont désormais également ciblés" (recent events show private individuals are now also targeted). COMCYBER-MI and ADAN run prevention work. The advice to holders:

- do not show holdings or gains online;
- avoid wallet screenshots;
- be discreet about crypto activity, including offline;
- impose a delay on releasing large amounts;
- never share private keys;
- in an emergency, call 17.

**"Idées reçues" page.** The ministry describes abductions of crypto professionals, including mutilation to extract a crypto ransom, and cites Article 224-2 of the Criminal Code. It argues against a supposed impunity: blockchain is "transparente", crypto is "nativement traçable", and funds can be seized or confiscated. It notes that the AGRASC asset-recovery agency has seized €90 million in crypto-assets since 2014 and that CASPs must freeze funds and report under MiCA/TFR.

**June 2026 plan (media-reported).** Minister Laurent Nuñez reported 77 cases of sequestration, abduction or extortion, or attempts, since January 2026, against 45 in all of 2025 (Journal du Coin). He also reported 200 arrests (Capital). The plan has three axes: intelligence sharing and partnership with ADAN, an expert network, and operational and international coordination.

Evidence class: official ministry communications (items 1 and 2) and media-reported ministerial statements (item 3). They are not audited statistics or judicial findings.

The pages support three points: private holders are an officially recognised target group; the State's own advice treats disclosure of holdings as a risk factor; and police combine ransom-denial tactics with tracing. The ministry does not identify how victims are selected and says nothing about leaks, registers or tax data.

Adverse evidence: the traceability and seizure statements qualify simple "irreversibility" claims. Stolen funds that reach a regulated CASP, or a stablecoin whose issuer can freeze it, may be frozen. That recovery depends on tracing and cooperation after the fact, not on a refund right. The figure of 45 for 2025 conflicts with the "about sixty" asserted in QE 12853; the two counts use different definitions and sources.
