# Circle and Tether: issuer terms on blocking and freezing stablecoin addresses

Contractual terms from the issuers, read on 29 September 2026. [Circle USDC Terms](https://www.circle.com/legal/usdc-terms), last updated 12 December 2025. [Circle USDC Risk Factors](https://www.circle.com/legal/usdc-risk-factors). [Tether Terms of Service](https://tether.to/en/legal/), last updated 26 February 2026. The separate Circle blocklisting policy was not located and was not read.

**Circle.** USDC Terms, "Blocklisting": "USDC is issued and redeemed in accordance with Circle's blocklisting policy. Circle reserves the right to block the transfer of USDC to and from an address on chain as permitted under the blocklisting policy." Circle may also "freeze USDC and/or surrender associated USD held in Segregated Accounts" upon a legal order from a valid government authority. The Risk Factors page contains parallel "blacklisting" wording limited to "extraordinary circumstances exclusively per the terms of the blacklisting policy".

**Tether.** The Terms provide that Tether "may ... freeze any Tether Tokens held by you ... as required by applicable Law or where Tether, in its sole discretion, determines it is prudent to do so". Among its remedies, Tether reserves "the right to either freeze your Tether Tokens or seize and recover against any of your Tether Tokens".

**Relevance and limits.** On account-based chains, a fiat-backed stablecoin balance is an entry in the issuer's token contract, recorded against an address. The issuer can therefore see and control it at address level. Freeze volumes are measured in the Wu et al. preprint (2026). The terms show legal authority, not how often it is used or the criteria actually applied. Direction: **supports** a strengthened version of the thesis for issuer-controlled tokens: an address holding USDT or USDC is visible and can be frozen by the issuer. It does not address native-asset privacy.
