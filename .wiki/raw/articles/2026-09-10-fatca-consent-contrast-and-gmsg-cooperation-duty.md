# The FATCA contrast and the new GMSG cooperation duty

## FATCA: consent, specificity, suspicion

Austria has no FATCA implementing statute. The Model 2 IGA, BGBl. III Nr. 16/2015, applies directly. The Erläuterungen (262 BlgNR XXV. GP) record that no Erfüllungsvorbehalt under Art. 50 Abs. 2 Z 4 B-VG was adopted and that "[d]ie in Absatz 1 normierten Verpflichtungen finden unmittelbar auf Finanzinstitute Anwendung, sodass es keiner innerstaatlichen Umsetzung des Abkommens bedarf."

The mechanism is consent-based and account-specific. Article 2(1)(b)(i) requires the institution to obtain the account holder's US TIN "und Zustimmung zur Meldung", with written notice that absent consent the account will be reported only in aggregate form. Article 2(1)(d) makes consent a condition of opening a new account identified as a US account.

The Erläuterungen explain why: "Da das österreichische Recht den automatischen Austausch von Bankinformationen nicht ermöglicht, welcher jedoch Voraussetzung für den Abschluss eines 'Model 1'-Abkommens wäre, wurden auf Wunsch der Banken- und Versicherungswirtschaft Verhandlungen zum Abschluss eines 'Model 2'-Abkommens aufgenommen."

The group-request leg is suspicion-gated. § 4 Abs. 2 ADG admits a group request only where the requesting state has "Grund zur Vermutung … dass die von dieser Gruppe umfassten Personen steuerrechtliche Vorschriften des um Amtshilfe ersuchenden Staates nicht eingehalten haben" and describes the group and the concrete circumstances sufficiently.

## Correction on § 38 BWG

§ 38 Abs. 2 BWG in the current version contains no FATCA Ziffer. Z 10 covers the GMSG and, since BGBl. I Nr. 96/2025, the Krypto-MPfG. The IGA operates through Z 5, the customer's express consent, which is precisely why it is built on Zustimmung.

## The new cooperation duty on the CRS side

§ 5a Abs. 1 GMSG, in force 1 January 2026 (BGBl. I Nr. 96/2025): "Kontoinhaber und sonstige Kunden haben meldenden Finanzinstituten alle für Zwecke der Erfüllung der Melde- und Sorgfaltspflichten im Sinne dieses Bundesgesetzes erforderlichen Unterlagen und Informationen nach Aufforderung des Finanzinstitutes richtig und vollständig zur Verfügung zu stellen."

§ 108a GMSG makes intentional breach a Finanzordnungswidrigkeit punishable by a fine of up to EUR 5 000.

Because § 5 Abs. 1 GMSG reaches all account holders and other customers irrespective of reportable status, the cooperation duty and its penalty attach to persons who will never be reported.

## Research use

This is the sharpest proportionality contrast available. For transfers to the United States, Austria requires the customer's consent, an identified US account and, on the assistance leg, articulated suspicion. For CRS and CARF partners it mandates collection on the entire customer base, including persons no state will ever receive data about, and from 2026 backs the corresponding cooperation duty with a fine.

The comparison must be handled carefully. Model 2 was chosen because Austrian bank secrecy then barred automatic exchange, not because the legislature made a proportionality judgment. It shows a workable less-intrusive design exists, not that the legislature preferred it on rights grounds.

## Comparator caveat on Germany

Germany does have a blanket collection clause on the CRS side. § 6 Abs. 1 FKAustG, inserted by Art. 37 JStG 2024 with effect from 6 December 2024, obliges institutions to determine the account holder's tax residence "unabhängig davon, ob es sich bei dem Kontoinhaber oder dem sonstigen Kunden um eine meldepflichtige Person … handelt".

Two differences remain, and the Austrian argument should be framed on them rather than on a claim that Germany never does this. The German duty is confined to tax residence and foreign tax identification numbers; the Austrian duty extends to the full § 3 and § 12 dataset, which includes addresses, telephone numbers, standing orders, powers of attorney and hold-mail arrangements. And the German clause dates from December 2024, whereas Austria has had the power since 2016 and the duty since 2019.

On the crypto side the position is unchanged: § 14 KStTG limits record-keeping to reportable users and orders deletion, and Germany enacted no counterpart to § 20 Abs. 1 Krypto-MPfG.
