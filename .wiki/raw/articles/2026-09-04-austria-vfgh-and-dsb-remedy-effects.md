# Austria DSB and VfGH remedy effects

[Section 24(5) DSG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Anlage=&Artikel=2&Gesetzesnummer=10001597&Paragraf=24) requires a successful complaint against a private controller to be remedied by an order satisfying the complainant's request for access, correction, erasure, restriction or portability to the extent necessary to eliminate the violation. Under section 24(6), later compliance may make the individual complaint moot after the complainant is heard.

[Section 85 VfGG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10000245&Paragraf=85) provides that an Article 144 complaint has no automatic suspensive effect. A granted application suspends enforcement of the challenged judgment; it does not create a processing prohibition that the challenged judgment refused.

[Section 87 VfGG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10000245&Paragraf=87) requires the VfGH to annul a judgment where the complainant was constitutionally injured. The administrative court and authority must then restore a legal state consistent with the VfGH's view using their available powers. The VfGH does not itself issue the provider-facing correction or deletion order.

[Article 139 B-VG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Artikel=139&Gesetzesnummer=10000138) and [Article 140 B-VG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Artikel=140&Gesetzesnummer=10000138) distinguish regulation and statute review. Annulment ordinarily takes effect after publication, but the VfGH may set a future date, up to 18 months for statutes and, for regulations, generally six months or 18 months where legislative preparation is required. Earlier facts remain governed by the prior statute subject to the originating case and any different ruling.
