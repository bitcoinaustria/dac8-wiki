# ECtHR Big Brother Watch on end-to-end safeguards

In *Big Brother Watch and Others v United Kingdom*, applications 58170/13 and others, Grand Chamber judgment of 25 May 2021, the Court held that Article 8 does not prohibit strategic bulk interception as such. States have a margin in selecting the system, and individual suspicion is not necessarily the organising condition of preventive bulk acquisition (paragraphs 347 to 349).

The Convention requires end-to-end safeguards, including independent authorisation, supervision and ex-post review across acquisition, selection, examination, use, storage and transfer. The violations resulted from identified control defects, not from the word bulk alone (paragraphs 350 to 364 and 425 to 427). Judicial pre-authorisation was not made universally mandatory.

The national-security and secret-interception context differs sharply from statutory tax reporting. The narrow DAC8 use is a lifecycle checklist, especially for foreign recipients, onward transfer, individual selectors, deletion and binding review. The case is also adverse to a claim that generality alone decides proportionality.
