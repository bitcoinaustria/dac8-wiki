# FATF Forty Recommendations 1990

Recommendations 12–18 supplied an early core of the preventive financial-sector system: no anonymous or fictitious accounts, customer identification, reasonable measures to determine the person on whose behalf a customer acted, records retained for at least five years and scrutiny of complex or unusual transactions. Recommendation 16 said institutions should be permitted or required to report qualifying suspicions, followed by reporter protection and no tipping off.

The person-on-whose-behalf requirement was a predecessor, not the later comprehensive beneficial-ownership architecture. The 1990 text likewise did not yet impose one uniform mandatory STR rule across all jurisdictions.

These were standards addressed to governments and financial systems. Their enforceability depended on later EU and national law.
