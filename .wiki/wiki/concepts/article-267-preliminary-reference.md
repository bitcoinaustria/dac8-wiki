# Article 267 Preliminary Reference

> A preliminary reference is a court-to-court mechanism inside a live national case, not an individual petition to the CJEU.

## Function

Article 267 covers interpretation of the Treaties and validity or interpretation of EU acts. A national court refers only where an answer is necessary to give judgment.

The VfGH has used this mechanism, including in the Austrian data-retention review. It can ask whether DAC8 is valid under Charter rights or how a directive requirement must be interpreted before it decides the Austrian challenge.

## Last-instance duty

Consorzio and KUBERA require a last-instance court to refer a necessary unresolved question unless:

- it is irrelevant to the outcome;
- existing CJEU authority resolves it;
- the answer is so clear that no reasonable doubt remains under the demanding acte-clair standard.

A reasoned request improves the chance of serious consideration, but a party cannot manufacture necessity by proposing a broad policy question.

## Validity boundary

Under Foto-Frost, the VfGH may reject an invalidity argument but cannot itself invalidate DAC8. If it considers the Austrian case to depend on a serious claim that Directive (EU) 2023/2226 violates the Charter or Treaties, the validity question belongs to the CJEU.

The VfGH retains power to repeal Austrian implementing provisions. The analysis must identify whether the defect is mandated by DAC8 or is an Austrian choice.

## Return to the national proceeding

A preliminary ruling does not replace the national court's disposition of the underlying case. In the Austrian data-retention litigation, the VfGH stayed its proceedings in 2012, the CJEU declared Directive 2006/24/EC invalid in April 2014, and the VfGH then resumed the cases and annulled the Austrian core provisions in June 2014.

For DAC8 analysis, three questions therefore remain separate: what the CJEU decides about EU law, how the competent national court disposes of the pending case, and when any resulting relief becomes effective. Obtaining a reference is a procedural milestone, not by itself a final national remedy.
