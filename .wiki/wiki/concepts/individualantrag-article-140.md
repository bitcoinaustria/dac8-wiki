# Individualantrag under Article 140 B-VG

> The Individualantrag is a subsidiary exceptional remedy, not an actio popularis and not the default path for statutory data processing.

## Admissibility gates

The application must show that the challenged statute itself adversely affects the applicant's legally protected sphere:

- currently, not only potentially or in the future;
- directly, without a necessary intervening decision or discretionary act;
- in a type and extent determined by the statute;
- with no other reasonable way to bring the objections to the VfGH;
- through an exact challenge perimeter covering the provisions needed for an effective remedy without overreaching.

Each applicant must supply their own facts. Legal representation is mandatory; fees, service rules, and cost exposure require current verification before filing.

## What the retention case actually held

In G 59/2012 and G 62/2012 ua, Seitlinger and Tschohl proved active subscriber relationships, actual statutory retention, and the services/lines involved. Provider-facing wording did not defeat standing because the duty was designed to store the users' data automatically.

The alternative-remedy exception turned on extraordinary facts: retention across a practically untraceable set of providers, including communication partners' providers; continuous regeneration; and six-month deletion that could make later review ineffective.

The 11,129 other collective applicants were rejected because only Tschohl's own current/direct facts were pleaded. A campaign instruction to sign only when covered could not cure that substantive omission.

## The current adverse line

- G 147/08 ua and Tretter rejected an abstract challenge to data powers where no present retention duty and ordinary data-protection remedies existed.
- G 385/2018 (ELGA) and V 532/2020 (smart meter) enforced current-effect, exact-perimeter, and alternative-remedy limits.
- G 17/2024 held that an available request for a reviewable decision defeated a direct application; ordinary time and cost do not become unreasonable without special, extraordinary circumstances, and the applicants could ask the BVwG to initiate Article 140 review.
- G 178/2024, decided 28 April 2026, held that a section 24 DSG complaint to the DSB, BVwG appeal, and Article 144 complaint provide a reasonable route to challenge mandatory vaccination-record processing. Poor immediate prospects before the DSB were irrelevant. The Court left challenge perimeter, adequate pleading, and a possible independent GDPR admissibility problem undecided.

## DAC8 consequence

An ordinary DAC8 user normally knows their CASP; records are retained far longer than six months; and the DAC2 litigation proves the DSB → BVwG → VfGH/VwGH chain is operational. Those facts materially weaken the retention exception.

For a user, a direct Article 140 application is best treated as a fallback only if the claimant can prove why the DSB route is genuinely incapable of effective protection—not merely slower, inconvenient, or likely to fail. The strongest conceivable distinction is a specific irreversible injury that cannot be prevented through timely DSB/BVwG protection; generic cross-border transfer or breach risk is insufficient. The factual file should include the provider relationship, reportable period/activity, notices/self-certification, actual processing, affected provisions, deadlines, unavailable protection, and requested remedy.

A tax route must be proved rather than inferred from the Krypto-MPfG's BAO references. Although section 19(2) treats the provider report as an `Abgabenerklärung`, section 243 BAO requires a tax-authority Bescheid for a Bescheidbeschwerde. No ordinary pre-report user Bescheid has been identified. A later assessment route to the BFG is not automatically a remedy against the earlier provider processing or report.

## Different result for the provider

G 105/2025 and G 606/2015 accepted direct applications by businesses required to make material technical and administrative preparations for penalised data or tax-compliance duties. Present and direct effect can arise before the compliance date when delay would make the expenditure unavoidable; the business need not provoke a sanction.

An Austria-home CASP is therefore the strongest direct DAC8 applicant when its section 15 status is undisputed, the challenged duties operate without an intervening decision, and no ordinary determination route exists. It should plead completed or contracted interface, data-pipeline, storage, security, DPIA, and staffing work with invoices and sworn evidence. The official WFA supports the materiality of the overall burden but does not isolate the cost of any Austrian excess.

These precedents are adverse on the merits: both broad reporting or recordkeeping systems survived proportionality review. Standing and constitutional success must not be conflated.

The broader successful-case corpus confirms the same method. G 37/02 ua, G 118/2015 ua, G 100/05 ua, and G 20/2015 ua show that direct technical duties, capital expenditure, pre-commencement preparations, and penal exposure can establish a company's current effect. G 52/2024 shows that a person need not be the formal criminal-law addressee where the rule directly makes the intended legal relationship impossible. None dispenses with exact applicant evidence, challenge perimeter, or the no-other-route audit.
