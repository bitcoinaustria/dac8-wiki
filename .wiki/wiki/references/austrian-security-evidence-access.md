# Austrian Security-Evidence Access

Existing security safeguards can be tested through documentary evidence without assuming that every audit or DPIA must become public. The applicable route depends on the information and the claimant's procedural position.

| Route | Reach | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| GDPR Article 15 | Personal data and information about the person's processing | Not general access to institutional audit files |
| IFG | Existing, available official records held within the competent organ's sphere | Proportionate secrecy, special access regimes and burden limits |
| DSB investigation | Necessary controller/processor evidence under Article 58 and section 22 DSG | Supervisory access does not guarantee full party disclosure |

## 1. Own consultation data

C-579/21, paragraphs 79–83, distinguishes dates and purposes of consultations of the person's data from employee identities. Identities require necessity for effective exercise of GDPR rights and consideration of employees' rights. Paragraph 82 directs unresolved lawfulness doubts toward supervisory investigation. This does not confer access to logs concerning other users or general security-audit reports.

## 2. Existing authority documentation

Sections 2(1), 3(2) and 7 IFG support precisely identified existing records. The IFG does not require creation of a new risk assessment or research report. As an evidential strategy, useful targets include existing DPIA or exemption memoranda, audit scope and completion records, documented remediation and already available anonymised control statistics. Their existence remains unproved.

Section 6(1) protects security, unaffected decision preparation, personal data and business secrets only within its necessity, proportionality and balancing requirements. Sections 6(2) and 9(2) require feasible, proportionate partial access. LVwG Niederösterreich, 23 February 2026, LVwG-AV-55/001-2026, reasons 2.3, rejects generic whole-document secrecy and requires examination of redaction. The case concerned a rescue-services contract and resulted in remittal, not full disclosure; transfer to DAC8 is methodological.

Section 8 provides four weeks, with a reasoned four-week extension possible. After non-disclosure, section 11(1) provides a separate written request for a decision within two months. Section 16 preserves special statutory access regimes: IFG should not be treated as a bypass around party-file restrictions.

## 3. DSB examination and party access

GDPR Article 58(1)(a), (b), (e), (f) and section 22(1)–(2) DSG permit necessary information demands, audits and inspection. Relevant material may include a DPIA, role/export matrix, access-review findings and remediation records tied to identified processing. Article 30(4) concerns production to the authority; Article 36(3)(e) requires the DPIA during prior consultation.

Since 1 September 2025, section 22(3) DSG refers to necessary and proportionate secrecy under section 6(1) IFG, while retaining supervisory-purpose limits. Party access under section 17 AVG remains restricted by protected interests, authority tasks and procedural purpose. These powers are not private discovery or an unconditional entitlement to every chosen investigation. Restricted party disclosure does not itself remove the authority's ability to inspect necessary evidence.

No identified decision establishes disclosure of an Austrian DAC8 DPIA or audit. Statutory-processing and Austrian DPIA-exemption defences still require separate assessment.
