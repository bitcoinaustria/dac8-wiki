# Austrian Risk-Based Register-Access Protection

Austrian register law supplies concrete comparisons for individual risk protection. The mechanisms restrict particular disclosures while preserving collection and important institutional access. They establish neither a DAC8 exemption nor an Austrian tax-system security failure.

## WiEReG: scope and practical operation

The [BMF Erlass](../../raw/articles/2026-09-19-bmf-wiereg-protection-erlass.md), section 8.3, explains § 10a's individual risk assessment and provisional protection: normally within 14 days unless manifestly unfounded, with a faster temporary hold available before registration. A merits decision follows within twelve months. Address-only restriction is required where sufficient; grants last five years or, for minors, until majority. These are administrative explanations of WiEReG, not independent rights created by the Erlass.

The [FAQ](../../raw/articles/2026-09-19-bmf-wiereg-protection-faq.md), section 13 question 6, identifies the retained access: § 12 authorities, credit and financial institutions, and notaries. Suppression for other obliged entities includes historical extracts but still signals the existence of a protected owner. Reporting obligations remain.

The [BMF statistics](../../raw/articles/2026-09-19-bmf-wiereg-risk-protection-statistics.md) report 609 Gewährungen at end-2025. Listed grounds include 310 kidnapping/extortion-risk entries and 101 life/bodily-integrity-risk entries, with overlapping grounds expressly permitted. The page does not sufficiently define persons, decisions, active grants or annual new grants as the unit. These are reported administrative figures, not attack counts or a measure of protection effectiveness.

## Address protection and limits

[MeldeG § 18(2)](../../raw/articles/2026-09-19-meldeg-address-access-protection.md) permits renewable restrictions of up to five years on a credible protected interest. Section 18(5) nevertheless allows disclosure to someone proving entitlement to assert a legal obligation, after notification and an opportunity to comment. Section 20(3) preserves essential statutory authority uses, with additional proportionality for linked queries.

The BMF Erlass, section 8.4, describes a useful interaction: a MeldeG restriction replaces residential-address details in WiEReG extracts with Austrian-residence information and a restriction marker. This illustrates selective fields and protection flags; it does not establish equivalent DAC8 controls.

Adverse authority remains material. [VfGH E 2189/2025](../../raw/articles/2026-08-27-vfgh-e2189-2025-wiereg-internal-data.md), [full judgment](../../../legal-docs/supporting/at-vfgh-e2189-2025-wiereg-internal-data.pdf), paragraphs 78–79, accepted the case-specific denial of protection where relevant information was already public in the Firmenbuch and disproportionate danger had not been demonstrated. The § 10a(2) text reproduced on page 33 requires materially elevated factual risk compared with comparable owners; mere disclosure of ownership generally does not suffice.

## DAC8 comparison and verification boundary

The research inference is to assess whether narrower fields, recipient restrictions, rapid temporary protection and individual risk review could reduce a demonstrated harm. Preserved authority access is a central limitation of the analogy. No new admissibility route or automatic suspension follows.

[Directive 2024/1640 Article 15](../../raw/articles/2026-09-19-amld6-risk-access-exceptions.md) and existing Austrian mechanisms must remain distinct. The [93/ME proposal](../../raw/articles/2026-08-27-austria-wiereg-2027-ministerial-draft.md) is not proof of enacted implementation; its live parliamentary docket still described a ministerial draft on this check. Full Austrian Article 15 compliance remains unverified, not disproved.

MeldeG was checked in the live RIS consolidation dated 19 September 2026. Direct current WiEReG consolidation retrieval failed; mechanism details rely on current BMF-linked guidance and the existing judgment. This limits any claim of an exhaustive amendment check.
