# DAC8 Challenge Scope and Residual Duties

> The smallest effective unit is not always a single phrase. A successful challenge should remove the identified interference without leaving an equivalent duty or deleting a privacy-protective ceiling.

## Parallel bases for the reportable-user dataset

The core reportable-user fields have two linked Union-law expressions:

- Article 8ad(3) specifies the information exchanged by the competent authority.
- Annex VI Section II.B specifies the provider information reported for the reportable user.

Section 18 Krypto-MPfG implements the corresponding provider report in Austria. A field-removal theory that addresses only Article 8ad(3) can leave the Annex VI provider duty standing. A theory that addresses only Annex VI can leave the authority-exchange command standing. National relief directed only at section 18 cannot invalidate the Union duty.

## Effective challenge bundles

| Desired practical result | Minimum legal perimeter | Duties that remain |
|---|---|---|
| Stop complete section 18 profile creation for a finally non-reportable user | Section 20(1) as a self-contained unit, limited to the all-customer, all-transaction and all-asset profile beyond necessary status due diligence | Annex VI Section III status and self-certification records; section 18 reporting for reportable users; MiCA, TFR, AML and accounting records under their own bases |
| Remove or condition a named report field for a reportable user | Exact section 18 item plus the matching Article 8ad(3) and Annex VI Section II.B items through national and EU review | Every unchallenged identity, provider and transaction field; general reporting architecture |
| Prevent routine wallet-address transmission under section 18 | No target exists because section 18(1)(3)(i) reports aggregate value and units, not the address | Any address records and access under MiCA, TFR, AML, CARF commentary, provider operations or a separate authority request must be traced and challenged separately |
| Shorten provider retention | Separate section 20(4) transmitted information, section 39 compliance records and raw records under other regimes | A section 20(4) win does not remove section 39; a section 39 limitation does not erase other lawful operational records |
| Delete Austrian or foreign authority copies | Identify the responsible authority, copy, source/recipient status, purpose and governing retention rule | Sections 20 and 39 govern provider-side records and do not themselves compel downstream deletion |
| Remove the Austrian valuation hierarchy | Sections 18(3) and (4) | Fair-market-value and reporting duties in section 18(1) and (2), Article 8ad and Annex VI remain |

## Classification of the main Austrian choices

| Rule | Classification | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Section 18(1) and (2) reportable-user fields | Substantially Union-mandated | Field-level EU validity or limiting interpretation is required for system effect |
| Section 18(3) and (4) valuation hierarchy | Austrian implementing detail within the EU fair-market-value duty | Removing the hierarchy does not remove the field |
| Section 20(1) complete information for all customers, transactions and crypto-assets regardless of reportability | National excess candidate beyond universal status due diligence | Prove what complete section 18 profile is actually created or retained for a finally non-reportable user |
| Sections 20(2) and (3) notice and rights information | Union-mandated safeguard | Removing them would weaken rather than strengthen the data subject's position |
| Section 20(4) ten-year deletion point for transmitted information | National maximum choice with disputed Annex VI attribution | Striking a deletion rule alone can remove the ceiling without creating a shorter period |
| Section 39 records for ten years | Union-mandated recordkeeping core, Austrian ten-year duration with a separately defined start date | Section 39 runs from the end of the reporting period; Annex VI V.B.1 runs from the end of the period within which reporting is required. Immediate repeal without replacement cannot satisfy the continuing EU five-year floor |

## Residual-duty traps

1. Deleting only “regardless of reportability” from section 20(1) can leave the command to process information for all customers.
2. Deleting only “store” leaves collection, recording and other processing verbs.
3. Annulment of section 20(4) can remove an outer deletion date while another legal basis continues storage.
4. Annulment of section 39 wholesale can remove the implementing rule needed for the Union five-year minimum rather than produce a five-year substitute. If the EU duty remains valid, delayed annulment and legislative replacement may be needed; do not equate annulment with an immediate right to erase all compliance records.
5. A provider-side order does not automatically alter the Austrian origin copy, a recipient-state copy or a derived risk file.
6. A technical-format win can be cured without changing the statutory report fields.
7. Removal of an Austrian provision cannot by itself invalidate DAC8; invalidity remains for the CJEU.

The requested relief should therefore identify the normative unit, the actor, the copy, the field or operation, the surviving duties and the intended legal and operational effect.
