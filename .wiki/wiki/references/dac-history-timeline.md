# EU Tax-Information Exchange Timeline: 1977 to the DAC Recast

> DAC8 is the seventh amendment to Directive 2011/16/EU, whose predecessor dates from 1977. Over five decades, exchange expanded from requests about specific taxpayers between authorities to annual reporting by financial institutions, platforms and crypto-asset providers. Review populations, reportable accounts/users and exemptions differ between regimes; this is not reporting on every customer. DAC8 also changed rules that apply to every category exchanged under the directive. The pending recast would merge all steps into one act; it is a proposal, not law.

## Phases

| Phase | Mechanism | Who supplies the data |
|---|---|---|
| 1977–2010 | Exchange on request and spontaneous exchange; automatic exchange only where authorities agreed on categories. The Savings Directive (2005–2015) is a separate, interest-only paying-agent regime. | Tax authorities; paying agents for interest |
| 2011–2014 | DAC1 is adopted in 2011, with mandatory automatic exchange of *available* information in five categories from 2015. DAC2 is adopted in 2014 and imports the OECD CRS, generally from 2016 (Austria 2017). | Tax authorities (DAC1); financial institutions (DAC2) |
| 2015–2018 | Corporate transparency: rulings (DAC3), country-by-country reports (DAC4), tax access to AML data (DAC5), reportable cross-border arrangements (DAC6). | Authorities, groups, AML-obliged entities, intermediaries |
| 2021–2025 | New sectors: digital platforms (DAC7), crypto-asset service providers and e-money (DAC8), Pillar Two top-up tax returns (DAC9). | Platform operators, RCASPs, large groups |
| 2026– | Recast COM(2026) 308 pending: consolidation, simplification, new register access. | — |

## Rules common to all categories: the Article 16 change

Article 16 governs the use of every item exchanged under the directive, whatever its category.

| Version | Permitted use without the sender's permission |
|---|---|
| 2011 (original) | Administration and enforcement of the Article 2 taxes; assessment and enforcement of taxes and duties under the Recovery Directive 2010/24/EU and of compulsory social-security contributions; tax-penalty proceedings. Other purposes only with the sending State's permission (Article 16(2)). Onward transmission to a third Member State with 10 working days for objection. |
| DAC8 (Article 1(7), 2023/2226) | Assessment, administration and enforcement of Article 2 taxes, VAT, other indirect taxes, customs duties **and anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism**. New Article 16(2) second subparagraph: use for **Union restrictive measures** (Article 215 TFEU acts) and sharing with the national sanctions authority without permission. Objection period for onward transmission: 15 calendar days. |

The extension therefore reaches financial-account (DAC2), employment and pension (DAC1), platform (DAC7) and all other DAC data, not only crypto-asset data. Customs and VAT use was largely available before through the Recovery Directive reference; AML/CFT and sanctions use without permission is new.

The sanctions exception is specifically for purposes covered by acts based on Article 215 TFEU and sharing with the competent national restrictive-measures authority. It does not authorize unrestricted reuse for any sanction or by any recipient. Purpose restrictions, official secrecy and data protection remain. Describing the use as "under conditions" does not mean sender permission is still required for this European exception.

### Austrian implementation boundary

The current EU-AHG § 15 expressly includes AML/CFT, but does not itself expressly reproduce the Article 215 sanctions exception. The enacted BGBl I 96/2025 PDF, printed page 26, Article 3 number 5, moves from a) to c), as does its HTML; this is not merely a display omission. Paragraphs 3 and 3a of § 15 retain ordinary consent/listed-purpose mechanisms.

Whether SanktG 2024 §§ 12(6), 14 or another national basis independently resolves this particular use of DAC-origin information was not conclusively established. Do not infer unlawful transposition, a blanket prohibition, unrestricted Austrian access or actual queries from this comparison. The EU-level permission and the complete national authorization are different questions. See the [primary-text comparison and limits](../../raw/articles/2026-09-30-at-euahg-purpose-implementation.md).

## Observations supported by the sources

- DAC2–DAC9 amend the same 2011 framework and use its exchange channels, confidentiality rule and purpose clause; the predecessor, Savings Directive and contextual OECD/US instruments are not amendments to it.
- The reporting party expanded from authorities (1977, DAC1) to private entities under several later regimes. Due diligence identifies who is reportable and can reach beyond reportable customers, but regime-specific exceptions remain. DAC2 Annex I Section V.A and GMSG § 34, for example, allow certain existing entity accounts up to USD 250,000 to remain unreviewed, unidentified and unreported unless the institution elects otherwise, until the threshold is exceeded. Independent AML/KYC duties are not the DAC2 reporting rule.
- DAC2 to DAC9 track OECD or G20 standards (CRS, BEPS Actions 5, 12 and 13, platform model rules, CARF, Pillar Two). The OECD 2026 platform-rules consultation proposes the same threshold change as the recast.
- Common public triggers (LuxLeaks for DAC3, Panama Papers for DAC5) are not named in those instruments' recitals; the Panama Papers are named in the DAC6 proposal.
- The recast shifts emphasis to simplification but widens tax access to AML and pension registers; the EDPS (Opinion 20/2026) asked for purpose limitation, narrower access and necessity-bound retention.

## Wording boundaries for public history pages

| Avoid | Source-backed distinction |
|---|---|
| Annual reporting about every customer | Reportable accounts/users are a subset defined by each regime; review and reporting are separate. |
| DAC3 covers only binding rulings, unrestricted five-year look-back | Qualifying instruments can be binding or non-binding; historical validity and turnover qualifications apply. |
| DAC6 abolished lawyers' notification duties; the rest is universally valid | The non-client-intermediary notification holding and the examined 2024 questions delimit the judgments. |
| DAC7 exempts fewer than 30 physical items | Fewer than 30 goods-sale activities facilitated by the platform, and total consideration ≤ EUR 2,000 in the reporting period. |
| DAC9 requires every group to file centrally on 30 June 2026 | Conditional central filing; calendar-year 2024 first returns due by that date. |
| CARF countries signed the exchange agreement in 2023 | The 2023 OECD package published the agreement text; signatures/activation are separate events. |
