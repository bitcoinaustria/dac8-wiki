# International AML, CDD and Crypto-Regime Genealogy

## Core finding

Two distinct lineages operate around the same customer and transaction data:

1. **AML/CFT:** UN treaty duties and post-1988 bank-supervision initiatives influenced FATF standards and EU AML legislation; EU and Austrian law create enforceable duties, while FATF evaluation and listing operate as a parallel pressure mechanism.
2. **Tax transparency:** OECD/G20 automatic-exchange standards and CARF → DAC8 → the Austrian Krypto-MPfG.

CARF and DAC8 are not FATF AML instruments and have a primarily OECD/G20 tax-transparency lineage. They expressly reuse AML CDD and beneficial-owner concepts, but that overlap does not merge their legal purpose or authority.

## Evidence and legal-effect classes

| Class | Instrument | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| binding legal instrument | convention in force for a state, EU secondary-law act, Austrian statute | creates state or private duties according to its legal form, domestic applicability and implementation |
| official non-binding standard | Basel statement, FATF Recommendation, OECD CARF | supplies policy and supervisory standards; not itself a directly enforceable Austrian customer duty |
| implementation assessment | FATF mutual evaluation, targeted update, national risk assessment | evaluates technical compliance and selected operational outcomes; not a causal impact study |
| empirical or strategic inference | market pressure, de-risking, cost and crime effects | requires independent evidence and cannot be inferred from adoption alone |

## Chronology

| Date | Development | Decision-relevant contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1988 | Vienna Convention | treaty duties to criminalise laundering and confiscate drug proceeds; no complete modern KYC system |
| 1988 | Basel statement | non-legal bank-supervision principles on customer identification, suspicious business and cooperation |
| 1989–1990 | G7 creation of FATF and first Forty Recommendations | customer identification, inquiry into the person on whose behalf a customer acted, five-year records, unusual-transaction review and an early permitted-or-required reporting framework |
| 1991 | First EU AML Directive | required Member States to enact customer-identification, record and reporting duties reflecting Vienna and FATF concepts |
| 1999–2001 | Terrorist-financing convention, Palermo Convention and FATF mandate expansion | broadened the regime beyond drug proceeds and added CFT, FIU and comprehensive preventive-system elements |
| 2001–2012 | Basel CDD, FATF and EU revisions | developed KYC/CDD, beneficial ownership, PEPs, ongoing monitoring and the risk-based approach |
| 2018 | AMLD5 | brought limited fiat/crypto exchange and custody activity into EU AML law before FATF's final 2019 virtual-asset standard |
| 2018–2019 | FATF Recommendation 15 and Interpretive Note | extended licensing, supervision, CDD, STR and existing Recommendation 16 transfer-information standards to VAs/VASPs |
| 2022 | OECD CARF | established a primarily tax-residence, reporting and automatic-exchange standard that also reuses AML CDD concepts |
| 2023–2024 | TFR and DAC8 | TFR recital 6 expressly translates FATF's 2019 VA/VASP framework into EU transfer law; DAC8 adapts CARF with EU-specific tax-transparency provisions |
| 2024–2027 | EU AML package | moves toward a directly applicable AMLR rulebook, AMLA and recast national AML infrastructure |

## Exact terms

- **KYC** is an operational and prudential umbrella term. Basel used it more broadly than AML alone, including legal, reputational, concentration and operational risk.
- **CDD** is the more exact FATF and EU term: identify and verify the customer, identify the beneficial owner, understand the purpose and nature of the relationship and conduct ongoing monitoring.
- **Travel Rule** means secure originator and beneficiary information transmitted in connection with a covered transfer under FATF Recommendation 16; TFR contains the applicable EU requirements for information accompanying a transfer. It is not a synonym for full CDD and does not require on-chain attachment.
- **CARF/DAC8 due diligence** determines tax residence and reportability. Use of existing AML information does not change its tax purpose.

## How FATF becomes practically consequential

FATF Recommendations are not a treaty and FATF does not itself fine an Austrian customer or revoke a CASP licence. Two paths should be separated:

- `FATF standard → political implementation → EU or national law → enforceable institutional or customer duty`
- `implementation → mutual evaluation and follow-up → if strategic deficiencies persist, possible action plan or listing → market and supervisory pressure`

Grey-listing means increased monitoring and an agreed action plan. FATF expressly does not call for blanket enhanced due diligence or de-risking against an entire grey-listed jurisdiction. Market participants may nevertheless reduce exposure; the IMF working-paper evidence shows potentially large capital-flow effects but does not convert listing into a legal sanction.

## Crypto-specific transmission

The crypto path is not a single FATF command:

- AMLD5 created an initial EU exchange-and-custody perimeter before FATF completed its 2019 VA/VASP revision.
- FATF then required jurisdictions to apply a broader global standard including Recommendation 16 information to covered VASPs.
- TFR recital 6 provides the clearest express EU adoption of the June 2019 Recommendations 15 and 16 framework.
- TFR is directly applicable EU law. FATF's standard is explanatory history and international context, not the immediate legal basis for a provider's EU duty.
- CARF/DAC8 remains a parallel tax-reporting regime and should be analysed separately when testing necessity, data fields, retention and remedies.

## Claims to avoid

- “The UN ordered FATF, which ordered the EU.”
- “FATF Recommendations are directly binding international law for customers.”
- “The Travel Rule was invented in 2019.”
- “The EU crypto AML perimeter began only after FATF 2019.”
- “CARF and DAC8 are FATF AML rules.”
- “Grey-listing legally requires blanket de-risking.”
- “Adoption or technical compliance proves effectiveness.”
