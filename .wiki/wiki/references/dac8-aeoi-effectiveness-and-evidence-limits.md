# DAC8, AEOI Effectiveness and Evidence Limits

> Modern evidence makes a general claim that automatic tax-information exchange is unproved or ineffective unsafe. The sustainable challenge is narrower: general utility does not establish the incremental necessity and proportionality of every DAC8 field, customer group, use, join and retention period.

## What now holds up

| Evidence | Defensible conclusion | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Commission DAC evaluation, 2018 to 2023 | DAC2 matching and recorded fiscal use materially improved; 18 Member States reported matching above 80 percent in 2023 | DAC8 outcomes, because no DAC8 annual report had yet been exchanged |
| Commission cost-benefit scenarios | Estimated annual benefits exceed recurring cost in each published scenario; the data-based scenario gives EUR 1.18 billion and a ratio of 1.82 | Ratios of 7.60 and 10.43 arise in the low-range and comprehensive scenarios, which add literature-based DAC4 benefit estimates; setup cost and rights externalities were excluded |
| Boas et al., Denmark | Strong administrative-data evidence of a large bank-AEOI compliance effect in one mature enforcement setting | Transferability to crypto or necessity of DAC8 units, counts and transfer fields |
| Baselgia, Switzerland | AEOI and the Swiss amnesty induced large and persistent disclosure effects | The separate contribution of each data field or a DAC8-like profile |
| Bénétrix, Emter and Schmitz | AEOI persistently reduced directly held household haven deposits | Complete effectiveness; displacement and more complex structures remained |
| Cong, Tang and Zhang | Expected information-transmission announcements were associated with exchange-level price responses; stronger sensitivity to local tax liabilities appeared on exchanges combining KYC and tax-authority reporting | Tax paid, lasting compliance, correct reports, a causal effect of KYC alone, short-run flows or field-level necessity |

## The Commission evaluation needs exact handling

SWD(2025) 365 reports that DAC2's aggregate unmatched rate fell from 15 to 8 percent across the relevant yearly assessments and that 18 Member States achieved matching above 80 percent in 2023. The older proposition that only a very small share could be matched must not be presented as current.

The evaluation's benefits remain methodologically layered. Member States do not all trace use to outcome, submitted data are not fully comparable or independently verifiable, and the EU-wide figures rely on extrapolation. The data-based cost-benefit scenario estimates EUR 1.18 billion benefits against EUR 650 million recurring cost, a ratio of 1.82. Ratios of 7.60 and 10.43 arise in the low-range and comprehensive scenarios, which add literature-based DAC4 benefit estimates. Both facts belong together.

The correct conclusion is neither “no benefit” nor “the headline proves proportionality.” It is that general effectiveness has substantial support while the magnitude, allocation and marginal contribution of particular design choices remain contestable.

## Litigation calibration

The empirical case changes the argumentative burden:

1. Concede the legitimate objective and credible general usefulness of automatic exchange.
2. Define the contested increment: for example, units, transaction counts, transfer type, the external-address aggregate, all-customer pre-processing or a ten-year Austrian record category.
3. Measure added tax-detection performance with and without that increment on the same test data.
4. Measure added inference, error, security and remedy cost.
5. Compare a concrete narrower design for equal effectiveness rather than pointing only to absent public studies.

This aligns with [[dac8-field-necessity-and-alternatives|DAC8 Field Necessity and Less-Intrusive Alternatives]] ([DAC8 Field Necessity and Less-Intrusive Alternatives](dac8-field-necessity-and-alternatives.md)) and avoids the overclaim identified in [[dac8-bulk-data-case-transfer-matrix|DAC8 Bulk-Data Case Transfer Matrix]] ([DAC8 Bulk-Data Case Transfer Matrix](dac8-bulk-data-case-transfer-matrix.md)).

## Strong adverse arguments to pre-answer

- DAC8 selected an aggregate middle position instead of transaction-by-transaction reporting.
- AEOI can deter non-compliance before authorities use a single report.
- Uniform cross-border coverage can reduce displacement and regulatory arbitrage.
- Units and counts can reveal implausible valuation or fragmented activity.
- Crypto-specific evidence associates expected reporting with market-price responses; the observed tax sensitivity was stronger on exchanges that combined KYC with information transmission.

The answer is not categorical denial. It is to demand proof of the contested increment and to demonstrate a less intrusive design that preserves the credible core benefit.

## A new narrower comparator

DAC8 itself already provides a conditional Identification Service route: where the competent authority uses such a service and a provider relies on its direct confirmation, the provider reports a reduced identity tuple. Recital 34 nevertheless states that use of the service does not affect due-diligence procedures or information-collection requirements. The pending 2026 DAC recast proposal preserves DAC8's transaction categories and develops validated-TIN mechanisms; it is a design comparator for further identification simplification, not current-law relief from transaction fields or provider-side collection.

## Claims to retire

- “Existing automatic exchange has never demonstrated tax effectiveness.”
- “The old two-percent TIN statistic is the current matching rate.”
- “The Commission proved an eight-to-ten benefit-cost ratio from observed data alone.”
- “Crypto market reactions prove additional tax collection.”
- “General AEOI benefits establish the need for every DAC8 field.”
