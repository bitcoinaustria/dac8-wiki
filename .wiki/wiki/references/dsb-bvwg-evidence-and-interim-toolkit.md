# DSB and BVwG Evidence and Interim-Protection Toolkit for Krypto-MPfG

> The DSB route can produce evidence and concrete relief before constitutional review. It is not automatically fast enough to stop the first 2027 report, and every preventive request depends on identified ongoing processing and claimant-specific urgency.

## 1. Identify the respondent before pleading the merits

The complaint must name the legal person responsible for the challenged processing, not a brand, platform or informal group label.

| Question | Evidence | Procedural consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Which entity contracts with the user? | Terms, account statement, invoice, imprint | Starting point, not conclusive controller proof |
| Which entity is named as GDPR controller? | Privacy notice and Article 15 response | Primary complaint respondent for provider processing |
| Which entity is the reporting provider under section 15 Krypto-MPfG? | Regulatory status, report notice, provider response | Connects the processing to the statutory reporting duty |
| Do group companies or processors determine essential means or purposes? | Controller arrangement, processing records, contracts, system ownership | May establish separate or joint responsibility under C-683/21 |
| What legal basis supports each challenged operation? | Krypto-MPfG nexus, privacy notice, processing record and provider response | Determines whether the Article 55(2) exception applies |
| Where is the controller's main establishment? | Corporate and decision-making evidence | May trigger the Articles 56 and 60 lead-authority process for operations outside Article 55(2) |

Article 55(2) applies to public authorities and to private bodies acting on the basis of Article 6(1)(c) or (e) GDPR. For provider processing carried out specifically to comply with Krypto-MPfG under Article 6(1)(c), Article 56's one-stop-shop mechanism does not apply. This does not make the Austrian DSB the deciding authority for every foreign provider. The file must first establish the controller, the sections 15 to 17 Krypto-MPfG nexus and any exemption, the exact statutory processing and the Member State concerned. A complaint may still be lodged with an authority identified in Article 77 even if another authority is competent to handle it.

Separate contractual, MiCA, TFR, AML or other provider operations require their own legal-basis and competence analysis. For those operations, the controller's main establishment may remain relevant under Articles 56 and 60.

Provider processing and later financial-authority processing are separate stages. A complaint against one controller should not silently assume responsibility for every downstream copy.

## 2. Anchor the complaint in existing processing

DSB 2023-0.632.875 makes a complaint about merely possible future processing vulnerable. The file should identify an act already performed or continuing, such as:

- collection or verification of tax residence and tax identifiers;
- classification as reportable or non-reportable;
- extraction, valuation or aggregation of transaction data;
- creation or retention of a section 18 annual profile;
- combination with provider-side MiCA, TFR or AML records;
- refusal or incomplete response to access, correction, restriction or deletion;
- a notice showing a defined forthcoming transfer of an already prepared record.

The statutory 2027 reporting deadline can establish temporal relevance. It does not replace proof of the challenged current processing.

## 3. Distinguish three EU-law operations

| Operation | Institution | Legal effect |
|---|---|---|
| Interpret national law consistently with EU law | DSB and BVwG within their jurisdiction | Can narrow an ambiguous provision but cannot rewrite clear national text contra legem |
| Disapply conflicting national law in the concrete case | DSB or BVwG where a directly effective EU rule controls | C-378/17 requires effective primacy within institutional competence; no general annulment |
| Declare an EU act invalid | CJEU after an Article 267 reference | Neither DSB nor a national court may independently invalidate DAC8 |

C-573/17 is the horizontal limit. Against a private provider, DAC8 cannot by itself impose a new burden on that provider or supply the sole ground for setting national law aside. The operative standard should be directly applicable GDPR or primary law. DAC8 remains relevant to show what Austria was and was not required to enact.

## 4. Evidence the DSB can obtain

| Issue | Likely holder | Target material | Disclosure boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact data and derivation | Provider | Field catalogue, valuation and classification rules, aggregation jobs, report preview | Article 15 gives personal data and an intelligible copy, not every internal document |
| Controller and processor roles | Provider/group | Article 26 arrangements, processor contracts, responsible decision-makers | Commercial details may be protected, but status cannot rest only on labels |
| Processing purposes and systems | Provider/authority | Article 30 record, data-flow map, system inventory | Article 30(4) makes the record available to the DSB, not automatically in full to the complainant |
| Necessity, risks and controls | Provider/authority | DPIA if required, risk assessment, access model, audit findings, incident response | Secrets and security interests may limit party disclosure; DSB and court can still review |
| Reportability and all-customer profile | Provider | Decision rule, intermediate tables, materialisation timing, non-reportable-user deletion | Must separate temporary operational data from a durable Krypto-MPfG dataset |
| Downstream correction | Provider/BMF/recipient | Original identifier, correction/deletion message, recipient acknowledgement | Access restrictions require a specific lawful and proportionate basis |
| Actual access and reuse | Authority | consultation logs, user roles, purpose code, onward recipient | Employee identity is not automatic; dates and purposes can remain accessible |

Sections 37, 39, 45 and 46 AVG support official investigation and broad suitable evidence. Article 5(2) GDPR places the compliance demonstration burden on the controller, but the complainant must still identify the challenged processing and enough facts to make the inquiry concrete.

## 5. Party access is not identical to authority access

Section 17 AVG provides file access subject to protected interests of other parties and third persons, authority tasks and procedural purpose. If the DSB obtains a DPIA, processing record or security evidence, access can be requested, but unfiltered disclosure is not guaranteed.

C-203/22 prevents trade secrecy from becoming an evidence-free zone. Confidential material can be reviewed by the DSB and court and balanced against data-subject rights even where the complainant receives only a restricted or reasoned form of access.

## 6. DPIA is an evidence path, not an automatic invalidity theory

Article 35(7) makes a DPIA useful because it should describe the processing, necessity, proportionality, risk and safeguards. The Austrian required list may capture certain high-risk automation, new technology or data combination. The Austrian exemption list includes customer accounting and public tax administration categories that a provider or authority may invoke.

The correct questions are:

1. What is the actual Krypto-MPfG processing operation?
2. Does it remain within the relied-on exemption?
3. Was it materially changed by aggregation, cross-purpose linking, systematic evaluation or long retention?
4. If a DPIA exists, does it address the real data layers and claimant-specific risks?
5. If no DPIA exists, what alternative risk and necessity assessment supports the processing?

C-60/22 and C-61/22 are adverse limits. A documentation defect does not automatically make the processing unlawful, and an alleged lawmaking DPIA defect does not automatically invalidate the statute.

Article 35(10) is conditional. If the State or controller relies on a lawmaking assessment, identify the assessment and map it to Article 35(7)'s processing, necessity, proportionality, risk and safeguard elements. The public Austrian WFA does not visibly contain that structure, but public silence does not prove no separate assessment exists. DSFA-A17 is a substantial authority-side defence for public tax processing; provider reliance on DSFA-A01 or A06 likewise requires operation-specific testing.

## 7. Interim-effect matrix

| Measure | Threshold | Immediate effect | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| DSB mandate prohibition, sections 22(4) and 25(1) DSG and section 57 AVG | Credible substantial impairment, a rationally grounded suspicion of unlawful processing and a claimant-specific substantial immediate danger to protected confidentiality interests, meaning danger in delay | Immediate prohibition; objection generally does not suspend a non-monetary order | Investigation must begin within two weeks; general fear and hypothetical future events are insufficient |
| Ordinary DSB corrective order under Article 58(2) | Established infringement; measure appropriate, necessary and proportionate | May limit or prohibit processing | Timely BVwG complaint generally has suspensive effect |
| Exclusion of suspensive effect, section 13(2) VwGVG | Interest balance plus danger in delay and urgent need for early execution | Allows ordinary order to operate pending review | Requires separate reasons; not automatic |
| Appeal against DSB refusal | Valid challenge to refusal | Preserves review of the refusal | Suspensive effect does not create the prohibition that was refused |
| Article 66 GDPR provisional measure | Urgent need to act to protect rights in cross-border processing | Territorial measure for up to three months | Exceptional route; does not replace lead-authority procedure |
| Section 85 VfGG suspensive effect | No compelling public-interest bar and a favourable balance of interests | Suspends the legal effect of the challenged BVwG judgment | Against a refusal, it does not create the refused processing prohibition |

The strongest interim application states the exact processing step to be paused, the date by which it will occur, why later correction cannot adequately address the claimed harm, and why a narrower restriction would not suffice.

## 8. Remedy menu

| Established defect | Narrow remedy to request | Wider legal consequence that remains separate |
|---|---|---|
| Inaccurate field or classification | Correct source and planned report; restrict pending correction; propagate correction to recipients | Does not invalidate the reporting system |
| Section 18 profile for a non-reportable user lacks necessity | Stop materialisation, delete or shorten retention of the identified profile | May support constitutional review of section 20(1) if the defect is statutory rather than implementation-specific |
| Unlawful or unsupported data join | Cease the join; delete derived record; restrict access; document lawful purpose | Does not prohibit every tax/AML use expressly authorised by law |
| Inadequate access or recipient disclosure | Provide intelligible data, purposes, periods, recipients and consultation information subject to lawful limits | Does not confer access to every secret or every employee identity |
| Inadequate security measure | Order specified risk-appropriate remediation or narrowly limit processing until remediated | A breach or abstract threat alone does not prove inadequacy |
| Direct conflict between national processing duty and directly effective EU law | Disapply the national rule in the concrete case | General removal of national text still requires constitutional or legislative action |
| Validity doubt about DAC8 | BVwG or another competent court refers under Article 267 | Only the CJEU may invalidate the Directive |
| Constitutional doubt about Krypto-MPfG | BVwG applies to the VfGH under Article 140 B-VG | Depends on the provision being prejudicial and the application being properly framed |

Section 24(2)(5) DSG requires the complaint to request a finding of the alleged infringement. If a successful complaint is attributable to a private controller, section 24(5) requires an order to comply with the complainant's underlying request for access, rectification, erasure, restriction or portability to the extent necessary to eliminate the infringement.

C-768/21 does not give the complainant a right to a chosen fine, sanction or other Article 58(2) measure. It does require an appropriate, necessary and proportionate corrective response where needed to remedy the infringement and enforce the GDPR fully.

C-46/23 confirms that the DSB may order erasure of unlawfully processed data under Article 58(2)(d) and (g) even without a prior erasure request and irrespective of where the controller obtained the data. It remains a power directed at unlawful processing, not a way around a continuing valid statutory retention or reporting duty. Section 24(6) DSG also creates a tactical limit: later satisfaction of the individual request can make the complaint moot after the complainant is heard.

Where correction or erasure is legally required, C-129/21 Proximus supports appropriate technical and organisational steps to notify recipients under Articles 5(2), 19 and 24. The DAC8 correction and deletion message types make a downstream propagation request technically concrete. They do not eliminate a recipient's separate lawful basis.

Section 24(5) DSG's request-based order is limited to complaints against private-sector controllers. Against the BMF or another public controller, identify the appropriate GDPR Article 58(2) corrective power rather than silently importing the private-sector clause. Article 58(2)(g) expressly permits an order to communicate a correction, erasure or restriction to recipients under Article 19. Section 30(5) DSG removes fines against public authorities but not those corrective powers.

Under section 28 VwGVG, the BVwG should decide the merits where the relevant facts are fixed or merits adjudication serves speed or cost saving. A requested merits disposition, a remittal, an Article 140 application and an Article 267 reference must be framed separately because only the first can immediately replace the DSB disposition within the available remedy.

## 9. Time-critical file for the first reporting cycle

There is no reliable ordinary-path guarantee of a final VfGH or CJEU ruling before 31 July 2027. A useful sequence is:

1. identify the controller, reporting provider, legal basis and the competent-authority route under Article 55 or 56;
2. request exact current data, purposes, categories, recipients, periods and a copy under Article 15;
3. document existing classification, aggregation and storage rather than only the future report;
4. file the DSB complaint with complete facts, legal grounds, evidence requests and relief;
5. request sections 22(4) and 25(1) relief only with evidence of claimant-specific immediate danger;
6. if an ordinary prohibition is ordered, address section 13(2) VwGVG separately;
7. put all facts, legal grounds and evidence motions into the BVwG complaint because section 27 VwGVG defines the review frame;
8. present Article 140 and Article 267 questions as decision-relevant alternatives, not as automatic next stages.
9. define the requested operative order separately for the provider, the Austrian authority copy, actual recipients and any derived system.

The referring court may request expedited CJEU treatment under Article 105 of the Rules of Procedure. Exceptionally, the President of the Court may order it ex officio. The number of affected persons, general economic importance or a desire for speed is not enough by itself.
