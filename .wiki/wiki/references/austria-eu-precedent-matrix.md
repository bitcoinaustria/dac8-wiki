# Austria and EU Precedent Matrix for a DAC8 Challenge

## Austrian route and standing

| Authority | What it establishes | DAC8 effect |
|---|---|---|
| VfGH G 147/08 ua; ECtHR Tretter | Mere existence of data powers without current retention is insufficient; access, deletion, DSB, and court routes matter. | Adverse to an abstract pre-processing challenge. |
| VfGH G 59/2012 and G 62/70/71/2012, joined in G 47/2012 ua; CJEU C-293/12 and C-594/12 | Automatic provider-side retention can directly affect users; exceptional distributed and ephemeral facts can defeat other-remedy objections. G 47/12 itself was Kärnten's abstract application. | Supportive only if comparable exceptional facts are proved; cite the individual dockets for standing. |
| VfGH G 62/2012 ua, 10 June 2014 | Each applicant must plead current/direct effect individually. | Strong warning against mass-form litigation. |
| VfGH G 385/2018; G 178/2024 | DSB → BVwG → Article 144 is a reasonable alternative for statutory data processing; poor first-instance prospects do not change that. | Strongly adverse to a primary direct Article 140 filing. |
| VfGH G 287/2022 ua | Individual DSB complaints led to BVwG Article 140 applications and successful annulment of section 9(1) DSG. | Direct proof that the alternative route can deliver norm review and annulment. |
| VfGH G 37-38/2024 | Civil proceedings are generally reasonable and may lead to court-initiated review or a party application after a first-instance merits judgment. | Independent alternative; ordinary cost and poor prospects do not restore direct standing. |
| Krypto-MPfG sections 2(2) and 19(2); BAO section 243 | The BAO applies mutatis mutandis and the provider report is an `Abgabenerklärung`, but a BAO complaint under section 243 requires a tax-authority Bescheid. | No ordinary pre-report BFG route for the private user has been identified; do not assume one without a concrete user-facing Bescheid and party status. |
| VfGH G 34/2022; G 231/2021 ua | Section 20a VfGG gives no applicant right to interim relief; an EO interim proceeding cannot itself support a party application. | Direct filing has no unique interim advantage; urgent and merits routes must be separated. |
| VwGH Ro 2023/04/0028; Ra 2023/04/0076; DSB 2024-0.215.259 | The one-year knowledge-based DSB limit is effective; immediate mandate prohibitions exist but require concrete weighty illegality and claimant-specific imminent danger. | File early; urgent relief is real, fact-heavy and revisable. General future fears do not suffice. |
| VwGH 2010/07/0171; Ro 2025/04/0021 | An unprovided declaratory order must be necessary and suitable to protect a subjective right prospectively; changed law and concrete punishment risk supported a separate interest in 2013. | No established user route, but potentially important counterauthority for a sanctioned CASP that proves competence, an unresolved duty and current risk. |
| VfGH V 532/2020 | Ordinary/civil/data-protection routes displace direct review absent retention-style extraordinary circumstances. | Adverse. |
| VfGH G 105/2025 | Material pre-deadline electronic and administrative preparations for a penalised data-supply duty establish current/direct business effect; the broad monitoring system nevertheless survived on the merits. | Strong CASP standing analogue and strong merits warning. |
| VfGH G 606/2015 ua | Costly technical tax-compliance preparations support direct standing without provoking a penalty; mass-tax recordkeeping and relative small-business cost were upheld. | Strong CASP standing analogue and adverse Article 6 StGG authority. |
| VfGH G 37/02 ua, V 42/02 ua | Direct technical duties and large implementation costs supported standing; the uncompensated cost rule fell while the facilities duty survived. | Strongest successful CASP analogue, but no authority for striking the reporting duty merely because compliance is costly. |
| VfGH G 118/2015 ua; G 100/05 ua; G 20/2015 ua | Pre-commencement business preparation, mandatory infrastructure, and direct penalised duties can establish current effect; actually regulated companies have stronger standing than associations. | Strong successful standing cluster for a provider-specific, invoice-backed filing. |
| VfGH G 52/2024 | A rule formally addressed to doctors directly affected a patient's contractual and private-life sphere; medical and sworn evidence established a real intended transaction. | Strong method for an indirectly affected user, subject to the separate DSB-route problem. |
| BVwG W258 2224229-1, PNR | DSB and BVwG can supply an effective domestic vehicle informed by later CJEU authority. | Positive procedural analogue. |
| VfGH E 2097/2021; VwGH Ro 2023/04/0021 | DAC2 reporting survived privacy, threshold, security, and non-referral attacks. | Closest and strongest adverse merits authority. |
| VfGH G 72-74/2019 ua | Suspicionless, broad data processing and intrusive surveillance can fail proportionality. | Helpful merits method, but it was legislator-initiated abstract review. |

## EU procedural law

| Authority | Rule | DAC8 use |
|---|---|---|
| Foto-Frost, 314/85 | A national court may reject invalidity but cannot itself invalidate an EU act. | A genuine DAC8 validity doubt must go to the CJEU. |
| Unibet, C-432/05 | EU law does not require a standalone action where incidental remedies are effective. | Supports using DSB/BVwG rather than demanding direct review. |
| État luxembourgeois, C-245/19 and C-246/19 | Article 47 can be satisfied by effective incidental review in tax-information cases. | Adverse to a claimed right to pre-exchange direct action. |
| C-414/24, Datenschutzbehörde | The judgment itself restates that Article 77 supervisory and Article 79 judicial remedies are concurrent and independent. It describes a national stay mechanism as a permissible coordination option, not as an already established Austrian DSB power. | Directly contradicts private-remedy exclusivity without deciding the availability or result of a concrete Austrian civil action. |
| C-655/23, Quirin Privatbank | The GDPR does not itself require a special free-standing preventive injunction independent of erasure. | Limits the civil route without making Article 140 exclusive. |
| ABNA, C-453/03 ua; Petlichev, C-56/25 | Ordinary national courts can carry directive-validity references and retain Article 267 powers despite constitutional review. | Direct counterexamples to a VfGH monopoly. |
| Atlanta, C-465/93 | Interim relief based on EU-act invalidity requires serious doubts, a reference, urgency, serious irreparable harm, Union interest and consistency. | Non-VfGH urgent relief exists, but the threshold is high. |
| Consorzio, C-561/19; KUBERA, C-144/23 | A last-instance court must analyze a properly raised EU question and may decline only under the limited relevance/settled/acte-clair exceptions, with reasons. | Improves accountability, but does not let the claimant compel referral. |
| Article 263(4) and (6) TFEU | A private direct action requires addressed or direct and individual concern, or a directly concerning regulatory act without implementing measures, and must be filed within two months. | No current user route against DAC8 or Austrian implementation; Article 267 remains the realistic validity vehicle. |
| Brasserie/Factortame; VfGH A 2/2022 | EU state liability requires a rights-conferring rule, a sufficiently serious breach, loss and causation; Austrian forum depends on whether wrong is legislative/supreme-court or execution-based. | Ex-post damages fallback, not a reporting stop or abstract validity action. |
| Star Fruit, 247/87; Commission Notice 2017/C 18/02 | Anyone may complain free of charge, but the Commission retains discretion whether to pursue Article 258. | Useful systemic pressure only; no individual remedy or suspension. |

## EU and ECHR merits

| Weight | Authorities | Relevance and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Helpful, distinguishable | Digital Rights Ireland; Tele2/Watson; La Quadrature C-511/18 ua; Commissioner of An Garda Síochána; SpaceNet | Strong strict-necessity, targeting, retention, and access-review principles, but the cases concern confidential traffic/location data and law enforcement. |
| Mixed | Ligue des droits humains, C-817/19; Opinion 1/15; Valsts ieņēmumu dienests, C-175/20 | Rigorous review of systematic processing, automated criteria, purpose limitation, minimization, time limits, and independent review; often preserves the scheme by strict interpretation. |
| Tax-cooperation adverse | Belgian Association of Tax Lawyers, C-623/22; G.S.B. v Switzerland; Othymia Investments | DAC6 reporting and targeted tax-information transmissions can be lawful when fields, purpose, access, safeguards and remedies are bounded. G.S.B. and Othymia did not decide periodic automatic exchange. |
| Effective-remedy mixed | Ferrieri and Bonassisa v Italy; M.N. and Others v San Marino; Tretter and Others v Austria | Review must be timely, binding and real; merely theoretical or years-late routes fail. Tretter separately defeats abstract victim status where no personal risk is shown and available mechanisms are unused. |
| Narrow invalidity analogies | Schecke, C-92/09 and C-93/09; Luxembourg Business Registers, C-37/20 and C-601/20 | Indiscriminate public disclosure failed proportionality. DAC8 exchange is not public, so the analogy is limited. |
| Remedy/security support | Schrems I, C-362/14; Berlioz, C-682/15 | Effective independent control and review remain material when personal data cross borders or support tax enforcement. |

## Bottom line

No identified precedent automatically invalidates DAC8. For the Austrian excess, the best case is narrower: section 20(1) creates and stores the full section 18 dataset for a person already shown not to be reportable, without a clear statutory deletion rule for never-transmitted raw data. For DAC8 validity, the best comparison remains the identity and home-address layer combined with per-asset annual categories, values, units, counts, transfer aggregates, long retention, onward authority access, and any proved public-ledger linkage. Both cases must confront DAC2, DAC6, and tax-exchange counterauthority rather than rely only on communications-retention cases.
