# DAC8 Equality and Technology-Neutrality Arguments

> Equality is a supporting line, not a substitute for the privacy and proportionality case. It becomes legally testable only through a narrow comparator tied to the purpose and consequence of a particular rule.

## Governing tests

Under VfGH G 356/02, equal facts generally require equal consequences and material factual differences may require differentiated rules. The relation between facts and legal consequence must rest on a rational ground. The review is deferential: G 174/2022 permits administrable typing, orientation to the usual case and some hardship; the Court does not select the optimal policy.

Under Charter Article 20, comparable situations must not be treated differently and different situations must not be treated alike without objective, reasonable and proportionate justification. Glatzel confirms that Article 20 is the general equality guarantee; Article 21 concerns specific discrimination grounds. Arcelor requires a concrete comparison by subject matter and purpose, but affords the EU legislature wide discretion for complex economic regulation and stepwise implementation.

## Ranked comparisons

| Comparison | Proposition that can be tested | Principal limit or adverse authority |
|---|---|---|
| Krypto-MPfG ten years for both the transmitted-information copy and compliance records versus GMSG five and seven years | Why does the Austrian crypto-reporting layer require ten years for both categories when the adjacent financial-account regime differentiates them and described five years as appropriate for privacy reasons? | DAC8 permits five to ten years for compliance records. Different data, audit needs and limitation periods may supply a rational ground. The comparison does not govern the BMF copy. |
| Reportable versus definitively non-reportable crypto user under section 20(1) | A full and persistent section 18 activity profile may be excessive after non-reportability is settled. | GMSG section 5(1) also processes reportable information for all account holders. The claim needs provider evidence showing an incremental, persistent crypto activity profile rather than ordinary due diligence. |
| A particular DAC8 field versus the corresponding DAC2 field set | Counts, units, transfer direction and the external-address knowledge category require a field-specific explanation beyond account, balance, income and gross-proceeds reporting. | E 2097/2021 upheld DAC2, and DAC8's impact assessment explains why transaction-derived aggregates were chosen. No general comparison of all crypto and bank data is sound. |
| Economically and tax-equivalent tokenised versus conventional product | If legal rights, tax treatment, risk and intermediation are materially the same, technology alone may not justify an additional field or weaker safeguard. | A concrete product pair is required. The legal classification, transfer method and visibility gap may be material differences. |
| Same payment function through crypto versus card or SEPA | Can expose a technology-specific burden where the underlying economic and tax facts are genuinely identical. | Often weak: use of a crypto-asset can itself be a taxable disposal and the provider may have different knowledge. VfGH G 606/2015 accepted equal cash/card obligations despite existing electronic traces. |

## Ten years versus five years

This is the cleanest enacted Austrian comparator. Section 5(3) GMSG requires provider deletion of transmitted information after five years, a period described in the 2018 materials as appropriate for data-protection reasons. Section 7a GMSG uses seven years for compliance records. Krypto-MPfG sections 20(4) and 39 use ten years for both layers. The correct claim is not that this difference proves invalidity. It is that a maximum-period choice needs a record-layer-specific rational ground when an adjacent automatic tax-reporting regime uses a differentiated 5/7 structure.

The factual inquiry must identify the exact records, applicable tax limitation and correction periods, frequency of later use, open-proceeding exceptions, backups and the consequence of earlier deletion. The authority-copy retention basis must be analysed separately because section 20(4) addresses the provider.

## Technology neutrality

MiCA recital 9 describes “same activities, same risks, same rules” and technology neutrality as principles guiding EU financial-services legislation. It also keeps instruments already regulated by financial law in the existing regime regardless of issuance or transfer technology.

That recital is neither a standalone fundamental right nor an automatic rule for tax cooperation. Its defensible use is narrower: for a concrete pair of economically and tax-equivalent activities, identify the DLT-specific fact that rationally justifies each additional reporting field or different safeguard. The principle also supports regulation where DLT creates a materially different visibility, intermediation or enforcement risk.

## Arguments not established

- crypto users are a suspect class or “wealth” discrimination category under Charter Article 21;
- all crypto products are comparable to all bank or securities products;
- a different treatment is unlawful because a less burdensome design exists;
- all-customer processing is unique to DAC8;
- technology neutrality prohibits DLT-specific legislation;
- the five-year GMSG period automatically invalidates the ten-year Krypto-MPfG period.

## Evidence gates

1. Fix the claimant's nationality, tax residence, reportability and relevant legal status before selecting the Austrian equality guarantee.
2. Identify a concrete comparator pair by economic function, tax treatment, risk, provider knowledge and data purpose.
3. Compare the exact field, record layer, retention actor and safeguard rather than the regimes as a whole.
4. Obtain the provider's real implementation for reportable and non-reportable users.
5. Test the stated justification against tax timelines, use statistics and technically workable narrower choices.
