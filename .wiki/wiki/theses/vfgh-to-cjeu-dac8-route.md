# VfGH-to-CJEU DAC8 Route

> Revised verdict: there is no single optimal claimant. A user should mirror DAC2—DSB → BVwG → Article 267/140 review—and may separately assess an Article 79 GDPR action, while an Austria-home CASP may have strong direct Article 140 standing based on current statutory and implementation burdens.

## Coordinated two-track route

### Track A: provider

1. Use an Austria-home CASP whose section 15 status is undisputed and whose section 20 duties operate without an intervening decision.
2. Preserve exact completed or contracted data-pipeline, aggregation, storage, security, DPIA, and staffing work.
3. Separate the unavoidable DAC8/MiCA/AML baseline from the cost of processing confirmed non-reportable users under section 20(1).
4. Bring a precise Article 140 application centred on Article 6 StGG and section 1 DSG, subject to checking whether a genuine ordinary determination route exists.
5. Request a narrow Article 267 interpretation reference if the result depends on whether DAC8 permits or requires the Austrian universal-processing addition.

G 105/2025 and G 606/2015 show that material pre-compliance technical and administrative costs can create current/direct effect and that a business need not provoke a penalty. They are also adverse merits authorities: broad data and tax-compliance systems survived. Standing is not success.

G 37/02 ua, G 118/2015 ua, G 100/05 ua, and G 20/2015 ua strengthen this standing method with successful direct business applications. Their limits matter: the VfGH did not hold that every expensive compliance regime is disproportionate, and G 37/02 preserved the underlying surveillance-facilities duty while removing the uncompensated cost allocation.

A provider-specific administrative declaratory route must also be checked rather than assumed away. VwGH 2010/07/0171 accepted an unwritten declaratory order where changed law, future conduct and a concrete punishment risk created a separate need for legal protection; Ro 2025/04/0021 restates that the order must actually remove a present or future threat to a subjective right. This may fit a sanctioned CASP better than a user, but only if a competent authority and an unresolved concrete duty can be identified. It is not an established Krypto-MPfG procedure.

### Track B: user

1. Select an actual user of an Austrian-covered CASP with post-1 January 2026 processing.
2. Use a genuinely non-reportable user for the section 20(1) national-excess case, or a reportable Austrian user for a broader DAC8 validity case. As at 10 September 2026 the OECD activation table shows Austria with only eleven activated third-country CARF relationships — Guernsey, Iceland, Indonesia, Japan, Jersey, Korea, Liechtenstein, New Zealand, Norway, South Africa and the United Kingdom — so residence outside those states is the operative test for non-reportability, not the CARF signatory list. See [[non-eu-tax-resident-claimant-advantage|Drittstaaten-Steueransässigkeit als Klägerprofil]].
3. Make legally matched Article 15 and, where applicable, Article 17 or 18 requests to establish fields, purposes, recipients, retention, deletion, and any refusal. Do not invoke Article 21 mechanically where the CASP relies on a legal obligation under Article 6(1)(c).
4. File a precise section 24 DSG complaint against concrete processing by the identified controller, not a generalized policy objection. Observe the one-year knowledge and three-year long-stop periods. VwGH Ro 2023/04/0028 confirms that the one-year subjective limit is compatible with the GDPR; do not assume that continuing storage restarts it.
5. Appeal a DSB decision to the BVwG within four weeks. The BVwG—not only the VfGH—is already able to make an Article 267 reference and can apply to the VfGH under Article 140 where it has constitutional concerns about a prejudicial national provision. The Article 140 branch depends on the provision being national rather than unionsrechtlich determiniert. For section 20(1) that is now evidenced rather than inferred: DAC8 Annex VI Section V(A) to (F) contains no all-customers processing duty, the explanatory materials assign section 20 to Article 25 DAC and cite no directive source for Abs. 1 while citing one for Abs. 3 and Abs. 4, and Germany transposed the same annex without an equivalent clause.
6. Continue through Article 144 VfGH review within six weeks of the BVwG decision and separately assess VwGH review.

This is not theoretical. The Austrian DAC2 claimant used this chain through DSB, BVwG, VfGH, and VwGH. Austrian PNR litigation likewise shows a DSB/BVwG vehicle applying later CJEU authority. G 287/2022 ua supplies even more direct institutional proof: individual DSB complaints reached the BVwG, the BVwG filed Article 140 applications, and the VfGH admitted them and annulled section 9(1) DSG.

The DSB can itself provide concrete EU-law relief before judicial escalation. C-378/17 requires a national administrative body applying EU law to disapply conflicting national law within its competence. Against a private provider, C-573/17 means that the operative conflict should rest on directly applicable GDPR or primary law, not DAC8 alone. The result is non-application in the case, not general annulment of the Krypto-MPfG and not invalidation of DAC8.

C-26/22 and C-64/22 require diligent supervisory investigation and full judicial review of law and fact. This makes the DSB/BVwG route an evidence-producing merits path rather than only a procedural bridge to the VfGH or CJEU.

Nor is Article 267 reserved to constitutional courts. ABNA shows ordinary courts referring directive-validity questions and obtaining partial invalidation. CJEU C-56/25, Petlichev, confirms that a specialist court retains its Article 267 powers and EU-law duties despite available or parallel constitutional review and need not await constitutional annulment before disapplying conflicting national law. The indispensable gate is an admissible, decision-ready underlying case, not prior access to the VfGH.

The route can start before the first report. Section 20 processing has operated since 1 January 2026; the first provider report for 2026 is due by 31 July 2027. Before transmission, the CASP is normally the concrete respondent. Later BMF receipt, onward exchange, and foreign-authority processing are separate acts and may require separate cases.

### Independent judicial GDPR route

CJEU C-414/24, decided on an Austrian reference on 18 June 2026, confirms that the Article 77 supervisory complaint and Article 79 judicial remedy are concurrent and independent. The DSB cannot reject a complaint merely because the same non-final dispute is already before a court. An affected user can therefore separately assess a civil GDPR action against the controller or processor; that court may itself refer a decision-relevant EU-law question under Article 267 and, under Article 89(2) B-VG, must apply to the VfGH if it has constitutional concerns about a prejudicial Austrian statute. After a first-instance merits judgment, a party may combine its appeal with a party application under Article 140(1)(1)(d), as the VfGH explains in G 37-38/2024.

This is an additional legal vehicle, not a guaranteed injunction or a merits shortcut. CJEU C-655/23 holds that the GDPR does not itself require a special free-standing preventive injunction where erasure is not sought, although national law may provide one. The claim and requested relief must be grounded in the GDPR and Austrian procedural law, and the CASP will rely on its express Krypto-MPfG legal duty. These limits do not restore the claimed exclusivity of direct Article 140 review.

VfGH G 231/2021 ua adds a procedural separation: an enforcement-order interim proceeding cannot itself support a party application under Article 140(1)(1)(d). Any urgent injunction must therefore be distinguished from the civil merits case that may later produce a party application. No post-C-655/23 OGH merits decision resolving the previously suspended Austrian preventive-injunction cases was located as of 27 August 2026.

### Interim protection

A direct Article 140 application does not automatically suspend the challenged processing. Section 20a VfGG permits EU-law-required interim protection in a pending VfGH case, but G 34/2022 holds that the provision creates no applicant right; any such order is made ex officio.

The DSB route has its own urgent mechanism. Under section 25(1) DSG, a complainant who credibly shows a substantial impairment of protected confidentiality interests can trigger section 22(4), which permits an immediate section 57 AVG mandate decision prohibiting all or part of processing where a substantial immediate danger and danger in delay are present. DSB 2024-0.215.259 requires a rationally grounded suspicion of unlawful processing and a claimant-specific substantial immediate danger; general worries and hypothetical future events do not suffice. VwGH Ra 2023/04/0076 confirms real use of such a mandate prohibition, while also showing that it may be lifted after investigation. Civil interim relief may also be available under Austrian law. None of these measures is automatic, and any attempt to suspend the effect of an EU act faces the strict Unibet/Zuckerfabrik/Atlanta conditions. Direct VfGH review has no unique interim-protection advantage.

The procedural effect must be pleaded separately. An objection to a non-monetary section 57 mandate prohibition generally does not suspend it. An ordinary DSB prohibition can be suspended by a timely BVwG complaint unless suspensive effect is separately excluded under section 13(2) VwGVG. A complaint against refusal of an urgent measure does not itself create a stop. No ordinary route guarantees a final VfGH or CJEU decision before the first provider report on 31 July 2027.

## ECHR effective-remedy check

ECtHR Ferrieri and Bonassisa v Italy, final on 11 May 2026, requires compulsory tax access to financial data to be accompanied by timely, binding and practically effective independent review. A possible tax case only after an uncertain future assessment, a theoretically asserted civil route, and a non-binding guarantor were insufficient. The judgment does not require prior notice, a direct constitutional action, or automatic suspension; two judges dissented on domestic-remedy exhaustion and the bypassed possibility of an Article 267 reference.

Othymia Investments and G.S.B. are important but limited counterauthority. Both concerned targeted tax-information transmission, not periodic DAC8 automatic exchange. Othymia rejected a general prior-notice right and treated two-stage review as sufficient for the remaining Article 13 complaint; G.S.B. relied on the legal framework, safeguards and real review. Tretter rejected an abstract Austrian surveillance complaint for lack of victim status where no personal risk was shown and available information and review mechanisms had not been used; it left the separate exhaustion objection open. The ECHR question is therefore whether a concrete Austrian route is timely and real, not whether it is a direct Article 140 application.

## Accessory and ex-post routes

EU-law state liability is a damages backstop, not a pre-report challenge. Direct legislative or supreme-court wrong belongs under Article 137 B-VG; administrative and lower-court execution belongs to the ordinary AHG courts. Both require a rights-conferring EU rule, a sufficiently serious breach, loss and causation. The AHG supplies only money and penalizes failure to avoid damage through available remedies.

An Article 258 complaint is a free, low-cost parallel channel for a systemic Austrian transposition or remedy defect. Star Fruit confirms that the Commission cannot be compelled to proceed and supplies no individual suspension, annulment or damages. An ECtHR application is later still: personal victim status, domestic exhaustion and the four-month period are mandatory, while Rule 39 is exceptional. Neither route makes direct Article 140 review exclusive.

Article 263 direct review was also checked. The publication periods for DAC8 and the identified 2025 implementing act have elapsed, and ordinary user standing is not established. It is not a current substitute for the Article 267 validity route.

## Direct Article 140 fallback

The retention litigation proves a direct filing can lead to an Article 267 validity reference. But its admissibility depended on unknown/distributed providers, communication partners' providers, continuously regenerated records, and a six-month deletion horizon. In G 178/2024 the VfGH held that DSB → BVwG → Article 144 was reasonable for mandatory health-data processing even if first-instance success looked poor. The Court did not decide challenge perimeter, adequate pleading of the concerns, or whether the GDPR's direct applicability created another admissibility problem.

For a DAC8 user, a direct application should therefore be fallback only when concrete facts establish retention-style exceptional circumstances and show that DSB/BVwG protection cannot prevent a specific irreversible injury. Predicted DSB defeat, normal delay, ordinary cost, several known CASPs, or the DSB's inability to invalidate an EU act are insufficient. Every claimant and every challenged provision must be pleaded separately. The 11,129 rejected AKVorrat co-applicants show the cost of generic mass pleading.

Do not combine the user and CASP into one undifferentiated application. They rely on different rights, factual injuries, alternative routes, and challenge perimeters. Coordinated separate proceedings preserve each case's strongest standing theory.

## Candidate Article 267 issues

For the Austrian excess, the strongest interpretation question is:

> Do Article 8ad and Annex VI Sections II and III of Directive 2011/16/EU, as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/2226, read with Directive Article 25 and GDPR Articles 5(1)(b), (c), and (e), 6(3), and 23, permit or require national law to make a provider determine, collect, store, and process, for every customer regardless of reportable-user status, the transaction-derived inputs and annual aggregate information described in section 18 Krypto-MPfG?

This question targets section 20(1), not DAC8 as a whole. Universal self-certification can be separated from universal transaction-profile creation.

For a reportable-user validity case, the question remains narrower than “DAC8 is mass surveillance”:

> Are the identity-linked annual per-asset and per-category values, units, counts, retail-payment and transfer aggregates required by Directive (EU) 2023/2226 compatible with Charter Articles 7, 8, and 52(1), having regard to generalized coverage without individualized suspicion, pre-report processing, retention and access, effective remedies, and any proved ability to correlate the report with public-ledger data?

Each question must be necessary to decide its concrete proceeding. Austria's section 20(1) addition, its ten-year choice for transmitted information, and any national implementation defect must be separated from EU-act validity because national discretion cannot by itself invalidate DAC8.

## Claimant fit

- **Austria-home CASP:** strongest direct Article 140 candidate, but needs a provider-specific costed counterfactual.
- **Non-reportable user:** strongest national section 20(1) privacy claimant; citizenship is irrelevant, and non-reportability must be proved against the participating-state rules at the filing date.
- **Reportable Austrian user:** strongest vehicle for challenging mandatory DAC8 reporting itself, but faces the adverse DAC2 trilogy.
- **Foreign-home passported provider:** use only if Austria actually asserts duplicate reporting; MiCA Articles 60 and 65 otherwise support home-state allocation.

## What a citizen cannot control

- The claimant may request a reference but cannot compel it.
- The DSB is not the Article 267 court. It creates the reviewable case; the reference request belongs primarily before the BVwG.
- A lower court may reject an invalidity argument; it may not itself invalidate an EU act under Foto-Frost.
- A last-instance court must assess a properly raised request and explain a non-referral under Consorzio/KUBERA, but can decline if the question is irrelevant, already settled, or genuinely clear.
- The VfGH may initiate Austrian norm review ex officio when the provision is prejudicial; this does not eliminate admissibility and jurisdictional gates in the underlying case.
- No route supplies an automatic pre-report stop. A claimant must prove the factual and legal thresholds for DSB or civil interim relief, and EU-validity-based suspension faces the Atlanta conditions.

## Merits obstacles

- The DAC2 trilogy rejected broad privacy, no-threshold, cybersecurity, all-customer processing, and non-referral arguments.
- Belgian Association of Tax Lawyers, C-623/22, broadly upheld the DAC6 framework; C-694/20 succeeded only for lawyer-client confidentiality.
- DAC8 reports annual aggregates—not wallet addresses, dates, transaction IDs, or counterparties.
- Tax cooperation, specified fields, GDPR, routing rules, and non-public exchange distinguish public-disclosure and communications-surveillance cases.

## Claimant file

- actual CASP contract and reporting jurisdiction;
- provider status under the Krypto-MPfG;
- 2026 activity mapped to section 18 categories;
- notices, self-certification, access request, processing and retention proof;
- exact data actually held, transmitted, and accessible;
- evidence supporting any ledger-correlation or physical-security theory;
- complete statutory perimeter: reporting, fields, retention, access, exchange, safeguards, sanctions, and remedies;
- one decision-relevant proposed question and one precise remedy.
