# VfGH-to-CJEU DAC8 Route

> Revised verdict: there is no single optimal claimant. A user should mirror DAC2—DSB → BVwG → Article 267/140 review—while an Austria-home CASP may have strong direct Article 140 standing based on current statutory and implementation burdens.

## Coordinated two-track route

### Track A: provider

1. Use an Austria-home CASP whose section 15 status is undisputed and whose section 20 duties operate without an intervening decision.
2. Preserve exact completed or contracted data-pipeline, aggregation, storage, security, DPIA, and staffing work.
3. Separate the unavoidable DAC8/MiCA/AML baseline from the cost of processing confirmed non-reportable users under section 20(1).
4. Bring a precise Article 140 application centred on Article 6 StGG and section 1 DSG, subject to checking whether a genuine ordinary determination route exists.
5. Request a narrow Article 267 interpretation reference if the result depends on whether DAC8 permits or requires the Austrian universal-processing addition.

G 105/2025 and G 606/2015 show that material pre-compliance technical and administrative costs can create current/direct effect and that a business need not provoke a penalty. They are also adverse merits authorities: broad data and tax-compliance systems survived. Standing is not success.

G 37/02 ua, G 118/2015 ua, G 100/05 ua, and G 20/2015 ua strengthen this standing method with successful direct business applications. Their limits matter: the VfGH did not hold that every expensive compliance regime is disproportionate, and G 37/02 preserved the underlying surveillance-facilities duty while removing the uncompensated cost allocation.

### Track B: user

1. Select an actual user of an Austrian-covered CASP with post-1 January 2026 processing.
2. Use a genuinely non-reportable user for the section 20(1) national-excess case, or a reportable Austrian user for a broader DAC8 validity case.
3. Make legally matched Article 15 and, where applicable, Article 17 or 18 requests to establish fields, purposes, recipients, retention, deletion, and any refusal. Do not invoke Article 21 mechanically where the CASP relies on a legal obligation under Article 6(1)(c).
4. File a precise section 24 DSG complaint against concrete processing by the identified controller, not a generalized policy objection. Observe the one-year knowledge and three-year long-stop periods.
5. Appeal a DSB decision to the BVwG within four weeks. The BVwG—not only the VfGH—is already able to make an Article 267 reference and can apply to the VfGH under Article 140 where it has constitutional concerns about a prejudicial national provision.
6. Continue through Article 144 VfGH review within six weeks of the BVwG decision and separately assess VwGH review.

This is not theoretical. The Austrian DAC2 claimant used this chain through DSB, BVwG, VfGH, and VwGH. Austrian PNR litigation likewise shows a DSB/BVwG vehicle applying later CJEU authority.

The route can start before the first report. Section 20 processing has operated since 1 January 2026; the first provider report for 2026 is due by 31 July 2027. Before transmission, the CASP is normally the concrete respondent. Later BMF receipt, onward exchange, and foreign-authority processing are separate acts and may require separate cases.

## Direct Article 140 fallback

The retention litigation proves a direct filing can lead to an Article 267 validity reference. But its admissibility depended on unknown/distributed providers, communication partners' providers, continuously regenerated records, and a six-month deletion horizon. In G 178/2024 the VfGH held that DSB → BVwG → Article 144 was reasonable for mandatory health-data processing even if first-instance success looked poor. The Court did not decide challenge perimeter, adequate pleading of the concerns, or whether the GDPR's direct applicability created another admissibility problem.

For a DAC8 user, a direct application should therefore be fallback only when concrete facts establish retention-style exceptional circumstances and show that DSB/BVwG protection cannot prevent a specific irreversible injury. Predicted DSB defeat, normal delay, ordinary cost, several known CASPs, or the DSB's inability to invalidate an EU act are insufficient. Every claimant and every challenged provision must be pleaded separately. The 11,129 rejected AKVorrat co-applicants show the cost of generic mass pleading.

Do not combine the user and CASP into one undifferentiated application. They rely on different rights, factual injuries, alternative routes, and challenge perimeters. Coordinated separate proceedings preserve each case's strongest standing theory.

## Candidate Article 267 issues

For the Austrian excess, the strongest interpretation question is:

> Do Article 8ad and Annex VI Sections II and III of Directive 2011/16/EU, as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/2226, read with Directive Article 25 and GDPR Articles 5(1)(b), (c), and (e), 6(3), and 23, permit or require national law to make a provider determine, collect, store, and process the complete reportable transaction dataset for every transaction and crypto-asset of every customer regardless of reportable-user status?

This question targets section 20(1), not DAC8 as a whole. Universal self-certification can be separated from universal transaction-profile creation.

For a reportable-user validity case, the question remains narrower than “DAC8 is mass surveillance”:

> Are the identity-linked annual per-asset and per-category values, units, counts, retail-payment and transfer aggregates required by Directive (EU) 2023/2226 compatible with Charter Articles 7, 8, and 52(1), having regard to generalized coverage without individualized suspicion, pre-report processing, retention and access, effective remedies, and any proved ability to correlate the report with public-ledger data?

Each question must be necessary to decide its concrete proceeding. Austria's section 20(1) addition, its ten-year choice for transmitted information, and any national implementation defect must be separated from EU-act validity because national discretion cannot by itself invalidate DAC8.

## Claimant fit

- **Austria-home CASP:** strongest direct Article 140 candidate, but needs a provider-specific costed counterfactual.
- **Non-reportable user:** strongest national section 20(1) privacy claimant; citizenship is irrelevant, and non-reportability must be proved against the participating-state rules at the filing date.
- **Reportable Austrian user:** strongest vehicle for challenging mandatory DAC8 reporting itself, but faces the adverse DAC2 trilogy.
- **Foreign-home passported provider:** use only if Austria actually asserts duplicate reporting; MiCA Articles 60 and 65 otherwise support home-state allocation.

## What a citizen cannot control

- The claimant may request a reference but cannot compel it.
- The DSB is not the Article 267 court. It creates the reviewable case; the reference request belongs primarily before the BVwG.
- A lower court may reject an invalidity argument; it may not itself invalidate an EU act under Foto-Frost.
- A last-instance court must assess a properly raised request and explain a non-referral under Consorzio/KUBERA, but can decline if the question is irrelevant, already settled, or genuinely clear.
- The VfGH may initiate Austrian norm review ex officio when the provision is prejudicial; this does not eliminate admissibility and jurisdictional gates in the underlying case.

## Merits obstacles

- The DAC2 trilogy rejected broad privacy, no-threshold, cybersecurity, all-customer processing, and non-referral arguments.
- Belgian Association of Tax Lawyers, C-623/22, broadly upheld the DAC6 framework; C-694/20 succeeded only for lawyer-client confidentiality.
- DAC8 reports annual aggregates—not wallet addresses, dates, transaction IDs, or counterparties.
- Tax cooperation, specified fields, GDPR, routing rules, and non-public exchange distinguish public-disclosure and communications-surveillance cases.

## Claimant file

- actual CASP contract and reporting jurisdiction;
- provider status under the Krypto-MPfG;
- 2026 activity mapped to section 18 categories;
- notices, self-certification, access request, processing and retention proof;
- exact data actually held, transmitted, and accessible;
- evidence supporting any ledger-correlation or physical-security theory;
- complete statutory perimeter: reporting, fields, retention, access, exchange, safeguards, sanctions, and remedies;
- one decision-relevant proposed question and one precise remedy.
